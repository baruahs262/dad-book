# The Tell: senior reader's report (cold read, 60 chapters, c.73,200 words)

## 1. Pitch

In 2010 sixteen-year-old Sanchez Roberti watches her father walk to the village bakery in Ayrshire. He never comes back. Four years later she seduces and drugs Benedikt Voss, courier for a smuggling route run by the Ayr logistics firm TransPacific, and photographs his coded map. She is chased through Zurich and Lisbon, then shot and captured near Nazaré. Her captor has fallen for her, and she lets him take her down the route itself as his assistant, from Tallinn by ship to Belarus and a Russian compound near Smolensk. Behind her comes the NCA officer who trained her, now acting without official backing. The cargo is looted Cham temple statuary. The man she is falling for sat beside her father in the car that took him.

## 2. Verdict

**Query plus first three chapters: yes, request the full.** Chapter 1 ends on a haunting image (the dropped bakery bag). Chapter 2's poker scene hooks and Chapter 3's heist is well engineered. The only warning sign is stiff dialogue ("They are rude and discriminating").

**Full read: not an offer yet. I would send a revise-and-resubmit letter and offer if the revision lands.** The plotting is unusually disciplined for a debut. Dates and time zones check out, and the payoffs are properly planted: the window board (Ch 1 to Ch 59), "Caledonia", the sleeve wiped over the glass and the cufflink tell. The last four chapters really move. Against that, the prose is only serviceable, the middle sags and the romance asks too much.

**Chances.** A Big Five imprint as it stands: low, because the voice does not stand out. After a strong revision: possible as a mid-level debut. A digital-first list (Bookouture, Canelo, Boldwood, Hera, Avon): good now, pitched as romantic suspense.

## 3. Five biggest weaknesses

**1. Momentum stalls.** Part Two (Ch 7 to 13) delivers seven chapters of backstory straight after the heist hook. Some action is also told twice from a second point of view: the Zurich chase (Ch 5 and 14), the airport (Ch 16 and 17) and Fanhais (Ch 22 to 25, with overlapping time stamps). *Fix:* run Ayr as an alternating strand or cut it to three chapters, and reduce Maxwell's replays to a paragraph each.

**2. No antagonist in the middle.** From Ch 31, when Benedikt becomes her protector, until Kozlov arrives in Ch 55, nobody is actively threatening her. Slater is almost entirely offstage. The ship chapters (Ch 36 to 46) are mostly procedure, and Damian's suspicion (Ch 39, 40, 43) is dropped. *Fix:* have Damian tip off Ayr to start a deadline, put Slater on the page, and cut the ship section by a third.

**3. The romance and the ending are not earned.** In Ch 57 Benedikt confesses to sitting in the abduction car and saying "Get rid of him". She answers "I should have used it". The reconciliation then happens off the page ("they talked on into the night"), and by Ch 60 it is "Yes, us" and "for a lifetime". *Fix:* write that night as a full scene. Give Benedikt a costly act of restitution on the page first, or make her final reason tactical (he is the witness against Kozlov).

**4. Plausibility.** Maxwell's legal reasoning in Ch 35 is backwards: a civilian he recruited, trained and tasked is acting for the state, so her evidence carries the same taint as his. Drake's reaction (Ch 30, 47, 60) is also far too mild. She drops her stick five days after being shot (Ch 41) and is running by Ch 53. She beats four armed men after evening classes (Ch 24 and 25). Belarusian police hand their pistols to a Dane (Ch 56). *Fix:* make the admissible core Daniel's waybill and memory card, stretch her recovery, win the fights by trickery, and keep Zhuk's men armed.

**5. Stiff dialogue and an uneven line.** Almost nobody uses contractions, the Scots included. Emotions are labelled rather than shown ("Sanchez was frightened"). There are tics: Maxwell's "Look," and Benedikt's "Is that clear?" / "Will you?". Point of view sometimes hops within a scene (Ch 16, 39, 42). *Fix:* a line edit, one point of view per scene, and far fewer emotion labels.

## 4. Continuity errors

1. **Ch 15 against Ch 4 and 29.** Benedikt says "I will send you a picture of a man", but earlier "The cameras never caught his face", and later he "did not know his name, or his face."
2. **Ch 18 against Ch 26.** At midday on Thursday Benedikt says "By this evening she will see sense", which means Adriana has already been approached. Adriana says the men came "last night."
3. **Damian.** First "head of his personal security" and "oldest friend" (Ch 4, 34). From Ch 39 to 43 he is "from the Tallinn office", and he does not sail.
4. **Ch 35 against Ch 7.** "You were eighteen" at training (October 2012), but her eighteenth birthday was before November 2011, so she was nineteen.
5. **Ch 31 against Ch 41.** The doctor's "a week or two before she walks without a stick" is followed by the stick being left behind five days later.
6. **Ch 57 against Ch 1.** "They took the camera from his van", but he had walked to the bakery.
7. **Ch 19 against Ch 17.** "The officer from the airport" reads as the airport policeman, not Luís.
8. **Ch 20.** "A man in a dark car sat reading a newspaper" is never explained.
9. **Ch 13.** An annual game ("Every April") is said to "settle the quarter".

## 5. One hand?

Yes. The syntax and the motifs are consistent throughout. But Ch 1 to 4 and 56 to 60 are controlled, while Ch 32 to 38 and 49 to 55 slip into stock phrasing: "He looked like a dethroned king, lost and dismayed" (Ch 52), "Anatoly was at his deceitful best" (Ch 58), "the gleam was back on his countenance" (Ch 55), "Sanchez was reasonable and understood the rationale behind it" (Ch 58). Tonally off: "Thud!" (Ch 22), "SANCHEZ!" (Ch 26), "God damn!" (Ch 27), "get the hell out of this place" (Ch 44).

## 6. Comparables and length

Comparables: *The Little Drummer Girl* (a young woman recruited to get close to the enemy, who falls for him), *Red Sparrow* (seduction as tradecraft), Daniel Silva's antiquities-smuggling novels, and *Codename Villanelle* (a pursuer obsessed with his quarry). Position it as commercial romantic suspense and thriller with a Scottish heroine. Justify the 2014 setting, which could include a nod to Crimea on the Russian border.

**Target: about 85,000 words.** Cut about 5,000 of duplicated scenes and add about 15,000 for Slater on the page, the Rudnya night and the fallout (MacCallum, Patterson, Adriana, Luís's family, Maxwell's own reckoning).
