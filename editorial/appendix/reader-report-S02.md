Section spans the end of Part I (ch 8–10, April 2014, Zurich / "Stockholm, Denmark") and the opening of Part II (ch 11–15, a flashback to March–April 2012, Ayr). About 9,000 words.

Note on missing ids: ch 8, ch 9, ch 10, ch 11 and ch 11 are not in paras.json. They are blank lines or separators in book.md. No text is missing: ch 10 runs straight into "Part II" in book.md at lines 383–391.

## 1. Chapter-by-chapter summary

### Chapter 8 — "4:30 AM 13th April 2014, Zurich, Switzerland" (ch 8)
- **POV:** Mostly Sanchez at first ch 8, then Stephen ch 8, back to Sanchez [ch 8], then Stephen ch 8. The POV slides between them mid-scene.
- **Setting:** Zurich streets before dawn, then an apartment-complex car park. They are in the BMW 5 Series from ch 5 (ch 5).
- **Beats:**
  - The driver, first called only "her companion", makes random turns to test the tail. The grey Peugeot stays with them (ch 8).
  - We're told it's the weekend before Sechseläuten and that Böögg effigies line the streets (ch 8).
  - Sanchez goes over her escape from the hotel and can't see how she was followed. She reasons that Benedikt is too powerful to need a discreet tail ch 8.
  - Stephen swerves into an apartment car park. The Peugeot drives past, and he waits twenty minutes ch 8.
  - Stephen checks the street on foot, flattening himself against the wall to look both ways, and sees nothing ch 8.
  - He announces "our little delusion worked" and says they must rest, then head for Portugal ch 8.
  - We learn in passing that Stephen is her **brother** (ch 8). They have a protocol of no calls during a mission, and she has been away four days.
  - Sanchez sleeps. Stephen drives the "good couple of hours" back to the apartment, thinking about the "mountainous task" ahead ch 8.
- **What the reader learns:** the driver's name (Stephen) and that he's her brother. They have a longer mission that runs through Portugal. Nobody knows who is in the Peugeot.
- **Ending hook:** Weak. "The glitter in his eyes was shining bright…" (ch 8). There is no question or threat to carry the reader over. (The dramatic irony that Maxwell is in fact still on them only pays off in ch 19.)

### Chapter 9 — "8:00 AM 13th April 2014, Stockholm, Denmark" (ch 9)
- **POV:** Benedikt, with slips into Damian [ch 9].
- **Setting:** Benedikt's hotel suite in Stockholm.
- **Beats:**
  - Benedikt is "flabbergasted". His Basel team has found nothing, the search has run "two days", and his "higher up[s]" are pressing him (ch 9).
  - Through a passport-office contact he knows she lived in **Ayr** for two years. His team has searched her Ayr flat and found nothing: no friends, rent always paid (ch 9).
  - He admits a dangerous attraction to her and remembers "the passionate indulgence they had" (ch 9).
  - He decides she was after "that map", a cryptic, classified map of "a dangerous dealing" that too many rich people depend on ch 9.
  - He wonders about Trevor: probably an alias, possibly shadowing her, possibly a decoy (ch 9).
  - He deduces she stayed on past Basel, lists the "ten stations", crosses out the first four and Basel, and keeps Hamburg, Freiburg and Zurich ch 9.
  - He briefs Damian: three teams, reports every thirty minutes, CCTV from every station ch 9.
  - He secretly phones an unnamed trusted contact and asks whether Sanchez and Trevor have any link to **TransPacific** ch 9.
- **What the reader learns:** Ayr, the map, TransPacific (its first mention in the book), that Benedikt's rival "peers" and "higher-ups" exist, and his infatuation.
- **Ending hook:** Good. The secret call about TransPacific is the best hook in the section. It also sets up Part II's TransPacific plot cleanly.

### Chapter 10 — "4:00 AM 14th April 2014, Zurich, Switzerland" (ch 10)
- **POV:** Stephen, then a loose omniscient.
- **Setting:** A one-bedroom apartment, then the road to St. Gallen–Altenrhein Airport, then a flight.
- **Beats:**
  - The alarm goes off. Stephen hardly slept, having decided against leaving the night before so Sanchez could rest (ch 10).
  - He showers, wakes "San" and cooks breakfast. The 9:00 AM flight is mentioned ch 10.

- **What the reader learns:** Almost nothing new: they are heading to Lisbon.
- **Ending hook:** None. **Part I ends on "enough time for them to rest and rethink their go forward plan"** (ch 10), the flattest possible close for a first act.

## 3. Continuity, logic and factual errors

### A. Geography and travel

1. **"Stockholm, Denmark"** (ch 9). Also in book.md l.23, 107, 145, 175, 209 and 731. Stockholm is in **Sweden**, and so is the "Kronor" of S01. The same error recurs at (ch 9) ("before coming to **Denmark**"). **Fix:** "Stockholm, Sweden" everywhere; "before coming to Sweden".

2. **The train geometry is impossible** ch 9.
   - In 2014 the real Copenhagen–Basel overnight train (CityNightLine *Aurora*) left Copenhagen at 18:10. It ran via Roskilde, Odense, Kolding, Padborg, Flensburg, Neumünster, Hannover, Fulda, Frankfurt Süd, Mannheim, Karlsruhe, Offenburg, Freiburg and Basel Bad, and **terminated at Basel SBB**.
   - It did not call at **Hamburg**, and **Zurich is not "between Copenhagen and Basel"**: it lies beyond Basel.
   - The text also contradicts itself: "ten stations between Copenhagen and **Zurich**" (ch 9), then "10 places between Copenhagen and **Basel**" (ch 9).
   - It contradicts its own rule too. Benedikt crosses out stations within four hours of Copenhagen, yet Hamburg would be among the earliest, while Freiburg is the stop right before Basel.
   - And having just said she "chose not to get off" at Basel (ch 9), he then treats Basel as a possible exit and crosses it out (ch 9).
   - **Fix:** He learns from Basel CCTV that she never came through the Basel SBB concourse. He reasons she got off at a late stop (Freiburg or Basel Badischer Bahnhof, on the German side of the border) and continued by road or regional train. He sends teams to **Freiburg, Basel Bad. Bf. and Zurich**. Zurich stays a hunch, and his reasoning becomes sharp rather than muddled.

3. **"Headed south to St. Gallen–Altenrhein"** (ch 10). From Zurich, Altenrhein is **east-north-east**, on Lake Constance, about an hour away, not 1.5 hours (ch 10). Ch 22 (S03) says "about an hour". And if the apartment is two hours out along the A3 (ch 19; (ch 8)), the direction depends on where the apartment is.
   - **Fix:** Settle where the apartment is. Take the direction out, or make it correct.

4. **No Lisbon flights from Altenrhein in 2014** [ch 10]. The only scheduled route was People's Viennaline to Vienna, plus holiday charters. TAP Portugal never flew there. This matters because ch 22 builds a whole scene around a TAP counter at Altenrhein. **Fix (book-wide, coordinate with S03):**
   - either keep Altenrhein and have them fly to Vienna and connect to Lisbon, which also muddies the trail;
   - or use Basel-Mulhouse or Geneva (TAP served Geneva), chosen as the "less watched" airport.

5. **"It was a long flight"** (ch 10). Central Switzerland to Lisbon is under three hours. **Fix:** "a three-hour flight, enough time to…", or cut the line.

6. **Where is the apartment?** ch 8 vs [ch 10.
   - Ch 8 says "once he leaves the city it would take a good couple of hours to reach the apartment" (ch 8), and ch 19 agrees: two hours on the A3.
   - Ch 10 is datelined "Zurich" and talks of "bail[ing] out of Zurich" (ch 10) and being "ready to leave Zurich" (ch 10).
   - **Fix:** Dateline ch 10 with the real town (for example near Walenstadt or Chur) and cut the "leave Zurich" phrases.

7. **Ayr as "a large city"** (ch 11) and "the port City of Scotland" (ch 9). Ayr is a town of about 46,000. It's the county town of Ayrshire and a small harbour, not Scotland's port city. **Fix:** "a town the size of Ayr" and "the harbour town of Ayr on Scotland's west coast". The needle-in-a-haystack line (ch 11) can stay if softened: "Even in a town the size of Ayr…".

8. **A glass tower with a 100 ft atrium fountain in Ayr** ch 12. Ayr has no such building, and a reader from Scotland will balk. A global logistics firm would base its Scottish hub at **Glasgow Prestwick Airport**, a real freight hub a few miles north of Ayr.
   - **Fix:** Make TransPacific's building a big modern depot and office block on the Prestwick/Ayr road. Cut the atrium down to "a two-storey glass atrium with a fountain".
   - It also removes a plausibility problem: she would have noticed a skyscraper in Ayr in four months.

9. **"Far East side of Scotland, a place called Fraserburgh"** (ch 14). Fraserburgh is in the **north-east** (Aberdeenshire). **Fix:** "the far north-east, a fishing town called Fraserburgh".

10. **A 45-minute taxi ride inside Ayr** (ch 15). Nowhere in Ayr is 45 minutes by taxi, even in traffic. **Fix:** Put John in a nearby village (Prestwick, Maybole, Annbank), or make the ride fifteen minutes.

11. **"Bohemian Football club"** (ch 15). Bohemians is a Dublin club. For an Ayr man, use **Ayr United** (nicknamed "the Honest Men", which is thematically sharp here). Kilmarnock also works. Celtic and Rangers carry sectarian baggage.

12. **"254 Brendstad Street"** (ch 15). The name reads as Scandinavian, and a house number of 254 is unlikely on a terraced street in a Scottish town. **Fix:** Something like "54 Craigie Road" or an invented "Bruntsfield Street".

13. **"Seaport Park"** (ch 12). There's no such park in Ayr. Real options are the Low Green, the Esplanade, Rozelle Park and Belleisle. An invented name is allowable, but the Low Green by the sea keeps the "breeze" motif.

14. **"Gribin"** (ch 15) vs **"Girbin"** in ch 1 (ch 1). One use of each in the whole book. **Fix:** Choose one and use it consistently.

### B. Timeline (present-day line, ch 8–10)

15. **The Stockholm-to-Zurich dateline is impossible.**
    - The bellman says she left the Stockholm hotel at about 2:30 AM on 13 April (S01 ch 6).
    - Ch 8 has her in Zurich at 4:30 AM on the same day (ch 8), and ch 5 has the train arriving at 4:20 AM.
    - Stockholm to Copenhagen alone is about five hours, and the only direct Copenhagen–Basel train left at 18:10.
    - Ch 19 (S03) redates the same train arrival to "4:00 AM **15th** April".
    - **Fix:** One master timeline. Suggested version:
      - she escapes about 2:30 AM on Sun 13 April;
      - takes the first morning train to Copenhagen;
      - takes the 18:10 overnight to Basel;
      - gets off at dawn on **Mon 14 April** and is collected by Stephen;
      - ch 8 is the car chase at about 6:00 AM on the 14th;
      - ch 9 is Benedikt at about 8:00 AM on the 14th, which makes the "two days" (ch 9) close to true;
      - ch 10 is the departure on the 15th or 16th, to agree with ch 22 (5:00 AM 16 April).

16. **Ch 9's clock is wrong in several ways at once** ch 9.
    - The dateline says 8:00 AM on the 13th, only about five hours after she vanished, yet the text says "It was two days now".
    - A team has somehow already searched her flat in **Ayr** and interviewed the landlord (ch 9).
    - "We need to find her in next four hours, **as the morning will dawn**" (ch 9): it is already 8 AM.
    - "Benedikt had one final job left **for the night**" (ch 9), then he lies down on the sofa to rest (ch 9).
    - **Fix:** Move ch 9 to the small hours of the next day (see item 15). Otherwise, have the Ayr search *ordered* rather than finished, and cut "for the night".

17. **"Rest for the night and leave first thing tomorrow morning"** (ch 8), said at about 5 AM. **Fix:** "Let's get back to the apartment, rest today, and leave first thing tomorrow."

18. **The 4:00 AM alarm vs the 4:00 AM wall clock** ch 10. Stephen showers for twenty minutes after the alarm, and the clock then reads 4:00. **Fix:** Dateline "3:40 AM", or "The wall clock showed twenty past four."

19. **A pre-booked 9:00 AM flight** (ch 10) vs ch 22, where they **buy tickets at the counter** and the flight "leaves in about 45 minutes". **Fix:** Decide which. Buying at the counter is more fugitive-like, so drop "catch the 9:00 AM flight" from ch 10.

20. **"Four long rumpus nights"** (ch 8) vs S01 ch 7, where she "checked into the hotel three days back". **Fix:** Make them agree.

21. **Busy streets at 4:30 AM on a Sunday** [ch 8] vs "given it was a weekend, people were still in repose" (ch 8). The text contradicts itself. **Fix:** Empty streets. They make a tail *more* conspicuous, which is better for tension.

22. **Sechseläuten** (ch 8).
    - In 2014 it fell on **Monday 28 April**: Easter Monday was the 21st. So 12–13 April was **not** "the last weekend before" it.
    - It's a Zurich festival (a local half-day holiday), not "the traditional spring holiday in Switzerland".
    - There is **one** Böögg, a snowman effigy burned on the Sechseläutenplatz. There aren't several "decorated around the streets".
    - "the winter of Effigy" is garbled: the Böögg is the effigy *of* winter.
    - **Fix:** "Two weeks before Sechseläuten, Zurich's spring festival, when the guilds burn the Böögg, a snowman effigy of winter…". Or cut it: it adds colour but no plot.

### C. Timeline (Part II flashback, ch 11–15)

23. **"It was a Monday when she wrote to them"** (ch 13). She wrote the email on the evening of **Friday 16 March** [ch 12, ch 13]. **Fix:** "It was a Friday evening when she wrote to them, and not until the following Friday that she received a response."

24. **Ch 13's "2:00 PM" dateline** (ch 13) continues a scene that began around 11:30 AM ch 12, mid-stride. **Fix:** Merge ch 12 and 13, or drop the time.

25. **Ch 14's content runs past ch 15's dateline.** Ch 14 is dated 16 April. Two weeks of shadowing plus "a month before she was assigned a desktop" ch 14 puts the address-list search in mid-to-late **May**, yet ch 15 is dated **30 April**. Ch 15 also covers "the next several weeks" (ch 15). **Fix:** Redate ch 15 to about 21 May, or cut "a month" to "a week".

26. **"The first four months in Ayr"** (ch 15). She had been there "over four months" by 15 March (ch 11), so it's about six by 30 April. **Fix:** "Her first months in Ayr".

27. **A "summer intern" who starts on 16 April** [ch 13, ch 14]. That's term time, and she is supposedly in school or college [ch 13: "headed straight to college to continue with her schedule"]. The text shows her working full days on the floor [ch 14, ch 15]. **Fix:** Call it a **part-time placement** (evenings and Saturdays). That also motivates the after-hours searching [ch 14, ch 15] and the night shift.

28. **"Two months had gone by"** (ch 16, 18 June) vs ch 15 (30 April plus "several weeks"). That's roughly consistent. But **ch 16 then presents John's name in the notebook as a new discovery** ("She noticed John's name invariably scribbled…", book.md l.609), while ch 12 ch 12 says the notebook was the very reason she came to Ayr. **Fix:** In ch 16, have her notice something else, such as the addresses or the to-do lists.

### D. Character logic and plot holes

29. **Sanchez's age is the most dangerous inconsistency in the book, legally and commercially.**
    - The evidence:
      - early teens in Feb 2010 (ch 1);
      - "after school hours", "school", "from school" in 2012 [ch 11, ch 12, ch 13];
      - a "first-year college student" with "professors" [ch 13];
      - a "naïve teenager" in 2014 (ch 9);
      - a "passionate indulgence" with Benedikt (ch 9) (the S01 honey-trap);
      - "twenty-something" later (book.md l.1111).
    - As written she may be **17 or younger** when she seduces a much older man. No mainstream publisher will take that, and an agent will stop reading.
    - **Fix:** Fix her birth year. Suggested:
      - 16 in 2010 (change "early teens" to "sixteen");
      - 18 in March 2012, a first-year student at the University of the West of Scotland's Ayr campus (real, and it fits well);
      - 20 in 2014.
    - Replace "school" with "university" or "college" in [ch 11, ch 12, ch 13]. Change "naïve teenager" (ch 9) to "naïve girl".

30. **Where is Stephen in 2012?** In 2014 he is her partner in the mission ch 8. In Part II she says "Besides Aunt Naiena she knew no one in Ayr" (ch 11) and works entirely alone. **Fix:** One sentence placing him (in the army, at university in Glasgow, estranged, abroad), or a phone call in ch 15.

31. **Where is her mother?** Ch 1 has a "family" and a "resting family". Part II has only an aunt. An orphan-in-Ayr setup needs one line.

32. **Why didn't she know her father's employer?**
    - "TP" beside John's name has been "always a mystery" (ch 12).
    - Her father was a delivery driver (ch 13) in the same region.
    - A girl who has spent two years on this would know which firm her father drove for.
    - If he worked for TransPacific, "TP" is obvious. If he didn't, the text needs to say so ("her father drove for a small Kilmarnock courier").
    - **Fix:** Make her father self-employed or with a subcontractor. Then TP means nothing to her until she sees the logo. It stays a coincidence, but a fair one.

33. **The coincidence of the logo** (ch 12). After four months in a small town she has never seen a tall branded tower. With the Prestwick fix (item 8) it's a depot off her usual routes, which is much easier to accept.

34. **She applies under her real name and cites her father's job** [ch 13]. That's reckless if she believes TransPacific staff are connected to his disappearance, especially as she later lies to John by phone. **Fix:** A sentence of her weighing it: "Roberto was a common enough name; no one here would connect it…". Or use it as a later payoff, with someone in TransPacific recognising the name.

35. **"Why the cops failed?"** (ch 15). A good seed, but it raises a question: did she give the police the notebook? If not, the failure is hers, not theirs. **Fix:** One line: "The police had had her father's notebook for a week and returned it without comment", which makes the conspiracy thought land.

36. **The flight risk in 2014** (ch 10). Benedikt has her photo and name, and passport data is his only good lead (ch 9). Yet she leaves her waist-length hair loose and flies under her own name. Ch 24 does show them changing looks in Lisbon, but at this point the text doesn't register the danger. **Fix:** Stephen says "Hair up, hood up", or she ties it back. Two lines.

## 4. Pacing and flow

**Ch 8 (about 1,700 words): a chase with no chase.**
- The Peugeot is shaken in two paragraphs ch 8. Everything else is interior recap:
  - Sanchez rehashes the hotel escape the reader has already seen (ch 8);
  - Stephen checks the street in step-by-step detail ch 8;
  - two closing paragraphs of general brooding ("mountainous task", "glitter in his eyes") ch 8.
- **Fix:** Cut about 40%. Keep:
  - the evasion (make the Peugeot close in once, say at a red light, before Stephen's swerve);
  - Stephen's street check, tightened;
  - the "brother" reveal, made into a line of dialogue rather than a buried clause (ch 8).
- Cut ch 8 entirely.

**Ch 9 (about 1,900 words): the best-built chapter, but slowed by rumination.**
- (ch 9), (ch 9) and ch 9 each circle the same point: she's a professional and he's rattled.
- The station deduction ch 9 is a good procedural beat. Once the geography is fixed it will read as intelligence.
- The TransPacific phone call is the chapter's engine. Get there faster: cut (ch 9)'s longing by half, and merge ch 9.

**Ch 10 (about 800 words): cut or rethink.**
- An alarm clock, a shower, breakfast, clothes, carry-ons, an uneventful drive, an on-time flight.
- It closes Part I on "enough time for them to rest and rethink their go forward plan" (ch 10). It adds nothing ch 22 doesn't re-tell with more tension (Maxwell at the airport).
- **Options:**
  - (a) Cut ch 10 and end Part I on ch 9's secret phone call about TransPacific, which is a strong curtain and hands directly to Part II's TransPacific plot;
  - (b) or cut ch 10 to two paragraphs and end on an unsettling beat, for example Stephen noticing the same hooded man twice in the terminal.

**The Part I to Part II break.**
- Launching a 2012 flashback block after only ten chapters, just as the chase heats up, parks the tension for many chapters (Part II runs through ch 18).
- A structural option for the author: **interleave** 2012 chapters with 2014 ones, one flashback every two or three present-day chapters. That's standard in the genre and would keep both lines alive.
- If Part II stays a block, it needs a stronger opening than ch 11's mood summary.

**Ch 11 (about 450 words): all summary.**
- It should merge into ch 12, keeping about three sentences of backstory and one concrete, painful memory of the father.
- As written the reader doesn't know *why* John Patterson matters. One sentence would give Part II a spine: "the only name in her father's notebook that the police had never asked about".

**Ch 12–13 (about 3,900 words together): the section's biggest drag.**
- The run from the lobby to the job offer is dramatised at the level of a job-application manual:
  - the kiosk (ch 13);
  - the bath (ch 13);
  - agonising over the email ch 13;
  - the **full** cover letter, eight paragraphs ch 13;
  - the full acceptance email ch 13;
  - recommendation letters (ch 13);
  - two days of worry (ch 13);
  - clothes (ch 13);
  - the interview, including a **240-word customer-service answer** (ch 13);
  - the offer ch 13.
- **Fix:**
  - keep the cover letter but cut it to its heart: the Santa/postman opening and "My father was a delivery man", about 120 words;
  - reduce the acceptance email to one line;
  - cut (ch 13) to a sentence ("She talked about empathy, never transferring the call, owning the problem; she could see David's pen stop moving");
  - cut (ch 13), (ch 13) and (ch 13).
- That saves about 1,500 words. Spend them on danger inside TransPacific.

**Ch 14 (about 700 words).** Efficient. The elimination of the four Johns is a nice micro-procedure. The line "It took a month…" should either be cut or have its time handled better.

**Ch 15 (about 1,900 words).**
- The phone ruse and the doorstep scene are the section's best dramatic material. John putting his shoes on and saying "Let us take a walk instead" is excellent.
- But the chapter **rushes its climax into summary**: "For the next several weeks she tried talking to him… stalked him…" (ch 15).
- **Fix:** Dramatise one of those later encounters (for example she follows him to a pub or a depot and sees someone else meet him), or end the chapter on his threat and open ch 16 with the weeks.

## 6. Strengths

- **The TransPacific bridge.** Benedikt's secret call, "find out whether they have any association with TransPacific" (ch 9), followed at once by a 2012 Sanchez walking into TransPacific's lobby, is well-engineered cross-cutting. It rewards the reader and makes Part II feel necessary. Protect it, and consider making it the last beat of Part I.
- **Benedikt's attraction as a weakness.** "It was absurd, but there was also a sense of bereavement that bothered him, it was as if he had lost a loved one" (ch 9). It gives the antagonist an interior life and a flaw a thriller can exploit later. Keep the idea, but trim the "fatally attracted" phrasing.
- **A good mind-image in the author's own voice.** "His mind had become a mud puddle, the more he was stirring, the muddier it became" (ch 9). It's homely, precise and distinctive. This is the author on a good day; more like it.
- **"Deep in his heart, he knew that to get to her he had to think like her"** (ch 9). A strong turn for the antagonist, which the station deduction then acts on.
- **The cover letter's heart.** "As a child, my most exciting moments were always the Christmas time when Santa magically delivered my wish list toys… I also grew up thanking the postman…" (ch 13), and "My father was a delivery man, who traveled places to make sure he delivered things that mattered most to people on time" (ch 13). The reader knows her father vanished while she writes him into a job application to the company she suspects. That's genuine dramatic irony and quietly moving. Trim it, but keep it.
- **John's doorstep scene.** Well conceived throughout:
  - "He looked at her empty hands, and with a confused look blurted 'You said you have a packet for me.'" (ch 15);
  - "He was back in a minute with his shoes on. 'Let us take a walk instead.'" (ch 15). It shows his fear of being overheard at home without saying so. Here the formal "Let us" suits the old man.
  - The single word "Gribin" landing on him (ch 15) is the right way to reveal a connection.
- **The elimination of the four Johns** ch 14. Clean, logical, satisfying procedure.
- **Seeded details that pay off.** Gymnastics (ch 13) foreshadows the window climb in S01. The choir and concert [ch 11, ch 13] give her a past life. The innovation contest (ch 15) is a plausible route up the company ladder.
- **Stephen's street check** ch 8 is sensible tradecraft (back to the wall, check both ends), and ch 19 retells it from Maxwell's side with nice irony. Keep it, tightened.
- **Adele's "Set Fire to the Rain"** (ch 12) is a correct and evocative 2012 detail. (Late March 2012 also brought a record Scottish heat spell, so her "warm weather" (ch 12) can be kept if "summer was around the corner" becomes "spring had come early".)

## 7. Honest assessment

An agent reading this section would find a real idea buried under problems a professional reader catches in the first two pages. The architecture is promising: a young woman stealing a secret from a smitten crime boss, a present-day chase, a flashback that shows how she got inside the logistics company at the heart of it, and a nice bridge between the two. But the section opens on "Stockholm, Denmark", then puts a woman who left Stockholm at 2:30 AM in Zurich two hours later. Benedikt's deduction runs on a railway that doesn't exist, and Part I ends on an on-time flight with nothing at stake. Part II then spends nearly 4,000 words on a job application before its first real scene. The prose problems (malapropisms, POV drift, stately and contraction-free dialogue) would get a manuscript rejected alone, but they're fixable in a line edit. The structural and credibility problems are what make an agent stop.

**The single most important fix: settle Sanchez's age and a master timeline.** Her age is the one issue that makes the book unsellable rather than merely rough: as written she may be a minor during a sexual honey-trap. It also drives the school/college contradictions throughout Part II. So:
- fix her birth year, making her 18 in 2012 and 20 in 2014;
- then build one dated timeline for April 2014 (Stockholm, Sweden → Copenhagen → overnight to Basel → Zurich → airport) and for spring 2012;
- apply both to every dateline in ch 5–10 and 19–23.

Once these hold, cut ch 10, end Part I on the TransPacific phone call, and compress ch 11–13 to half their length. The section would then read as a thriller.
