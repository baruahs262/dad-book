# S05 — Chapters 31–40 (ch 31–40) — Developmental Read

Section length: ~8,200 words. Geography: Valado dos Frades / Nazaré (Sanchez, Stephen, Jules, Adriana, the four hired men), Lisbon and the motorway north of it (Maxwell, Benedikt, Francis, Damian). Calendar: Thursday 17 April to Friday 18 April 2014. **18 April 2014 was Good Friday.** The text never uses this, and it causes one factual error (see §3).

Paragraph ids ch 32, ch 35, ch 36, ch 38 and ch 39 do not exist in paras.json. They were blank or scene-break lines in book.md, so no text is missing.

## 1. Chapter-by-chapter summary

### Chapter 31 — "8:00 AM 18th April 2014, Valado dos Frades, Portugal" ch 31
*(In book.md this dateline alone is set in bold italics with a `<sup>th</sup>`. Every other chapter uses plain bold. Normalise it.)*
- **POV:** Sanchez (loose omniscient; it drifts into Stephen at (ch 31)).
- **Setting:** Jules's hilltop house and study, Valado dos Frades.
- **Beats:**
  - The church bell wakes Sanchez. She takes in the view of vineyards, white houses and the distant shore. ch 31
  - Breakfast with Jules's mother: *sopas de cavalo cansado*, and a warm exchange in Portuguese. ch 31
  - Jules's cartographer's den is described. He puts the phone photos of the map on a big screen and works for an hour. ch 31
  - Decoding: it is a "secret pathway map". Seven colours stand for days of the week, and the dot numbers give the timing. He places the start "three days from now". ch 31
  - The route: Tallinn → Ventspils → Gdynia by sea, then Warsaw → Viciebsk (Belarus) by road, with Russia implied beyond. ch 31
  - They memorise the route, delete the files and shred the notes. Jules drops them in the main street. ch 31
  - "One more job left before leaving Portugal." (ch 31)
- **Reader learns:** the shape of the whole second half of the book (the route) and that the journey starts in about three days.
- **Hook:** mild. The "one more job" points to Adriana.

### Chapter 32 — "7:30 PM 17th April 2014, Lisbon, Portugal" ch 32
- **POV:** Maxwell.
- **Setting:** outside an Asian restaurant in Lisbon, Benedikt's dilapidated safe house, a motel "a couple of miles north".
- **Beats:**
  - Maxwell leaves the restaurant. The GPS shows Benedikt's van has not moved, and he drives past to confirm. (ch 32)

## 3. Continuity, logic and factual errors

### A. Dateline and timeline

1. **Ch32 is out of order (ch 32).** Its dateline, "7:30 PM 17th", comes after ch31's "8:00 AM 18th". Ch32 is the direct continuation of ch30 (4:30 PM 17th, where Maxwell enters the Asian restaurant "for the next one hour").
   - **Fix:** move ch32 to sit between ch30 and ch31, or fold its three paragraphs into the end of ch30.

2. **Ch33: time of day contradicts the dateline.** The dateline is 12:30 PM, but the taxi rides under a "midafternoon sun" (ch 33) and Sanchez says "we had a good late afternoon lunch" (ch 33) at about 1 PM.
   - **Fix:** pick one. See item 4 for the recommended retiming.

3. **A three-hour hole between the break-in and its continuation.**
   - Ch33 ends with the break-in moments after they arrive and go upstairs (about 1:15–2:00 PM) ch 33.
   - Ch34/35 at 2:00 PM confirm "They arrived a few minutes back" (ch 34) and "We saw them entering the house" (ch 35).
   - Ch36 picks up the same second ("Stephen came out of the bathroom startled" (ch 36)) but is dated 4:30 PM, and the gunshot "rattled the late afternoon quietness" (ch 36).
   - So either Sanchez unpacked for 2.5 hours, or the datelines of ch36–40 are wrong.

4. **Maxwell's missing three hours.**
   - At about 2:20 PM he is "less than 30 minutes" from Adriana's (ch 34), yet he only reaches the area at about 5:40 PM (ch41) and the house at 6:05 PM (ch43).
   - Meanwhile the whole crisis unfolds without him.
   - **Recommended retiming**, which fixes items 2–4 together and keeps the later chapters' times:
     - Ch33 phone call: about 3:00 PM (let the Jules session run until after lunch).
     - Arrival at Adriana's: about 3:45 PM.
     - Ch34/35: about 3:50 PM.
     - Van U-turn near Lisbon: about 4:10 PM.
     - Maxwell's drive from the Lisbon outskirts becomes "well over an hour" (Lisbon to Nazaré is about 120 km, 1h15–1h30), so he arrives about 5:40, which matches ch41.
     - Keep ch36 at about 4:00 and ch37 at about 4:15, and adjust ch39/40 by a few minutes.

5. **Ch38 is dated before ch37 but narrates events after it.**
   - Benedikt's failed calls in ch38 (ch 38) are the unanswered rings on Joseph's phone in ch37 (ch 37).
   - The call that then gets through ("Didn't I instruct you to call me every ten minutes? What took you so long?" (ch 38)) is William's call in ch37 ch 37.
   - Ch38's 4:30 PM therefore precedes ch37's 4:45 PM but depicts later events.
   - **Fix:** date ch38 about 4:50 PM and place it after ch37. Better still, merge it into ch37 as a cutaway to the other end of the line (see §4).

6. **Ch38 contradicts itself on direction.**
   - Benedikt orders "loop around … back to Nazaré" (ch 38).
   - Then, after the call: "We need to head back to Lisbon" (ch 38).
   - Then: "By now they were on their way to Nazaré" (ch 38).
   - **Fix:** after "head back to Lisbon", write "Francis … swung the van round once more" and delete the Nazaré sentence.

7. **Ch38 also has a 2.5-hour gap.** Benedikt U-turns towards Lisbon at about 2:20 PM (ch35, fifteen minutes out of Lisbon) but is still aimlessly on the motorway at 4:30 PM (ch38).
   - **Fix:** either retime as in item 4, or have ch38 open at the Lisbon house or warehouse ("Benedikt had not sat down since they came back…"), which also sets up ch42.

8. **Marty's "twenty minutes" (ch 40).** The warehouse (ch42) is "a few miles south of the Lisbon dockyard", which is about 125 km and 1h20+ from Nazaré. Ch42 is dated 6:30 PM and Marty calls at about 5:50.
   - **Fix:** "We should be there in an hour and a half" / "a little after seven". Then adjust ch42's dateline to about 7:15 PM, or move the warehouse.

9. **Wrong locations in datelines.**
   - Ch34 (ch 34) and ch35 (ch 35) happen in Lisbon, but the datelines say Nazaré.
   - Ch38 (ch 38) happens on the motorway near Lisbon, but the dateline says Nazaré.
   - **Fix:** "Lisbon, Portugal" for all three.

10. **Ch39/40 retell the same shooting with different details.**
    - Ch39: Sanchez "collapsing in a pool of blood" beside the car, then the second shot "hit the car trunk" [ch 39].
    - Ch40: shot in the leg while "sprinting towards the front of the car", then aimed "at the car's tire but missed" (ch 40).
    - These can be reconciled (a miss at the tyre could hit the rear panel), but make them agree: "the second shot punched into the boot" in both. Also make Sanchez fall "a few meters from the car" (ch 39) consistent with her having just put Adriana into the back seat.

### B. Geography and facts

11. **"Highway A12" (ch 34) is the wrong motorway.** The A12 runs south from Lisbon over the Vasco da Gama bridge to Setúbal. Lisbon to Nazaré is the **A8** (exit for Nazaré/Valado dos Frades), sometimes the A1 then the A15.
    - **Fix:** "Right after the van joined the A8 for the final dash north…". The U-turn also only works near Lisbon, so say "at the first toll plaza" or "at Loures".

12. **Distances.** "Less than 30 minutes" (ch 34) from a point fifteen minutes out of Lisbon [ch 34, ch 35] is impossible, since the whole trip takes about 1h20. See item 4.

13. **Good Friday bells (ch 31).** On 18 April 2014 (Good Friday) Portuguese Catholic parishes traditionally keep their bells silent from Maundy Thursday until the Easter Vigil. A church bell pealing "with persistence" at 8 AM is wrong for that day.
    - **Fix:** either change the waking sound (a rooster, a tractor, Jules's mother's radio), or better, use it: "The bells were silent — it was Good Friday; Jules's mother would later tell them the bells had 'gone to Rome'."
    - Good Friday is also a national holiday in Portugal. Shops shut, the streets are quiet and there are processions in Nazaré, which is ready-made texture for the phone-booth and taxi scenes.

14. **Valado dos Frades landscape [ch 31, ch 33, ch 37].** Valado dos Frades sits in the flat, drained lowland of the old Pederneira lagoon, about 5 km inland from Nazaré. A "house on top of a hill" with a sea view is possible from the ridges nearby, but "grape vines in vast openness" is a stretch.
    - The section then calls the same countryside vineyards (ch 33) and "open paddy fields" (ch 37), and the later chapters (41, 43) also say paddy.
    - **Fix:** choose one crop. "Fields of maize and cabbages", or "vegetable plots and pinewood", suit the area. If vineyards, drop "paddy".

15. **Houses ch 33.** "Outside walls made of woods … covered with pale stuccoes" and "the ligneous house" do not fit rural Portugal, where houses are masonry, whitewashed and with terracotta roofs, as ch31 itself correctly says (ch 31). "Castilian wrought iron" (ch 33) is Spanish.
    - **Fix:** "whitewashed stone … wrought-iron grilles on the tall windows".

16. **"Local Discovery channel" (ch 31).** Discovery Channel is not a local broadcaster.
    - **Fix:** "a history programme on RTP", or "documentaries for Portuguese television".

17. **Portuguese dialogue:**
    - Jules's mother's "você slepp bem." (ch 31) is garbled. Use "Dormiram bem?" ("Did you sleep well?").
    - "Quem é esse?" (ch 33) is not how the Portuguese answer the phone. Use "Estou? Quem fala?" ("Hello? Who's speaking?").
    - "Venha aqui" (ch 33) is better as "Venha cá".
    - "Como se atreve a entrar…" (ch 36) should be the plural "Como se atrevem a entrar na minha casa?" because there are two intruders.

18. **".42 caliber gun" (ch 36).** There is no common .42-calibre handgun. Use "a pistol", "a 9mm" or ".45".

19. **The map logic ch 31 is internally incoherent.**
    - If the seven colours encode the weekday, the dot numbers are left doing nothing.
    - "The first dot had a number 7, which is exactly three days from now" does not follow.
    - "Three days … or three months from now" makes no sense for a weekday code.
    - **A clean fix that keeps everything:** the number is the date and the colour is the weekday. "The first dot carries 21, and its colour is Monday's. The twenty-first falls on a Monday in three days — and not again until July." (21 April 2014 and 21 July 2014 were both Mondays.) This makes "three days or three months" literally correct and matches the Tallinn chapters starting 21 April.

20. **"Viciebsk borders Russia" (ch 31).** The *region* (Vitebsk oblast) borders Russia; the city is about 40 km from it. The open Belarus–Russia border (the Union State) is correct.
    - **Fix:** "Viciebsk is a short drive from the Russian border…". The spelling "Viciebsk" is used consistently across the book. Keep it, but note that "Vitebsk" is far more familiar to English readers; this is an author's choice.

21. **"Twelve feet long picture frame" (ch 33)** in a "small dwell" is absurd. Use "a large framed photograph".

### C. Plot holes and unmotivated actions

22. **The guns left in the field ch 37, ch 40].** Sanchez "knew both possessed guns" (ch 37) and has just seen one fly from William's hand, yet she and Stephen run off and leave both weapons in the grass. Marty then picks one up and shoots her (ch 40). The plot turns on a professional's elementary mistake.
    - **Fix:** she grabs one gun (and later drops it when shot, or hands it to Stephen). Marty uses *his own* pistol, or one that landed in the tall grass where she couldn't find it: "She searched the grass for the second pistol; it was nowhere."

23. **The dead police officer is not seen [ch 36, ch 39].**
    - Ch36 has two thugs "in a scuffle with a man" (Maxwell's officer).
    - Ch43 finds him "lying in a pool of blood" in the backyard, "shot twice on the chest".
    - Sanchez and Stephen pass through that backyard in ch39 [ch 39] and see nothing. Sanchez even thinks "all four intruders were lying unconscious in the backyard". Nobody hears the two shots that killed him.
    - **Fix:** let them find the body (a huge stakes-raiser, and a clue for Sanchez that someone else is protecting her). Or move the officer's fight to the lane out front and let Sanchez hear the two shots as she slides down the drainpipe. That would also explain the "loud screaming voice" (ch 36).

24. **Sanchez "in her sleepwear … pajama pockets" (ch 37)** in the early or late afternoon, minutes after arriving and "busy unpacking" (ch 33).
    - **Fix:** cut it, or motivate it (she had showered and changed into a T-shirt and track pants). It matters because it explains why she has no phone or weapon on her.

25. **The car keys ch 39.** Adriana says the keys are on the kitchen table. Nobody fetches them, yet Stephen "hurled the key in the ignition".
    - **Fix:** "Stephen snatched the keys from the kitchen table and sprinted…".

26. **Adriana's car or van.** It is "the car" ch 39, then "the old Peugeot van" (ch 39), then "the car" again. Pick one.

27. **"Adam" (ch 35)** is named once and never appears again in the manuscript. In ch48 (book.md l.1869) Benedikt explains that he found Adriana through John Patterson's phone records.
    - **Fix:** name Adam at his first appearance (the trusted man Benedikt calls in ch8, l.361–365), or replace the name here with "the man he had trusted with the Sanchez file".

28. **The U-turn is unmotivated ch 35, ch 34].** Benedikt, desperate for Sanchez, turns back to Lisbon after a short call, and "Benedikt knew this was his chance" is never explained. Readers will assume a trap for Maxwell, and none comes.
    - **Fix:** let the second call say that a policeman is at the house: "A policeman. Turn round. I will not be seen within fifty miles of that house. Bring her to me." This justifies the U-turn and the warehouse rendezvous, and it plants his ch44 line ("That police officer at Nazaré wasn't an accident").

29. **"Get them and meet me at the warehouse" (ch 35)** comes at about 2:05 PM. So why do the thugs wait (in the text) until 4:30 PM to go in?
    - **Fix:** the retiming in item 4 closes this.

30. **What Maxwell hears on the bug [ch 34 vs ch 35].**
    - Maxwell hears "We need to be there as soon as possible" through a bug that is in the van, while in ch35 Benedikt says "We need to go right now" indoors.
    - The bug then dies (because of the gun, as ch44 reveals). That is fine, and a nice setup, but make the two lines identical and have Benedikt say it in the van.

31. **Maxwell's knowledge of Adriana [ch 32, ch 34].** He "knew where she was headed" and posted a guard, and later wonders "How did Benedikt found about Adriana?" The reader has not been told how Maxwell knows. If earlier chapters don't establish it, add a clause.

32. **"That ill-fated night" (ch 33).** Adriana expects Sanchez to ask about it. The phrase appears nowhere else in the book, and the photo of Adriana's "partner" (ch 33) is never followed up.
    - Adriana's evening dress ("waiting for her date") (ch 33), her "flat" phone voice (ch 33) and her later stony silence in ch41 ("demonstrated no penitence") all read as setup for a betrayal, and none comes.
    - **Fix:** either pay these off (her partner vanished with Sanchez's father; she has a date because she's rebuilding her life; she's in contact with someone) or trim them so readers aren't primed for a twist that never arrives.

33. **Adriana's map work is dropped.** She was to "enlarge and print" the map [ch 33] and give a second opinion (ch 33). After the raid it is never mentioned.
    - **Also:** Sanchez's phone, the *only* surviving copy of the map after Jules's shredding ch 31, is left in a house the thugs "ransacked" (ch 39). Does Benedikt get it?
    - **Fix:** one line in ch39 settles it: Sanchez snatches her phone from the wrecked room, or finds it gone, which is a stakes-raiser.

34. **Sanchez's fighting ability [ch 36, ch 37.** She knocks out two men with an elbow and a groin strike, and another two with a kick and a chokehold. Damian has called her "well trained" earlier, but the reader has not seen where she trained. If an origin is established elsewhere, fine. If not, plant one line before ch36.

35. **The choke "to a point he was breathless … unconscious" (ch 37)** is followed by Stephen kicking the *other* man. Positions are unclear:
    - (ch 37): the man "checking on their friends" (William) is behind Sanchez, and his partner is guarding Stephen.
    - ch 37: she kicks the man behind her (William), then chokes the other. Stephen then kicks "the person who was guarding Sanchez", who is William again.
    - Ch43 says William is "the last man whom Sanchez knocked off".
    - Choreograph it once, cleanly.

36. **Authorial foreshadowing with no payoff (ch 37):** "Something they would regret for the rest of their life." Nothing is regretted. Cut it.

37. **Structure: PART IV (ch 36)** falls between ch36 and ch37, in the middle of a gunpoint scene. Parts II and III have no headers in book.md at all (only PART I, IV and V exist), so the part structure itself needs repair. A part break mid-scene reads as an accident. Move PART IV to the start of the Tallinn movement (ch49) or to the capture (ch42), and supply the missing II and III headers.

## 4. Pacing and flow

- **Ch31 opens slowly ch 31.** About 700 words of waking, view, breakfast and study go by before the plot moves. The view paragraph is lovely but overlong, and the "promise … not wasting time" beat is stated twice [ch 31]. Trim the waking to half and keep the mother and the soup, which are character.
- **The Jules decoding ch 31** is the right length and one of the best-paced scenes in the section. Weaken it only by removing the reaction beats: "Sanchez and Stephen had no clue" (ch 31), "puzzled looks" (ch 31), "captivated" (ch 31), "astounded" (ch 31). That is four reaction shots in ten paragraphs.
- **Ch32 is a stub (3 paragraphs) and misplaced.** Merge it into ch30.
- **Ch33's house tour ch 33** is about 600 words of exterior, fence, gate, door, hinge and interior before any dialogue. For a scene that ends in a raid, the description works only if it pays off (escape routes, drainpipe, basement, garage). The *useful* details (drainpipe, front and back rooms, detached garage, basement) are missing from the tour and are invented on the fly later. **Swap the decorative details for the tactical ones.**
- **Ch34 and ch35 are both dated 2:00 PM and cover the same call from both ends.** The cross-cut is a good idea, but ch35 is thin (the silent lunch, Francis sulking) and ends vaguely. They could merge into one chapter that alternates the two cars.
- **The Benedikt phone beats repeat three times.** Ch35 has the calls, ch37 William's call, ch38 the same call from Benedikt's end, and ch40 Marty's call. Each opens with "What took you so long?" [ch 37, ch 38, ch 40] and each henchman is "petrified" at speaking to the chief. **Merge ch38 into ch37** (a cutaway to the van while the phone rings in Joseph's pocket) and cut Damian's paragraph of diagnosis (ch 38) to two sentences.
- **Ch36–37's two fights overlap heavily:** the fragile-looking-girl-beats-men beat, the gunman's disbelief, then the same thing again. The second fight ch 37 is better than the first because it has a plan and a bend in the road. Consider shortening the first (one man down, then the gunshot) so the second lands harder.
- **Ch37's search ch 37** is overwritten, especially the "refrained from taking any advantage … any shade of lust" aside. Cut it to three lines.
- **Ch39 is well shaped:** the ransacked house, the search, Adriana in the basement, the dash to the car, the shot, the separation. It is the emotional peak of the section. The only drag is Adriana's recovery ch 39.
- **Ch40 retells ch39's shooting from Marty's side.** Worth keeping for his POV, but cut the duplication. Start at the moment he raises the gun, and skip the waking-up sequence (ch 40) or compress it to two lines. The drag-to-car business ch 40 can also be tightened.
- **Momentum overall:** from ch33's raid to ch40 the section is essentially one continuous 90-minute action sequence spread over eight short chapters with five POVs. The constant datelines and POV resets work against the propulsion. A tighter version would run: ch33 raid → ch34/35 merged (cars) → ch36 fight 1 → ch37 (with the ch38 cutaway) fight 2 → ch39 shooting → ch40 Marty (shortened).

## 5. Prose problems (section-specific)

**1. Malapropisms and wrong words.** This is the most damaging issue for an agent's first read.
- "There she was! **Sacred.** Sitting in the corner" (ch 39). This means *Scared*, and it reads as unintentional comedy at a tense moment.
- "traces of the **rouges**" (ch 39): *rogues*.
- "the car engine **revered** to life" (ch 39): *revved*.
- "**Shaving off all inhabitations** he aimed at Sanchez's leg" (ch 40): *Shaking off all inhibitions*.
- "Sanchez didn't **buzz**" (ch 40): *budge*. The same error recurs at book.md l.1869.
- "they **equivocally** expressed agreement" (ch 31): this means the opposite; the author wants *unequivocally*.
- "She was a short woman, **stoically** built" (ch 31): *stockily*.
- "she **lowered** the curtains and the room was filled with bright sunlight" (ch 31): *drew back / opened*.
- "Her eyes appeared lost, **starring** in the vacuum" (ch 39): *staring*.
- "in a state of **disbelieve**" (ch 37), "with **disbelieve**" (ch 39): *disbelief*.
- "any other **reckon force**" (ch 40): *reckoning*, or simply "any more fighting".
- "with utmost **suave**" (ch 37): *suavely / smoothly*.
- "She **hurled** to the bathroom" (ch 33): *hurried*.

**2. Register mismatches (a thesaurus word where a plain one belongs).**
- "the **ligneous** house" (ch 33).
- "the midafternoon sun's **coruscating** light" (ch 33).
- "a sense of achievement reflected from his **orotund** voice" (ch 35), repeated at (ch 40).
- "it appeared **aberrantly unusual**" (ch 34).
- "confirming the **perturbations** he was contemplating with" (ch 34).
- "the monologue of the motor humming sound" (ch 40).

**3. The same verbs over and over:** "sauntered" x4, including an injured man who "**sauntered his way** towards the house" (ch 40) and gunmen who "commanded them to **saunter**" (ch 37); "blurted" x4, for lines that are not blurted [ch 33, ch 37]; "delirious" x3, for *agitated* [ch 35, ch 38, ch 39]; "perplexed/puzzled" x6; "loud" x14.

**4. Emotion labelled, not dramatised.**
- "His agitation was at the extreme." (ch 34)
- "His mind was convoluted." (ch 34)
- "Sanchez was shattered to pieces. Her mission got curtailed too fast too soon." (ch 36) This flattens the best cliffhanger in the section. End on the gun instead.

**5. Head-hopping inside scenes.**
- Ch36 moves from Sanchez to Adriana to the intruders to Sanchez in five paragraphs ch 36.
- Ch37 moves from the gunman to William to Sanchez ch 37.
- Ch40 moves from Marty to Sanchez falling asleep (ch 40).
- Pick one POV per scene and put a line break before switching.

**6. Missing possessives and articles** (see §2): "Jules mother", "Stephen curiosity", "Benedikt restlessness", "It took her few minutes" (ch 31), "After few minutes" (ch 33), "for few seconds" (ch 37).

**7. Dialogue punctuation.**
- The opening quote mark is missing at (ch 33) (`I am Sanchez Roberto…`).
- There is a stray closing quote after "BANG”" (ch 39).
- Punctuation falls outside the quotes (`“Come to the kitchen”, Jules shouted` (ch 31)).
- A question mark ends a command: "…if you see anything unusual call me?" (ch 34).
- "Let us get freshen up" (ch 31) should be "Let's get freshened up."

**8. Contradictory tags.** "This time with a **belligerent** tone he stated, 'How far are you?'" (ch 40), followed by "Benedikt tone was much **calmer** this time" (ch 40).

**9. Authorial intrusion and odd asides.**
- "Something they would regret for the rest of their life." (ch 37)
- "He was thrilled about something" (ch 40) (what?).
- "It was bizarre as he was worried more about the girl than the man" (ch 37).

**10. "The girl" (21 uses) and "a fragile looking girl" (x2 in narration, [ch 36, ch 37]).** In henchman POV it is fine once. As the narrator's default it reads as condescending and dates the prose. Vary it: Sanchez, she, the woman.

**11. Pronoun ambiguity.** "He was known for his calm stature, never giving away anything … He never recalled once when he had raised his voice" (ch 38). The two "he"s are Benedikt and Damian, but the sentence reads as if they are the same person.

## 6. Strengths

- **The Jules chapter has real warmth.** Jules's mother in her bouffant skirt and red kerchief, the *sopas de cavalo cansado* ("red wine with old breadcrumbs. According to the lady, it provides great strength to face the day" (ch 31)) and the cluttered study make one of the few genuinely *lived-in* scenes so far. Protect it.
- **The decoding scene is a strong set piece.** It turns a MacGuffin into a concrete itinerary (Tallinn, Ventspils, Gdynia, Warsaw, Viciebsk) that powers Parts IV–V. Jules's "I knew this was coming" (ch 31) and his mischievous smile give him voice. Once the colour/number logic is fixed (§3 item 19) this is a scene a reader will remember.
- **Adriana's entrance is an excellent unsettling image:** the derelict house, then a woman in diamonds, an evening dress and Miss Dior at lunchtime. "It appeared as if she was waiting for her date instead of them." (ch 33) This is intrigue done well. It just needs a payoff.
- **The parallel calls.** "They are here!!" (ch 34) (to Maxwell) against "We saw them entering the house!!" (ch 35) (to Benedikt) is a smart cross-cut showing both hunters closing on the same door.
- **The bug and the gun.** Francis tossing his pistol into the glove compartment (ch 35), so that the transmitter dies and Maxwell loses the van (ch 34), is a lovely, understated accident, paid off in ch44. Keep it exactly as quiet as it is.
- **The drainpipe motif.** Sanchez escaping by drainpipe (ch 36) echoes her hotel escape earlier (book.md l.131), and the thug climbing a drainpipe (ch 33) mirrors her. Stephen's wry line is a good voice moment: "The height was manageable, even if he would have fallen, it wouldn't have killed him." (ch 36)
- **The separation at the car** is the best writing in the section:
  - "Don't be a fool. Can't you hear the footsteps?" (ch 39)
  - "The screeching sound of the tires was dreadful as if providing its final resistance from leaving Sanchez behind. … He kept looking at the rear mirror till the house faded away from distance." (ch 39)
  - A sister ordering her brother to abandon her, and him obeying in tears, is a strong character beat, and it sets up his guilt and Maxwell's contempt in ch41.
- **Low-level henchman POV.** William's dread ("It's the chief" (ch 37)) and Marty facing Sanchez's fearless stare ("He had been in several occasions pointing guns at people before, and every time he had seen fear… Never once he had encountered anyone with such grit" (ch 40)) humanise the opposition and build Sanchez through enemy eyes. That is a better device than narrator praise.
- **Benedikt seen through Damian** ch 38. A decade-long lieutenant who has never seen his boss lose control is an efficient way to show that Benedikt's obsession is personal. It foreshadows the ch42/44 romantic turn.
- **The second fight's plan** ch 37: picking the bend in the path, closing her eyes, counting it down. This is good suspense construction.

## 7. Honest assessment

Chapters 31–40 have the raw material for the book's first real action movement: a warm safe-house interlude, a clever decoding scene that sets the destination for the rest of the novel, an eerie hostess, two hunters converging on one house, a double fight, and a sister sacrificing herself so her brother can escape. The scene *ideas* are commercial, and several moments (Adriana in her evening dress, the bug killed by a thrown gun, Stephen watching Sanchez shrink in the mirror) show real instinct. What sinks the section on a first read is not the story. It is that the machinery doesn't hold together. Datelines contradict the text and each other: ch32 is a day early, the break-in jumps from about 2 PM to 4:30 PM mid-scene, ch38 comes before ch37, and three chapters labelled "Nazaré" happen in Lisbon. The drive times are impossible: 30 minutes and 20 minutes for a 120 km trip, on the wrong motorway. The heroine loses the fight because she leaves two loaded guns in the grass. A policeman is shot dead in a backyard she walks through without noticing. An agent or editor who notices one of these starts looking for the next, and in this section they will find them.

The prose problems are the same as the rest of the book but come thicker under action pressure. "Sacred" for scared, "rouges", "revered", "Shaving off all inhabitations" and "didn't buzz" all land at moments of maximum tension, where an unintended laugh does the most harm. The head-hopping and triple-told phone calls sap the momentum the fights build.

**Single most important fix:** rebuild the afternoon of 18 April as one coherent clock and map. Put ch32 back before ch31. Retime the arrival at Adriana's to about 3:45 PM so that Maxwell's drive (A8, well over an hour) and Benedikt's U-turn make him arrive at about 5:40 as ch41 requires. Fold ch38 into ch37. Correct the datelines to Lisbon where needed. Make Marty's trip to the Lisbon warehouse take 90 minutes, not 20. Close the two biggest plot holes along the way: Sanchez takes a gun (Marty shoots her with another), and they see or hear the police officer's death. Once the timeline and logistics hold, the strong scenes in this section can work.
