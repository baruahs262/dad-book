## 1. Chapter-by-chapter summary

### Chapter 16 — *"18th June 2012, Ayr, Scotland"* (ch 16)
- **POV:** Sanchez (close third).
- **Setting:** Sanchez's flat, then John Patterson's house in Ayr, on a "Sunday afternoon" that the text also calls "cold".
- **Beats:**
  - Two months after her last confrontation with John, Sanchez rereads her father's notebook and notices that John's name appears in many places: against addresses, in footnotes and on to-do lists. She has an "epiphany" (ch 16).
  - She goes straight to John's house, wakes him by hammering on the door, stops him closing it on her and threatens him with the police (ch 16).
  - John gives in: "It will ruin us." ch 16.
  - She promises him anonymity (ch 16). He mentions "that package" (ch 16), and says the package instruction came from head office, from **Evan Slender**, head of sales operations for Scotland and England (ch 16).
  - He will not say why he is so frightened (ch 16). She leaves with a sense of accomplishment mixed with uncertainty ch 16.
- **What the reader learns:** a package was involved in whatever happened to her father, and the order came from Evan Slender at TransPacific's head office.
- **Ending hook:** weak. The chapter ends on "more questions than answers" (ch 16), stated rather than dramatised.

### Chapter 17 — *"1st May 2012, Ayr, Scotland"* (ch 17)
- **POV:** Sanchez.
- **Setting:** TransPacific HQ, the executive floor conference room.
- **Beats:**
  - She looks Evan up in the global address list. He is executive chairman of sales operations, a co-founder, early fifties, in the Forbes top 300, and almost invisible in public (ch 17).
  - "Months went by" with no progress. Her process-quality idea is shortlisted as one of the top three innovations in the company, and Evan is the chief guest at the judging ch 17.
  - She prepares a pilot and quantified results (ch 17). There is a long description of the octagonal executive conference room (ch 17).
  - Five judges attend, Evan among them, along with Dave and other business managers (ch 17). She presents last, with £5M savings over five years and a working prototype (ch 17).
  - She wins. Part of the prize is working directly with Evan's office. She cries ch 17.
  - **Bradley Faucet** from Evan's executive office introduces himself and tells her to report to him the next day (ch 17).
- **What the reader learns:** Evan is powerful and secretive, and Sanchez is now inside his office.
- **Ending hook:** moderate. Bradley's card and brisk exit (ch 17).

### Chapter 18 — *"15th May 2012, Ayr, Scotland"* (ch 18; the Part III header is ch 18)
- **POV:** Sanchez.
- **Setting:** Evan's office floor, Bradley's cabin, an Italian coffee shop, then a summary covering about a year.
- **Beats:**
  - Bradley becomes her manager. He is capable and always around, which blocks her snooping ch 18. She starts arriving at 6:00 AM to search files and drawers and finds nothing (ch 18).
  - When a high-profile visitor comes, the floor is sealed off. Evan's assistant, over lunch, lets slip the name **Benedikt** ch 18.
  - Bradley catches her arriving early and says he has video of her searching ch 18. She panics and blurts out Benedikt's name ch 18.
  - Bradley turns: "I have no interest in why you are sniffing around. However, I am interested in you." ch 18.
  - At the coffee shop he says Evan runs a smuggling route from Europe to Russia, that Benedikt is the "custodian" of the quarterly route plan, and that executives, officials and politicians are involved. He blackmails her into helping him (ch 18).
  - He refuses to explain himself ch 18. He orders her into gym, kickboxing, karate and rock climbing, "You are already enrolled" (ch 18).
  - A year of training follows, then "the discussion about getting details of the map from Benedikt" (ch 18). She leaves TransPacific, never sees Bradley again, and credits him for the lead and the training that won her the map (ch 18).
- **What the reader learns:** how Sanchez got to Benedikt and where her skills came from. (Later chapters reveal that Bradley is Maxwell of Scotland Yard, book.md l.2127–2129.)
- **Ending hook:** it closes Part II by pointing back to the map theft in Ch2–3, which is a satisfying loop. But the most dramatic material, planning the Stockholm operation, is covered in one sentence.

### Chapter 19 — *"4:00 AM 15th April 2014, Zurich, Switzerland"* (ch 19)
- **POV:** Maxwell. This is the first time he is named. He is the "shadow" of Ch4–5 and the "Trevor" of Ch7/9.
- **Setting:** Zurich station, Zurich streets, the A3 motorway, a dead-end lane of apartment blocks outside the city, and a petrol-station café.
- **Beats:**
  - The train halts and Maxwell wakes to find Sanchez gone. He sees her get into a BMW. His plan had been to tail her at Basel ch 19.
  - He flashes ID at a taxi driver and follows the BMW, which starts making evasive turns ch 19.
  - He sees the BMW turn into an apartment complex, drops his taxi, calls a second cab through a ride-share app and waits in a corner café. A young man (Stephen) comes out to check the street and Maxwell stays hidden. This mirrors Ch8 from the other side ch 19.
  - He makes the cab driver move to the passenger seat and follows the BMW about two hours along the A3 to a lane marked "Kein Ausgang" ch 19.
  - He finds the BMW parked outside a block of two-storey flats (ch 19).
  - At a petrol-station café he eats, makes a call, and a man in his mid-thirties hands over an Audi R8 and a packet (ch 19).
  - He fixes a GPS transmitter under the BMW's boot and sleeps in the Audi ch 19.
- **What the reader learns:** who the shadow is, that he is professional and has some official ID, and that he now has a tracker on Sanchez's car.
- **Ending hook:** quiet. He trusts the receiver to wake him.

## 3. Continuity, logic and factual errors

### A. Master timeline problem (highest priority)
The datelines in Part III contradict each other and contradict Part I.

1. **Ch19 (ch 19) is dated 15 April, but its events belong to 13 April.** Ch19 retells the Zurich arrival already shown in Ch5 ("4:00 AM 13th April 2014, Zurich") and Ch8 ("4:30 AM 13th April"). The details match: the BMW at the exit, the evasive driving, the hide in an apartment complex, the pursuer passing, and Stephen checking the street.
   - Ch8 calls the day "a weekend" (13 April 2014 was a Sunday). ch 19 calls it "a lazy Tuesday", which matches the wrong dateline.
   - *Fix:* date Ch19 to the same day as Ch5/Ch8 and delete "Tuesday".
2. **Ch22 (ch 22) is dated 16 April, but it must be 17 April.**
   - In Ch21 (17 April, about 8:50 AM) the informant says the tickets were "bought only this morning" and the flight "departed" ch 21.
   - In Ch22 the pair buy tickets at the counter and the flight leaves 45 minutes later.
   - Ch24 has them landing in Lisbon at "12:00 PM 17th April".
   - So Ch22 happens on the morning of 17 April, between about 5:00 and 7:00. That is *before* Ch21's 7:00 AM landing. Ch22 also says the tracker was fitted "the day before" (ch 22), so Ch19 must be 16 April if Ch22 is the 17th.
   - **On the order question:** Ch21 before Ch22 is wrong on the dates, but it can work as a deliberate step back of a few hours. What cannot stand is a dateline a day *earlier* than the chapter it follows when the reader has no way to see why. There are two options:
     - (a) Redate Ch22 to "5:00 AM 17th April" and **move it ahead of Ch21**. The order becomes Ch19 → Ch20 (Benedikt, 4:00) → Ch22 (Maxwell, 5:00) → Ch21 (Benedikt, 7:00) → Ch23. The reader then watches Benedikt race towards a flight they know has gone, which is good dramatic irony.
     - (b) Keep the current order but redate Ch22 to 17 April, so the jump back is a visible device.
   - I recommend (a).
3. **Ch10 already showed the escape flight, and the details disagree.** Ch10 (l.369–389, "14th April") has Stephen and Sanchez leave the flat at 5:00, drive to St. Gallen-Altenrhein for a pre-booked **9:00 AM** flight, and "the flight departed on time". Ch22 has them **buy tickets at the counter** with 45 minutes to go, and Maxwell reaches the airport around 6:00, which puts departure near 7:00. Ch21 has Benedikt told at about 8:50 that it has already left.
   - *Fix:* cut Ch10's airport and flight paragraphs so Ch10 ends as they drive off, and let Ch22 carry the airport scene. Make the departure about 7:00 everywhere.
4. **The whole Part I/III calendar needs one decision.** If Lisbon stays on 17 April (the rest of the book is built on it: Tallinn on Monday 21st, Sub Rosa on Wednesday 23rd, Ventspils on the 23rd), then the Stockholm theft has to move from the night of 12–13 April to the night of 15–16 April.
   - That puts the Zurich arrival (Ch5/8/19) on 16 April, a Wednesday, and the flight (Ch10/22) on 17 April. Ch8's "rest for the night and leave first thing tomorrow" then works.
   - As it stands, Benedikt's team takes **four days** to check the Zurich station CCTV (ch 20) after he had personally narrowed the search to Zurich and demanded results "in next four hours" (Ch9, l.177). That makes him look incompetent.
   - The S01/S02 editors should be told.
5. **Ch23 is dated 9:00 AM, which is physically impossible.** Benedikt was stuck in traffic at 8:35 (ch 21), reached the Altenrhein rental office after that, and then faced an "hour-long ride" back to the Zurich jet (ch 21). The earliest realistic time is about 10:00. ch 21 says "next 20 minutes", which contradicts the "hour-long ride" in ch 21.
   - *Fix:* date Ch23 "10:00 AM" and make ch 21 "the last twenty minutes of the drive".
6. **Ch16–18 datelines run backwards.** Ch16 is **18 June 2012**, then Ch17 is **1 May 2012** and Ch18 **15 May 2012**. The texts themselves cover "Months went by" (ch 17), "two months in her new role … next few months" (ch 18) and "For the next one year" (ch 18).
   - Ch16's "Two months had gone by since her last confrontation" fits the 30 April confrontation in Ch15, so Ch16 is about right. Ch17 must come later, say September 2012, and Ch18 later still.
   - 18 June 2012 was a Monday, not a "Sunday afternoon" (ch 16).
   - *Fix:* redate Ch17 to about "September 2012" and Ch18 to about "November 2012", or give Ch18 a date range. Change "Sunday" or the date.
7. **Season errors in Ch16.** Mid-June in Ayr is called "a cold afternoon" (ch 16). ch 16 mentions "the rough edges of the morning brittle cold" on her cheek during an *afternoon* visit. *Fix:* grey, drizzly June, and drop "morning".
8. **Ch18's internal sequence is muddled.** She starts the 6:00 AM visits in ch 18, before the "two months in her new role" of ch 18. Bradley then catches her "a week of her early morning ventures" in (ch 18), which would be months later.
   - *Fix:* move ch 18 after ch 18, so the Benedikt lockdowns motivate the early mornings, and keep "a week" as it is.
9. **Poker visits before 2014 don't fit this timeline.** Ch7 says "Rita" came to the Stockholm tournament twice before. Ch18 has her recruited for the map job only after "about a year" of training, from about mid-2012. An April 2013 visit is just possible; April 2012 is not, since she had only just started her internship. Flag for S01.

### B. Geography and factual errors
10. **"Stockholm, Denmark"** (ch 20). Stockholm is in Sweden, and the same error runs through Ch2–9. Ch2 calls it "this small Danish town" with a 40-minute train to Copenhagen, uses kronor, and has "rich Danish coffee".
    - The simplest fix is to relocate to **Helsingør** (Elsinore), a small Danish town with a castle, about 45 minutes from Copenhagen by train.
    - For ch 20: from Helsingør a jet can reach Zurich in about 1h40, so "Zurich by 6:30" becomes plausible. From Stockholm it is 2h20+ in the air, which is why he actually lands "shy of 7:00" (ch 21).
11. **"Kein Ausgang"** (ch 19). That means "no exit" as in a door. A Swiss dead-end road is signed **"Sackgasse"**, or just the white T pictogram on blue. The signs are blue, not yellow.
12. **160 km/h on the A1** (ch 21), described as "the highest speed one could possibly drive without getting into trouble" (ch 21). The Swiss motorway limit is 120 km/h, and 160 counts as a gross offence under the "Raser" rules. *Fix:* "130, the most a Swiss motorway would forgive".
13. **"the Ritz close to the St. Gallen-Altenrhein airport"** (ch 21). There is no Ritz there. Use "a hotel by the lake in Rorschach" or "a lakeside hotel near the airfield".
14. **TAP from St. Gallen-Altenrhein** [ch 22]. TAP has never flown from Altenrhein, a tiny regional airport whose scheduled service was essentially Vienna. "TAP020" is also not a real flight-number format: TAP's code is **TP**, as in "TP 921".
    - *Options:* have them fly from Friedrichshafen or Zurich; or route via Vienna, with Maxwell buying the same connection; or keep Altenrhein but make it a small charter or regional flight with a connection. At the very least use "TP" numbering.
15. **Jet terminology.** "final step of the jet bridge" (ch 21) should be the air-stairs, since a private jet does not use a jet bridge. "retractable air stairs flung open from the clamshell-shaped plane door" (ch 21) is fine.
16. **A3 route and timing** (ch 19). Two hours on the A3 from Zurich overshoots the whole A3 corridor to Sargans, which is about an hour. In Ch22 the drive to Altenrhein is then "about an hour" (ch 22). If the flat is near Sargans or the Rhine valley, say "the better part of an hour on the A3, then the A13 north". Ch10 says they "headed south to St. Gallen", but Altenrhein is north-east of Zurich (flag for S02).
17. **Ayr's scale.** A 15-storey glass tower with a 100-ft atrium and a "port city" view (ch 17). Ayr is a town of about 46,000 with no such building, and the Stockholm hotel suite is also on the "15th floor". Either fictionalise the town or tone the building down. The duplicate 15th floor should go either way.
18. **Evan's position is described two ways.** John calls him the man "who heads sales operations for Scotland and England" (ch 16). Ch17 makes him a co-founder, executive chairman and billionaire (ch 17). A founder-billionaire as chief guest at an intern contest also strains belief.
    - *Fix:* keep him as the regional head of sales operations (UK), a wealthy senior figure, and drop the Forbes line. Or make John's line "the man who runs sales for the whole of Britain — one of the founders".
19. **Sechseläuten "spring festival weekend"** (ch 19) at 4:00 AM, with busy streets, sits against "a lazy Tuesday" (ch 19). Check the 2014 Sechseläuten date. In any case, 4 AM streets would not be "busy". Use "the streets were already stirring for the Sechseläuten weekend".

### C. Plot holes and unmotivated actions
20. **Ch16's "epiphany" gives her no leverage.** John's name in the notebook was already the clue that brought her to Ayr (Ch12). Noticing it appears "at many places" (ch 16) tells her nothing new and gives John no reason to fear the police.
    - *Fix:* let her find something specific, such as a to-do entry "Collect pkg from J.P. — Girbin — 13/2", dated the day before her father vanished. That turns "you'd need to answer the police" (ch 16) into a real threat.
21. **"That package" (ch 16) comes from nowhere.** The reader has never heard of a package connected to the father's disappearance. The irony is that Sanchez's own fake "packet" ruse in Ch15 now looks like an accident. Either seed the package in Ch1 or Ch11–12, or make the rhyme deliberate, with John flinching at her fake packet in Ch15.
22. **ch 16 isn't a revelation.** "a confirmation that the answer … was in TransPacific" is something she has known since Ch12–15. Cut it.
23. **Bradley's recruitment strains belief** ch 18.
    - A Scotland Yard undercover officer blackmails a first-year student intern with CCTV, sends her into a year of combat training, and has her seduce and drug a trafficking kingpin in the Ch2–3 heist. Later chapters need a real reckoning with this, beyond the passing sarcasm at l.2127. The present text never hints that he is anything but sinister ("shade of evilness in his smirk", ch 18).
    - He also knows a lot for someone who says he is "not sure about what they do" (ch 18). How does he know the route runs from Europe to Russia, is re-planned every three months, and that Benedikt keeps it?
    - *Fix:* keep the misdirection, but give one detail that pays off later, such as a single slip in his speech.
24. **The Stockholm heist is planned off-page** (ch 18). "After about a year they had their first real conversation, the discussion about getting details of the map from Benedikt." How did they know about the poker tournament, the map cylinder, the briefcase or the suite layout? The man waiting outside the hotel in Ch4 is presumably Maxwell. If they "never saw" each other again (ch 18), who planned the rope anchor and the escape? This is the biggest missed scene in Part II.
25. **Maxwell's "no support system"** (ch 19) conflicts with a man arriving within 30 minutes, at dawn in rural Switzerland, with an **Audi R8** and a tracker (ch 19). An R8 is also a two-seat supercar and the most conspicuous car possible for surveillance.
    - *Fix:* make it a grey Škoda or VW estate, set up in advance ("the Basel contingency, rerouted"), and delete "without any support system".
26. **Car type contradicts Part I.** Ch5 and Ch8 say the pursuer was a **grey Peugeot SUV**. Ch19 has Maxwell in a taxi and then a ride-share cab. *Fix:* "the only cab on the rank, a grey Peugeot SUV", and ideally keep one vehicle throughout.
27. **The two-taxi sequence is confusing** ch 19. Why dismiss the first taxi and summon a second? Why does the second driver end up in the passenger seat while Maxwell drives a stranger's cab for two hours? At ch 19 "the taxi fled with a screeching noise" means the driver took the wheel back, but that is never shown.
    - *Fix:* keep one taxi. Maxwell pays a fistful of francs to keep the meter running, stays in the back, and releases the driver at the end.
28. **Jurisdiction and ID.** Maxwell "flashed his identity card" to Swiss civilians [ch 19], to TAP staff [ch 22], and *hands the supervisor his visiting card* (ch 22). That is a British police warrant card in Switzerland, an undercover officer leaving a paper trail, and a supervisor releasing passenger data to a foreign ID.
    - *Fix:* have him present an Interpol liaison card, or better, make it cash and charm. He should never hand over a card. It is also a cheap, useful way for Benedikt's informant to learn about "the other person".
29. **"He had some chores to do before leaving the apartment"** (ch 19). He is not in an apartment. It should be "before they left the apartment".
30. **Tracker on a rental car.** The tracker is on a Hertz car that is returned that morning (ch 21). Benedikt's men are standing at that very rental desk. Missing that chance is fine, but either have Maxwell retrieve the tracker at Altenrhein or let Benedikt's men find it (a good beat: "someone else is following her").
31. **Sanchez and Stephen use their real names** for the car hire and the air tickets [ch 21, ch 22] after she used the "Rita" cover in Stockholm. Ch24 has Stephen admit, "We are liberal in using our identities". That partly defuses it, but Ch9 calls her a "seasoned professional", so give a reason (no time to get false papers).
32. **Benedikt drives an hour back to Zurich for his jet** ch 21] when Altenrhein takes business jets. He could have had the jet repositioned while he drove at 7:15.
    - *Fix:* at ch 21 Benedikt orders the jet flown to Altenrhein. Ch23 then happens there, which also removes the timing problem in item 5.
33. **Benedikt regresses on the motive.** Ch9 (l.167) has him conclude "It has to be that map", but ch 21 has him wonder if she is "a kleptomaniac and robbed me for fun". Doubt is fine, but phrase it as a defence against his fear: "Maybe — he wanted it to be — she was only a thief."
34. **The pursuer's picture.** In ch 20 Damian identifies "Trevor" on CCTV "approaching the exit". In ch 21 there is no good picture of him. *Fix:* in ch 20 have him say "a hooded man — we think it's Trevor".
35. **ch 21 "I might send you a picture of another person"** when they have none. Keep it, but have Benedikt say "if we get one".

37. **Head-hop and authorial intrusion** ch 21. The POV slides into Damian's head: "a mystery that Damian would never be able to solve". This is also physically odd, since in a small jet cabin "from a distance" is impossible.

## 4. Pacing and flow

- **Ch16–18 rush the story and linger on the furniture.** Two years of story pass in about 3,000 words, and the space goes to the wrong things:
  - The octagonal conference room gets 250 words of inventory (Aeron chairs, Waterman pens, hard-bound books) (ch 17).
  - The win itself gets two sentences (ch 17).
  - The year of training and the entire heist planning get one sentence each (ch 18).
  - *Recommendation:* cut ch 17 to three lines and write out one scene of the Bradley–Sanchez "map" conversation (about 800–1,200 words). It sets up Ch2–3 and seeds the Bradley = Maxwell reveal.
- **Ch16 hangs on an unmotivated turn.** John folds too fast because we never see what she found (see §3.20). The scene has one good exchange and would grow from 736 to about 1,000 words with the fix.
- **Ch17's "Months went by…" and "She was bereft of recourse but not hope"** (ch 17) is a summary of a summary. Fold it into the start of the contest beat.
- **Ch19 is procedural drag.** 1,720 words of turn-by-turn pursuit: taxi, second taxi, café, taxi again, A3, lane, 600 feet, blocks, parking rows, café, sandwich, coffee, phone call, R8, tracker, sleep. The coffee-shop counter-surveillance ch 19 is the best part. The A3 run (ch 19), the parking-lot geometry (ch 19) and the sandwich (ch 19) can each lose half. Target about 1,200 words.
- **The hunger motif is repeated five times** in Maxwell's chapters: coffee and sandwich (ch 19), "enough time to get a good breakfast" (ch 22), "famished being won the battle" (ch 22), "his hunger crawled back" plus the egg muffin (ch 22), and the muffin aphorism (ch 22). Ch24 then repeats it for Stephen. Keep one (ch 22's line is charming) and cut the rest.
- **The Benedikt chapters repeat themselves.**
  - Ch20 (488 words) is essentially a phone call. It could open Ch21 as a short scene.
  - Ch21 and Ch23 both end on Benedikt in the jet taking a call that Damian is sent away from.
  - *Recommendation:* merge Ch20, Ch21 and Ch23 into one or two Benedikt chapters. Keep the arrival, the motorway frustration, "too late", the Lisbon order, and the superior's ultimatum as the chapter's closing turn. Drop the first mystery call, or keep it as the chapter hook, but don't have both calls.
- **Minute-by-minute timekeeping.** "minutes" appears 33 times in 9,100 words ("fifteen minutes", "ten minutes into the wait", "five minutes' lead"). Some ticking clock suits a chase; this much reads like a logbook. Keep the times that matter (flight in 45 minutes, 8:35 on the dashboard) and cut the rest.
- **Scene order.** See §3.2. The recommended order is Ch19 → Ch20 → Ch22 → Ch21 → Ch23, or a merged Maxwell chapter (Ch19 + Ch22 with a section break) followed by a merged Benedikt chapter.
- **Part break.** The Part III header (ch 18) sits after Ch18. Part II ends well on ch 18, which loops back to the map. But Part III then opens by *replaying* Part I's Zurich arrival from a new POV without telling the reader. Fixing the dateline to the Ch5 date, or adding an epigraph-style dateline, would make the rewind deliberate.
- **Info-dumps.**
  - Bradley's coffee-shop speech (ch 18) packs the whole conspiracy into one paragraph, with a threat on the end.
  - Benedikt's "three days / Tallinn Monday / Sub Rosa Wednesday" (ch 21) is well placed and short, and is a model for the rest.

## 5. Prose problems (section-specific)

**a. Wrong-word choices (malapropisms).** This is the most frequent problem and the one most likely to get a query rejected on page one.
1. "Hesitantly with a low tone, he **blazoned**" (ch 16): "murmured".
2. "a shade of evilness in his smirk that made her **obnoxious** and uncomfortable" (ch 18): "uneasy".
3. "He went into a deep **subornation**" (ch 21): "rumination" (subornation means procuring perjury).
4. "I want you to trace and **captivate** two people" (ch 21): "take".
5. "Maxwell took the lady with utmost **incredulity**" (ch 22): "Maxwell had taken the lady completely by surprise".
6. "Every time their brief **innuendo** crossed his mind" (ch 23): "their brief night together".
7. "looking stark **apposite**" (ch 17): probably "stark opposite" or "starkly composed". Recast.
8. Also: "hard **bond** books" (ch 17) for "hard-bound"; "a mystique character from some gothic settings" (ch 18) for "mysterious"; "despise the thoughts" (ch 18) for "dismissed"; "aptly **complimenting** his demeanor" (ch 21) for "complementing"; "with a **passive** voice blurted" (ch 21) for "level"; "an unperturbed dissonance" (ch 23), which contradicts itself; "a hazy **occult**" (ch 22); "report the **incidence**" (ch 22), "the incidence repeat" (ch 18) for "incident"; "a happy **campaigner**" (ch 21) for "camper"; "a **gratuitous** excitement" (ch 16), "any **gratuitous** situation" (ch 22) for "unbidden" / "unforeseen"; "an epiphany moment" (ch 16), which is redundant.

**b. Over-dressed dialogue tags and the "blurted" tic.** "blurted" appears 11 times, and announced / declared / stated 15 times, often for calm speech.
9. "with a grating tone screamed" (ch 21); "Benedikt snapped the phone and screamed in frustration" (ch 21); "and blurted with a grating voice" (ch 21), all within a page.
10. "in an orotund voice she exclaimed" (ch 16).
Most tags should be "said", or no tag at all.

**c. Stacked or contradictory emotion words and clichés.**
11. "With an insipid look, she entered his cabin. She was petrified … couldn't shrug away her startled mien. … The earth caved on her, and her mind went numb." (ch 18). That is four clichés in three sentences. Pick one image.
12. "Despite the sheer darkness, it would be hard for anyone to miss the tremulous smile his face graced." (ch 19).
13. "Her over indulgent mind was delirious to find some headway but was miserably failing." (ch 16). "delirious" should be "desperate".

**d. Authorial telling and POV slips.**
14. "a mystery that Damian would never be able to solve" (ch 21).
15. "Gosh Am I in love? It can't be" (ch 23). The register breaks, and the thought is already clear from ch 23. Cut the line.

**e. Redundancy and double-telling.** "Benedikt looked at his watch it was 4:00 AM in the morning" (ch 20), directly under a 4:00 AM dateline. "He finally spoke." (ch 16) after he has just spoken. "No one knew the reason behind such unreasonable outlandish demand" (ch 18).

**f. Grammar and tense slips.** "If he leaves now he can be in Zurich by 6:30" (ch 20) should be "If he left now he could". "trailing a car on a highway is much easier" (ch 19) should be "was". "Make sure no one should come looking for them" (ch 22). "I was this close from nabbing her" (ch 21) should be "close to". "Benedikt senses" (ch 20), "Benedikt face" (ch 23) and "Maxwell tone" (ch 22) are missing possessives. "Maxwell Parked" (ch 19). "Sure, enough" (ch 19). "the Zurich airport private transfer Tarmac" (ch 21).

**g. Dialogue punctuation.** Dialogue opens in lower case: "“why don’t you leave me alone" (ch 16), "“it was something to do…" (ch 16), "“the damn municipality workers" (ch 21). Commas are missing before tags throughout: "“Go on” Benedikt…" (ch 20), "“Sure” Damian responded" (ch 21). Quote marks mix straight and curly (ch 16 closes without punctuation; ch 20 use straight quotes). This is a house-style pass, but it is fast to fix and has a big effect on how professional the pages look.

**h. American and British register mixing.** The POV characters are British or Scottish, but the text uses "gas station" (ch 19), "parking lot" (ch 19), "curb" [ch 19, ch 22], "rideshare app" and "cell", alongside British "boot" (ch 19). Pick British English for the Maxwell and Sanchez POVs.

**i. Numbers in narrative.** "5M pounds over 5 years" (ch 17) should be "five million pounds over five years". "160 KM/HR … 30 KM/HR" (ch 21) should be "km/h" or words. "about 7-10 blocks … 8 apartments … 2 by 2 lanes" (ch 19) is inventory prose and should be cut.

**j. Male-gaze boilerplate.** "A pretty looking female in her early twenties" (ch 22), and Bradley's "You are intelligent, beautiful and you are different" (ch 18). The second can stay as characterisation if the text registers that it is creepy. The first is a reflex and should become "a flight attendant".

**k. Formatting.** The Ch19–23 datelines are plain bold with "15th". Ch1–18 use bold italics with superscript ordinals. Standardise.

## 6. Strengths

- **The Bradley reversal (Ch18) is the best scene in the section.** It is a real turn. It is also a genuinely clever plant, since the reader later learns he is Maxwell. Protect this line almost verbatim:
  > "Look I know you are not telling me the truth, but honestly, I don't care. I have no interest in why you are sniffing around. However, I am interested in you." (ch 18)

  Sanchez blurting out "Benedikt" under pressure ch 18 is a credible, character-revealing mistake.
- **John's cracked line carries weight:**
  > "why don't you leave me alone. You don't know what you are looking for. It will ruin us." (ch 16)

  "Us" is ominous and does more than the paragraph around it.
- **The Ch8/Ch19 interlock is a smart piece of plotting.** Ch8 showed Stephen scanning the street from Sanchez's side. Ch19 shows the same moment from the watcher in the café:
  > "He saw a coffee shop at the street intersection. He bolted in, ordered a cappuccino and took the corner seat by the door. From where he was sitting he had a clear sight of all the four corners of the street." (ch 19)

  Readers enjoy seeing a scene from the other side. Keep it, and make it more obviously the same scene by echoing one detail from Ch8, such as Stephen putting his back to the wall.
- **Benedikt gets good small character beats:**
  - "Benedikt ignored the invite and opened the passenger side door instead." (ch 21). In one gesture it shows impatience and a boss who won't be chauffeured.
  - "I had my share of sleep, let us head straight to the airport." (ch 21)
  - "He had to leave the poker game in the middle, something he waited for the entire year." (ch 23). It humanises him and calls back to Ch2 neatly.
  - "Maybe she is a kleptomaniac and robbed me for fun." (ch 21). This is a wry self-deception that works if framed as wishful.
- **The Altenrhein rental desk has real texture:** "There was a person shouting from the back for parking the car at the main driving area, but they cared less to lend their ears to him." (ch 21). A real, slightly comic detail at the moment of failure.
- **The ticket queue has real tension:** "There was one passenger between him and Sanchez. The closest he came to her in years, a state of affair, which he accepted with great interest." (ch 22). Fix "in years" and "state of affair" and this is a strong, charged beat.
- **There are lighter touches.** The TAP "with arms wide open" exchange ch 22 and "When you are hungry even a simple egg muffin becomes the most sumptuous meal." (ch 22) give Maxwell some warmth.
- **The ticking clock is well built.** "His superiors had given him only the next three days … starting Monday he had to be in Tallinn … Wednesday was when project 'sub rosa' would commence" (ch 21). Clean, well placed stakes.
- **There is a dry aside worth keeping:** Evan "appeared to be in his early fifties, depending on when the photo was taken." (ch 17).
- **The structural idea is strong.** A cat-and-mouse with three parties is a good engine for Part III: Sanchez and Stephen running, Maxwell tailing, Benedikt chasing both. So is the reveal that the blackmailing manager and the lovelorn watcher are the same man.

## 7. Honest assessment

An agent who got this far would find a solid commercial premise and a chase with a good three-way design, delivered in prose that keeps tripping them. The wrong-word choices ("subornation", "captivate", "blazoned", "obnoxious", "innuendo") and the "blurted"/"grating voice" tags mark it as unedited on sight. Then come the continuity problems. Datelines run backwards in Part II, and Part III jumps a day back without explanation. The Zurich arrival is replayed with a different pursuit car. Ch10 has already shown the escape flight at a different time. Stockholm is placed in Denmark. The Part II backstory gives its space to furniture and one-line summaries while the dramatic core (planning the Stockholm heist with Bradley) happens off the page. The Part III chase is over-procedural, and Benedikt, meant as a formidable antagonist, spends a chapter in traffic and sends his security chief into the lavatory twice. What would keep a reader going is the Bradley turn in Ch18, the café counter-surveillance that mirrors Ch8, and Benedikt's growing and unwise obsession. There is a book here. At the moment it reads as a strong draft that was never line-edited or checked against its own calendar.

**The single most important fix is to rebuild the timeline so that one calendar holds across Parts I–III.** Pick the Lisbon date (17 April) as fixed. Move the Stockholm theft and Zurich arrival (Ch2–9, Ch19) so that the arrival is 16 April and the flight 17 April. Strip the duplicate flight out of Ch10. Redate Ch22 to 17 April 5:00 AM and put it before Ch21. Move Ch23 to about 10:00, or to Altenrhein. Fix the Ch16–18 dates to run forward. Most of the plot-logic problems in this section (four days to check CCTV, the impossible 9:00 AM jet, the pre-booked flight against the counter purchase, "Tuesday" against "weekend") are symptoms of that one calendar problem and go away once it is fixed. The prose pass comes after that.
