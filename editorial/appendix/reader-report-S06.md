# S06 — Developmental read, Chapters 41–49 (ch 41–49)

Section length: about 8,870 words. By chapter: 41: 507 · 42: 983 · 43: 1,445 · 44: 536 · 45: 742 · 46: 431 · 47: 1,035 · 48: 731 · 49: 2,456.

This section is where the book turns. Sanchez is shot and captured in Nazaré, flown to Tallinn, and the Benedikt–Sanchez captor/captive romance becomes the spine of the story. Maxwell, meanwhile, arrives everywhere about twenty minutes too late. The structural ideas are sound. The clock, the logistics and the repeated beats are not.

## 3. Continuity, logic and factual errors

### A. The evening clock of 18 April is broken. This is the most serious problem in the section.

Datelines as written: ch40 5:35 Nazaré → ch41 5:40 Nazaré → **ch42 6:30 Lisbon** → ch43 6:05 Nazaré → ch44 6:45 Lisbon → **ch45 6:30 Lisbon** → ch46 7:00 Lisbon → **ch47 7:45 "Lisbon" (airborne, "two hours into their journey")** → ch48 8:15 Lisbon → ch49 4:30 AM Tallinn.

1. **The Nazaré to Lisbon drive.** It is about 120 km, 80–90 minutes by the A8. In ch40, at about 5:45 PM, Marty says "We should be there in the next twenty minutes" (ch 40), and ch42 has them in Lisbon at 6:30 (ch 42). Neither is possible.
   **Fix:** have Marty say "an hour and a half", and set ch42 to about 7:15 PM.
2. **Maxwell's strand needs about 3.5 hours but gets less than 1.** He is in Nazaré at 6:05 (ch 43), with a few minutes of interrogation. Then:
   - Nazaré to Lisbon warehouse: at least 80 minutes, so about 7:40.
   - To the hospital: 20 minutes ch 43, so about 8:00, plus the search.
   - To Benedikt's house in Lisbon: about 8:30.
   - "Over an hour" to Montijo (ch 45): about 9:30+.

   Yet ch45 is headed **6:30 PM** at Benedikt's house ch 45. That is 25 minutes after he was standing in a field in Nazaré.
3. **The Benedikt strand contradicts the receptionist.** Benedikt reaches the clinic at 7:00 (ch 46), has 30 minutes of treatment (ch 46), then an ambulance and "less than twenty minutes" to the plane (ch 46), so wheels-up is about 8:00. Maxwell, arriving (by the chapter's own dateline) at about 7:30–7:40, is told they "left about twenty minutes back" (ch 45). That would be before Sanchez had even left the clinic.
4. **Ch47's dateline is 7:45 PM "Lisbon"** (ch 47), but the chapter is set mid-air, "two hours into their journey" (ch 47).
   **Fix:** date it by elapsed time, for example "11:00 PM 18th April 2014, en route to Tallinn", or leave the time out.
5. **Time zones and flight time.** Lisbon to Tallinn is about 3,400 km, roughly 4½ hours in a mid-size business jet. Portugal is UTC+1 in April and Estonia UTC+3. With a Lisbon take-off at about 8 PM, they would land at about 2:30 AM Tallinn time, not 4:30. The 4:30 AM landing (ch 49) only works if wheels-up is about 9:30–10 PM Lisbon time. That is one more reason to push the whole Lisbon evening later.
6. **Daylight.** Sunset in Lisbon on 18 April 2014 was about 20:20.
   - "The evening sun was fading fast" at 6:45 (ch 44) is wrong.
   - "The streetlights were appearing" at 6:30 (ch 45) is wrong.
   - "The darkness of the evening" at the warehouse at 6:30 (ch 42) is wrong.

   Retiming the evening fixes all three automatically.
7. **Recommended single clock.** Keep Nazaré as written (ch41 5:40, ch43 6:05) and change the rest:

   | Chapter | Suggested time | Event |
   |---|---|---|
   | ch42 | ~7:15 PM | Warehouse arrival |
   | ch44 | ~7:30 PM | Benedikt leaves the warehouse, about 15 minutes before Maxwell arrives (which makes the "still wet" blood true) |
   | ch46 | ~7:50 PM | Clinic |
   | — | ~9:00–9:30 PM | Wheels-up |
   | ch45 | ~9:30–9:45 PM | Maxwell at Montijo ("twenty minutes back") |
   | ch48 | ~10:00 PM | Phone call to Drake |
   | ch47 | late night, in the air | On the plane |
   | ch49 | 4:30 AM | Tallinn (now consistent) |

   Cutting Maxwell's hospital search or his house visit (see §4) makes this schedule comfortable.
8. **The inherited gap in Maxwell's timeline.** In ch34 ch 34, at about 2:30 PM, Maxwell was "less than 30 minutes" from Adriana's house. In ch41 he appears on the road at 5:40 PM. Three hours are unaccounted for.
   **Fix:** either account for them in S05, or have him delayed plausibly (for example, he followed the van toward Lisbon first).

### B. Geography and facts

9. **Montijo "airport"** [ch 45, ch 48]. Montijo is Air Base No. 6, a military base of the Portuguese Air Force. In 2014 it had no civilian private-jet terminal with a receptionist and revolving door. This was set up in ch27–28 [ch 27, ch 28], so it is a book-wide decision.
   **Fix:** switch to Cascais–Tires aerodrome, Lisbon's real business-aviation field, or to the private-aviation terminal at Lisbon Humberto Delgado. If Montijo is kept, give one line acknowledging a leased civil apron.
10. **"A few miles south of the Lisbon dockyard"** (ch 42). South of Lisbon's docks is the Tagus estuary.
    **Fix:** "across the river in Seixal" or "east along the docks toward Marvila".
11. **"Lisbon police"** [ch 43, ch 45] for a killing in Nazaré. Nazaré is policed by the GNR, and homicide goes to the Polícia Judiciária.
    **Fix:** "the local GNR post" or "the Polícia Judiciária".
12. **"Tallinn wasn't a significant business city that could demand overnight flights"** (ch 48). This is patronising about a national capital, and it is not the real reason. There was no direct Lisbon–Tallinn service; any commercial route meant a connection.
    **Fix:** "There is nothing direct to Tallinn. The best we can do is through Frankfurt at 6 AM, landing mid-afternoon."
13. **The travel desk undercuts its own urgency** (ch 48). Both the morning flight and the connecting flight "will still be in Tallinn only by tomorrow morning", which is about when a private jet would land. Drake's question, "How many hours you would save anyways?" (ch 48), is then fair and goes unanswered.
    **Fix:** have commercial options land mid-to-late afternoon on the 19th, so the jet saves about 10 hours.
14. **"It is a bruise"** (ch 46) for a bullet through the calf. The author probably means "a graze" or "a flesh wound". The healing time also changes:
    - "fine in a few weeks" (ch 46)
    - "back on her feet in a week" (ch 47)
    - walking on sticks the next morning (ch 49)
    - no visible limp by 26 April, ch71 (ch 71)

    **Fix:** "a through-and-through flesh wound, missed the bone. Sticks for a week or two."
15. **Forensics at the warehouse** (ch 43). The blood is "still warm with no clot formation". Drops of blood cool to room temperature within minutes and begin clotting in 5–10. Her wound was also already bound (ch 40).
    **Fix:** "The drops were still tacky, not yet dark. They could not be far ahead."
16. **A nursing home with an operating table** ch 46.
    **Fix:** "a private clinic".
17. **Freedom Square** ch 49. The Hotel Palace on Vabaduse väljak is accurate and good. But the "restaurants with open sitting areas, street food venues, and vendors selling souvenirs" (ch 49) describe the Old Town and Town Hall Square, a few minutes' walk up Harju Street. Freedom Square is a large, fairly bare plaza.
    **Fix:** have them walk up into the Old Town. "Turquoise colored multi haze occult" (ch 49) is garbled. The landmark is the War of Independence Victory Column, a tall glass-plated column bearing the Cross of Liberty.
18. **Kali** ch 49. Estonian kvass is correctly named, but it is fizzy and sweet-sour, not "smooth".
    **Fix:** "its sweet, malty fizz". Lower-case: *kali*.
19. **The dates.** 18 April 2014 was Good Friday, a public holiday in Portugal and the UK. Traffic, and Drake being "in office" late on a bank-holiday evening (ch 48), can pass. But a line acknowledging it ("even on Good Friday Drake was at his desk") would be cheap texture and would pre-empt a sharp reader.
20. **Scope note.** ch 49 correctly says John got a call "from Sweden". Seven datelines elsewhere in the book read "Stockholm, Denmark" (ch2–9, ch20). That error belongs to other sections, but this paragraph exposes it.

### C. Internal contradictions and logic

21. **What Stephen could have seen** (ch 43). "They were four of them, but when I fleeted, from the rear-view mirror I could only see three." When he fled, only Marty was there. The other two only arrived afterwards (ch 40), and Stephen knew all four had been knocked out (ch 39).
    **Fix:** "Only one came after us. The others were still out cold in the field. One of them may still be there."
22. **The officer's body** [ch 43]. Sanchez and Stephen crossed that backyard and searched the house in ch39 without seeing a dead policeman. Who killed him, and when, is never shown. Benedikt knows about him (ch 44) without the reader learning how.
    **Fix:** have Stephen and Sanchez hear the shots, or have Benedikt say "the man Adam's boys found watching the house".
23. **Van or car.** Maxwell planted the transmitter "underneath the glove box" of Benedikt's **van** (ch30 ch 30). In ch44 it is found "taped at the corner most position" inside the glove compartment of "the car" ch 44. Ch45–46 call it "the van" again [ch 45, ch 46].
    **Fix:** make it "the van" throughout, and have the bug under or behind the glove box, knocked loose.
24. **Francis's gun** [ch 44 vs ch 44]. He "had misplaced his gun somewhere" and fumbles for it, then says "this happened when I threw the gun in the compartment", which is exactly what ch35 (ch 35) showed him doing.
    **Fix:** "Francis reached into the glove box for the pistol he had tossed there at noon, and his fingers found something else."
25. **Two vehicles, one missing** ch 41. Maxwell was driving his own car, presumably the one that "went past him with great urgency" (ch 41), then appears on foot. After that he drives Adriana's van, and then "the car" [ch 43]. What happened to his own car? Later he tells Stephen "Take the car" (ch 48), but Maxwell has it.
    **Fix:** one line: Maxwell's car overshoots, he runs back, takes the van's wheel, and leaves his car on the verge; or Stephen follows in the van.
26. **The paddy-field crash** (ch 41). Stephen crashes, then single-handedly "dug the car out" of a field in moments while Adriana sits silent. This is either a scene or it should go.
    **Fix:** cut it. Have him skid onto the verge instead.
27. **Left or right** [ch 42 vs ch 43]. The conference room is "on the right" of the staircase, then "towards his left".
28. **"There were no windows or ceilings"** (ch 42) contradicts the "steel rods and pipe running throughout the roof" in the same paragraph.
    **Fix:** "no windows or skylights".
29. **"Soon all of them were back on the plane"** (ch 49). This should be *off*. As written, Damian gets off, talks, and everyone gets back on.
30. **The misattributed line** (ch 49). "The waiter appeared … greeting his guests, 'May I get a bottle of Kali?'" The waiter is ordering. It should be Benedikt's line.
31. **Benedikt's tracing logic is circular** ch 49.
    - "Knowing your name made it easy to trace your whereabouts. We already knew from the airport records that you were in Lisbon" makes the John Patterson phone trail redundant.
    - John "made a call to Lisbon", but Adriana lives in Nazaré (a Leiria-district number).
    - Ch35 (ch 35) credits the Nazaré tip to "Adam", who goes unmentioned here.

    **Fix:** compress to: "Your name gave me your TransPacific file. Your file gave me John Patterson. John's phone gave me a call from Stockholm, and ten minutes later his call to a woman in Nazaré. I put men on her house."
32. **Unmotivated actions.**
    - **Maxwell summons Stephen** (ch 48). A civilian, who has to be driven from Nazaré (about 1½ hours to Montijo) while the jet is ready in 30 minutes. No reason is given, and he apparently waits for him (they are together in Tallinn in ch52).
      **Fix:** give a reason: "She'll only trust you", or "I'm not leaving you here as their next hostage."
    - **Stephen never asks who Maxwell is** (ch 41), even though he knows him by his real name in ch53 (ch 53).
      **Fix:** one exchange in ch41 or ch43.
    - **Adriana is left alone** with a corpse, a ransacked house and a possible return of the goons [ch 43, ch 48]. After this she vanishes from the book.
    - **Benedikt books a traceable flight** after ordering "vacate all places" (ch 44). He files his real destination at a desk where a clerk can look him up by first name ch 45.
      **Fix:** have Maxwell see the flight plan on a board or press a ramp worker, or have Benedikt file for Helsinki and divert.
    - **The captive goes for a walk** (ch 49). Benedikt walks a captive who has escaped him twice through a public square with no guards mentioned.
      **Fix:** Damian's men trail twenty paces behind. This also adds tension.
33. **Maxwell's conduct has no consequences.** A Scotland Yard officer (ch53) with no Portuguese jurisdiction:
    - leaves a homicide scene;
    - shoots a restrained prisoner (ch 43);
    - dumps him at a hospital while flashing ID (ch 43);
    - tells the staff to call the cops and hold him until he returns, and never returns;
    - leaves the country.

    None of this has any consequence or even registers with him. William, Marty, the dead officer and the Lisbon police are never mentioned again in the book.
    **Fix:** at minimum, give Maxwell one beat of self-disgust or calculation, and let Drake raise the mess in ch48. That also answers "why is Drake furious".
34. **The kidnapped doctor** (ch 46) is still examining Sanchez in Tallinn [ch 49], then disappears. A forcibly abducted Portuguese doctor is a manhunt waiting to happen.
    **Fix:** make him Benedikt's paid physician (an echo of the doctor in ch6 (ch 6)), or pay him off on the tarmac.
35. **The receptionist** ch 45. She promises to call the other attendant and then asks for a date because she has "an off day tomorrow". The date thread is dropped.
36. **Sanchez's clothes** [ch 37, ch 49]. She was captured "in her sleepwear" and next day goes out to lunch. Add one line about clothes being delivered to the suite.
37. **The noon scene** (ch 49). Sanchez is "reposing right on the breakfast table", asleep two hours after breakfast.
    **Fix:** in the window armchair.
38. **Sanchez's memory** (ch 47). "Her memory from the past evening" refers to what is still the same evening.

## 4. Pacing and flow

- **Too many short chapters.** Nine chapters in 8,900 words, several under 550 words (ch41, 44, 46), and each opens with a dateline that fights the one before it. Suggested restructure into six chapters, with the new clock above:
  1. **ch41 + ch43 merged.** Stephen and Maxwell: road, house, body, William, warehouse. Cut the hospital search, and drop William at a hospital door in one sentence.
  2. **ch42.** Warehouse arrival, trimmed. The lair description ch 42 could lose about 40%.
  3. **ch44 + ch46 merged.** Bug discovery, clinic, conscripted doctor, take-off. Benedikt's yearning is stated once, not three times.
  4. **ch45 + ch48 merged.** Montijo, Tallinn, the Drake call, the jet. Cut the house break-in ch 45; Maxwell has no reason to think Benedikt would return to a burned safe house. Cut or shrink the flirtation ch 45.
  5. **ch47.** On the plane: Sanchez's decision. Keep, trimmed.
  6. **ch49.** Tallinn.
- **The same dead end, three times.** Maxwell arrives, finds it empty, and despairs at the warehouse ch 43, the hospital ch 43 and the house (ch 45). The receptionist makes a fourth ch 45. Each repeat weakens the next. One near-miss (the warehouse, with the blood still wet) plus the airport is enough.
- **Benedikt's lovesickness is restated in five chapters** [ch 42, ch 44, ch 46, ch 47, ch 49, ch 49]. It is the same register every time ("thousand emotions", "man of thousand shades", "many shades of hues", "euphoric", "exhilaration"). State it vividly once in ch42, show it through action (the gun he nearly uses on Marty, conscripting the doctor, the soup) and cut the interior repeats by at least half.
- **Info-dumps.**
  - Benedikt's tracing account ch 49 runs about 330 words of procedure in dialogue. Cut it to about 80 (see §3 item 31).
  - The Drake relationship (ch 48) is told, not shown. It would be better as a single sharp exchange.
- **Rushed.**
  - Sanchez's move from calculated charade (ch 47) to guilt (ch 49) to "dangerously attracted … getting increasingly addictive" (ch 49) happens within about 16 story hours and 2,000 words. The arc is the book's best idea, and it needs friction: one moment where she catches herself and distrusts the pull (she is a captive, and the book opened in Stockholm, which offers an irony she could name).
  - The paddy-field crash (ch 41) is a whole mini-action scene in one sentence.
- **The end-of-section hook leads into a retread.** Ch49 ends with "So tell me your story" and a flashback cue (ch 49). Ch50 then re-narrates the father's disappearance and the notebook map, which the reader already had in ch1 and ch11–18. The retelling has value as intimacy (her first time telling anyone, (ch 50)), but it should be summarised. Only what Benedikt's reactions reveal should be dramatised. (For the S07 editor.)
- **Scene order.** Ch43 runs its Nazaré scene from 6:05, while ch42 has already jumped to Lisbon at 6:30. The back-and-forth itself is fine, but the datelines advertise the contradiction. Once the clock is fixed, the cross-cutting between Maxwell and Benedikt, with Maxwell always about 20 minutes behind, can be a real engine. Make each Benedikt chapter end with a departure and each Maxwell chapter begin with an arrival.

## 5. Prose problems (section-specific)

**1. Wrong-word choices (malapropisms).** These are the densest and most damaging tic in this section.
- "his mind was a confetto" (ch 41). A confetto is a single sugared almond; the author probably means "a whirl" or "in pieces".
- "Adriana demonstrated no penitence" (ch 41). Penitence means remorse; the sense is "no reaction".
- "his deplorable being took a halt" (ch 41)
- "He had no curiousness, no threat, and demonstrated no impediment to find about the man" (ch 41)
- "a good amount of gore was lost" (ch 42). Should be "blood".
- "hoped to express through his libretto how much he was yearning for her" (ch 42)
- "with a rue of sarcasm in his tone" (ch 43)
- "He appeared absolvent" (ch 43)
- "kept looking at her with defiant eyes" (ch 47)
- "she decided to disdain her state" (ch 47)
- "his heart was drifting away to a state of alacrity" (ch 46)
- "John didn't buzz at all" (ch 49). Should be "budge"; also "Sanchez didn't buzz" at (ch 40).
- "announced in an equivocal note, 'I am ready to explore'" (ch 49). She is being unequivocal.
- "This was in his comfort alley" (ch 49)
- "Maxwell was relieved to expunge out of the situation" (ch 48), and "his trepidations were expunging" (ch 44)
- "the turquoise colored multi haze occult" (ch 49)

**2. Dialogue tags that fight the line.**
- "with a gravelly voice demanded, 'Sorry miss I don't have it with me'" (ch 45). An apology cannot be "demanded".
- "with a harmonious tone queried" (ch 45)
- "stated in an obligatory tone" (ch 42)
- "demanded with a frustrating tone" (ch 43)
- "with a daemon outcry he clamored" (ch 43)

Tag counts in the section: stated ×8, blurted ×7, announced ×7, enquired/queried ×8, demanded ×7. Default to "said", or to no tag.

**3. Abstract emotional stacking in place of a concrete image.**
- "He was a man of thousand shades, oscillating between hues of love and shadows of pain." (ch 44)
- "many shades of hues hidden deep within his blue eyes" (ch 42)
- "All his visible annoyance, unwanted trepidations, and pulsating vexations seemed to have faded away" (ch 47)
- "His heart was pulsating with such vigor that he could hear it oscillating between his chest and the back of his spine." (ch 42)

**4. Head-hopping within scenes.**
- (ch 41) "He couldn't believe Stephen left her": the stranger's thought, in Stephen's chapter.
- (ch 43) William's reasoning inside Maxwell's interrogation.
- [ch 45] the receptionist's mind.
- [ch 48] the office lady's mind.
- [ch 48] Drake's mind.

Pick one POV per scene and render the others through what can be seen.

## 6. Strengths (protect these)

- **The core dramatic engine.** A villain who is disarmingly, boyishly in love, and a heroine who decides, lying wounded on his plane, to use that love as her escape route, then finds the pretence leaking into feeling. This is the book's most distinctive and marketable idea. (ch 47) is the section's key turn: *"One thing was evident - Benedikt had a soft corner for her … More she weighed on the thought, more she was convinced about Benedikt being her key to escape."*
- **Dialogue with real character in it.** *"We are going to Tallinn. I guess you stole the map to be there. So, in a way, I am helping you." He didn't mean to portray it like sarcasm, but it came out like one.* (ch 47). This is witty, in character, and the narrative note is self-aware.
- **Sanchez's sharp test of him.** *"If you truly mean your words why don't let me go? I promise you, I will not keep anything against you."* (ch 49). It turns his declaration into a bargaining chip.
- **Banter that feels real.** *"You don't have patience. Do you?"* (ch 49) and *"I am looking for Estonian eat out place but looks like this place is influenced by tourists."* (ch 49), answered by Benedikt's eager hole-in-the-wall recommendation (ch 49). The picture of him "like a teenage boy who was excited to show some hidden treasure" (ch 49) is the best characterisation of Benedikt in the section.
- **Economical menace in the interrogation.** *"I am asking for one last time. The next bullet will hit the hand that is holding your leg."* (ch 43). This is chilling and precise, and a reminder that Maxwell has an edge.
- **A good comic beat.** *"Francis was still amazed by his accidental discovery. He finally got something right even if it was by accident."* (ch 44). The bug is destroyed by a tossed pistol, which pays off ch35 (ch 35) neatly.
- **The doctor as a mirror of Sanchez.** *"He had no answer for her. He himself was in captivation and unsure where he was headed."* (ch 47)
- **A clean read of the villain's anomaly.** *"She had eluded him for the past several days and in all seriousness, she should be a corpse by now. But Instead of questioning her, he was concerned."* (ch 47)
- **Atmosphere at the Tallinn landing.** *"Benedikt tucked his hand into his trousers pocket and paced down the flight stairs, unsure what was causing him to shiver more, the weather or the uncanny girl."* (ch 49)
- **Pulp pleasure in the lair.** A derelict warehouse with a keypad hidden in the vines and a floor tile that slides back over a spiral stair to a lit, carpeted bunker ch 42. Readers enjoy it. Tighten it, don't cut it.
- **Maxwell's procedural instinct.** He touches the blood to judge how far behind he is (ch 43). The idea is right even though the forensics need adjusting.
- **Well-chosen real detail.** The Hotel Palace on Freedom Square, *kali* at lunch, the pre-dawn cold. The Tallinn research is mostly accurate and lends authority.
- **A structure that works in principle.** The cross-cut pursuit, with Maxwell always twenty minutes behind, is a proven thriller structure. It needs a working clock, not a new idea.

## 7. Honest assessment

This is the hinge of the novel, and the author has built the right hinge. Sanchez's capture, the move to Tallinn, and the reversal in which the captive decides to play her captor's heart all set up Part V. The Tallinn half of ch49, for all its overwriting, shows the book at its most distinctive: a charming, dangerous man trying to impress a woman he is holding prisoner, and a heroine who is both manipulating him and slipping. An agent will respond to that.

The Portugal half of the section will lose the same agent first. The datelines contradict each other and the physical world:
- a 120 km drive done in 20–45 minutes;
- Maxwell in Nazaré and Lisbon 25 minutes apart;
- sunset two hours early;
- a mid-flight chapter dated before take-off.

Maxwell runs into the same empty room three times. Benedikt's yearning is re-announced in nearly every chapter in near-identical abstract language. A string of loose ends is dropped for good: a murdered police officer, a tortured and abandoned William, a kidnapped doctor, a stranded Adriana, a receptionist's date. The wrong-word problem is at its worst here (the "confetto", "penitence", "libretto", "absolvent", "multi haze occult" cluster). It pulls readers out of the scene at exactly the moments that should grip them.

**The single most important fix:** rebuild the evening of 18 April on one realistic clock and restructure ch41–48 around it into about five chapters. Use real drive times (Nazaré to Lisbon 80–90 minutes), sunset at about 20:20, a 2-hour time-zone shift and a 4½-hour flight to Tallinn. Cut Maxwell's hospital and house dead ends, keep one near-miss at the warehouse, and alternate each Benedikt departure with a Maxwell arrival. That one change fixes most of the section's continuity errors, removes the repeated beats, and turns a sequence that reads as confused into a tense chase.
