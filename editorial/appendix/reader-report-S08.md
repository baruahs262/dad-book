# S08 — Developmental read, Chapters 57–70 (ch 57–70)

~8,560 words, 14 chapters. Chapter lengths (words): 57: 333 · 58: 677 · 59: 1,003 · 60: 643 · 61: 717 · 62: 580 · 63: 949 · 64: 238 · 65: 318 · 66: 726 · 67: 755 · 68: 361 · 69: 1,025 · 70: 233.

Where this section sits in the arc: Sanchez is aboard the TransPacific ship as Benedikt's assistant. She is gathering evidence and looking for her father, Daniel Roberto. In Ch55 Benedikt blanched at the father's photograph. This section covers the last day in Tallinn (the Russian delegates' meeting), the voyage to Ventspils with the medevac set piece, the stay in Ventspils (Finch's lead to Albert Avilov in Viciebsk, and Sanchez admitting she is in love), and Maxwell and Stephen moving ahead to Gdynia. The itinerary (Tallinn, then Ventspils, then Gdynia by sea, Warsaw by road, Viciebsk) comes from Jules's map (book.md l.1243–1247).

## 3. Continuity, logic and factual errors

### A. Timeline and dateline contradictions
1. **The Ventspils–Gdynia passage is impossible as dated.** Ch69 is headed 25 April, and its evening stroll is "the day before their scheduled departure to Gdynia" (ch 69), which puts departure on **26 April**. Ch70 then has the ship "about to arrive" at Gdynia at **11:00 AM on 26 April** [ch 70]. Ventspils to Gdynia is roughly 200+ nautical miles, about 15–20 hours for a cargo ship. *Fix:* in ch 69 write "It was their last evening in Ventspils; the ship would sail at first light." Leave Ch69 on the 25th and have the ship leave in the early hours of the 25th→26th, or push Ch70 to the 27th. The simplest fix is to put the stroll on the evening of the **24th** and sail on the 25th.
2. **Ch69's dateline says "11:00 AM" but the scene is "a gorgeous evening"** [ch 69]. The chapter is a summary running from the 24th to the evening of the 25th. *Fix:* date it to the evening ("7:00 PM 24th/25th April") and move the summary material into Ch68's tail or a short transition.
3. **Arrival time in Ventspils.** At about 10–11 AM Finch says "Latvia was still six hours away" (ch 63), which means arrival around 4–5 PM. But Ch66 has the captain announce docking "in an hour" at 8 PM ch 66, and at 11:30 AM Benedikt says "few hours to Latvia" (ch 65). Tallinn to Ventspils, around Hiiumaa/Saaremaa, is about 190–200 nm, about 14–16 hours from a 4–5 AM sailing. So 8–9 PM is right and **"six hours" is wrong.** *Fix:* "Ventspils was still ten hours away."
4. **Datelines for scenes at sea.** Ch63, 65 and 66 are headed "Ventspils, Latvia", but the ship is in open sea. *Fix:* "At sea, en route to Ventspils" (or "Baltic Sea").
5. **"It had been three days since she boarded the ship"** (ch 64). She boarded before dawn on 21 April (Ch54). At 10 AM on the 23rd that is two days. *Fix:* "two days".
6. **Ch62 ends with "decided to retire for the night"** (ch 62). The ship sailed at 4 AM and "it was still dark" (ch 62), so this is dawn. *Fix:* "she crawled back into bed to steal a few hours' sleep."
7. **Ch67's time logic.** It opens with her drifting off, then the nightmare, then waking at "8:00 AM" in a "pitch dark" room [ch 67]. Sunrise in Ventspils on 24 April is about 06:00. *Fix:* wake her at 5 AM, or give the cabin heavy blackout curtains ("the blackout blind had kept out the morning"). Better still, date the chapter's first paragraph to the night before.
8. **"Undocking will take another few hours"** (ch 67). The ship docked the night before and stays two more days. The word means departure. *Fix:* "unloading".
9. **Recorder duration.** The pen "could record for about a couple of hours" (ch 60). It was switched on at about 9:50 and retrieved after the delegates left at 1:15 plus an hour ch 60. The one-hour meeting would still be captured, but add "the battery had died long before she retrieved it" or leave out the capacity.

### B. Gadget continuity (clashes with Ch56)
10. In Ch56 (l.2297) Maxwell's kit is **earrings** (left one a camera, right one a video recorder), a **voice recorder pen**, a knife, memory cards and a transmitter. In this section:
    - The pen is "a stealth video recorder" (ch 58), then produces only "the audio recording" (ch 62). *Fix:* "voice recorder pen" in ch 58.
    - "She took the camera out of her purse … small enough to hide in her hand's fist" (ch 59). Her camera is an earring she is wearing. *Fix:* either add a separate miniature camera to the Ch56 kit list, or have her unclip the earring camera to photograph the pages. That makes the drop more dangerous, since an earring on the floor is harder to explain.
    - A **satellite phone** appears from nowhere (ch 66). *Fix:* add it to the Ch56 kit or to her luggage.
11. **Which computer?** In Ch55 Benedikt "pushed the laptop towards her", a company laptop. In ch 62 she "turned her computer on and carefully downloaded all the images, videos". In ch 66 she uploads from "the laptop" and deletes the files from the camera, but not from the laptop. If it's TransPacific's laptop, this is suicidal. *Fix:* make it "her own laptop" and have her wipe it too, or skip the laptop and upload straight from the device.
12. **Satellite phone indoors.** Sat phones need a clear view of the sky, and she has latched the door and "guarded" the balcony (ch 66). *Fix:* have her step onto the balcony and crouch behind the rail while the upload crawls. That is both accurate and more tense.

### C. Plot holes and unmotivated actions
13. **Benedikt saw the camera fly out** (ch 59): "Benedikt saw the device flying out." This is never followed up, even though Benedikt is suspicious enough to have had her guarded. *Fix, two options:* (a) cut "Benedikt saw the device flying out" so the phone takes all his attention; or (b) pay it off later. For example, in Ch69 or at the Ch85 confession he reveals he noticed it and said nothing, which is a strong romantic and moral beat. Option (b) is better.
14. **The secret file is handed to her twice.** Benedikt is "extremely secretive about the meeting" and she has "no access" (ch 58). An hour later he gives her the file to tally (ch 59) and then to carry to the conference room (ch 60). *Fix:* one line of motive. Either the accountant is ashore, or he is rattled and short-handed (his distraction is set up in ch 59), or he is deliberately testing her.
15. **What is in the file? What is on the recording?** The file is "irrefutably apposite" (ch 59) and the audio covers "loads, money and some future deals" (ch 62). Neither is ever specified. The reader needs one concrete, damning detail (a manifest line, a code word, a sum). Later, Ch71 treats "items of archeological importance" (l.2756) as a new, "disturbing" discovery, which the file would surely already have shown. *Fix:* in ch 59 name one detail ("the cargo manifest listed 'machine parts'; the payment schedule beside it ran to eleven million euros"), and in ch 62 plant one line about a "guest in Viciebsk" or "the Roberto matter" that pays off later.
16. **Benedikt's POV is a cheat.** In Benedikt's own close third he "failed to get any information about her father … Daniel disappearance was an extreme hushed affair" (ch 69). He "tried making few calls … towards tracing Sanchez's father" (ch 59). Later he confesses "I told the Russians to get rid of him" (l.2958). A viewpoint character can't be shown ignorant of what he knows. ch 61 ("What he mentioned was partly true") and ch 63 ("the fear of Sanchez coming to know what he knew") are honest; ch 69 is not. *Fix:* rewrite ch 69 so he is looking for **where Daniel is now**, which is true, while the narration stays guarded about his part in the capture ("He knew how Daniel had been taken. He did not know where they had taken him.").
17. **The name slip** (ch 69). This is a great beat, because Sanchez never said "Daniel" to him on the page. But Benedikt doesn't react at all, and it isn't mentioned again. *Fix:* give him a visible reaction (a pause, "You told me," "I did not") and let Sanchez file it away. It is the section's best piece of detective work, so don't waste it.
18. **Finch is a "head stevedore" who sails with the ship** (ch 62). Stevedores are shore-side dock labour hired at each port. They don't sail. *Fix:* make Finch the **bosun** or **cargo foreman / chief cargo officer**. That also fits "He used to work on this ship" (ch 68).
19. **Finch's location contradicts a later chapter.** Here he is crew on the ship and says Daniel "used to work on this ship" (ch 68). In Ch72 (l.2858) Sanchez tells Albert's contact the message is "from his friend in Gdynia, where he used to work before". *Fix:* change l.2858 to "from his old shipmate Finch".
20. **Daniel "used to work on this ship"** (ch 68) needs to match Daniel's backstory elsewhere (the Ch1 family man, and the "evidence" he collected, l.2898). If Daniel was undercover as a crewman, say so. It is a strong mirror of Sanchez's own infiltration and worth one line from Finch.
21. **The medevac logic.** The doctor asks to "turn the ship back to the port" (ch 63). At 10 AM the ship is 5–6 hours out of Tallinn, so turning back is no quicker than going on. Any master would also call MRCC for a helicopter medevac as routine; the Estonian Border Guard and Latvian Navy both fly SAR helicopters. The dramatic question should be why a smuggling ship wouldn't want a state helicopter landing on it. *Fix:* the doctor asks for "a medevac", the captain refuses because a Border Guard helicopter means officials on deck, and Benedikt's choice to call one anyway becomes a real risk he takes for her. That deepens the scene and his arc.
22. **"Can we turn the ship back to the port? The most I can hold him is a couple of hours"** (ch 63), and later "Can he survive until evening?" (ch 63). The doctor's prognosis moves from "lose his leg" to "the worker will die" (ch 63). *Fix:* give the doctor one line of escalation (for example, "he's bleeding internally").
23. **Maxwell and Stephen "in Latvia"** (ch 64). They were last in Tallinn (Ch52–53), and their move to Latvia is never shown. Their plan is also odd: they abandon Latvia the morning the ship arrives there for a two-day stop. *Fix:* in ch 64, "They had crossed to Ventspils two days ago", and give Maxwell a reason to leap ahead, such as a tip from Drake about a transfer in Gdynia.
24. **Maxwell's Gdynia vigil pays off nothing** ch 70. Ch71 opens with Sanchez's summary of Gdynia, and in Warsaw the two of them lose her anyway (l.2764). *Fix:* either dramatise one short Maxwell surveillance beat in Ch71 (he sees her on deck, or sees the crates), or cut Ch70 and fold its one idea (Maxwell's jealousy) into Ch71.
25. **"What kind of business deal she could have with him"** (ch 70). Maxwell was told the arrangement in Ch53. It reads as though he never heard it. *Fix:* "He still could not fathom what Benedikt gained from their bargain."
26. **Benedikt openly admits to crime** (ch 61) ("We are in an illicit trade"). This is plausible only if Sanchez's knowledge was already established between them. Check Ch53–55. If he has never said it before, make it a deliberate, costly confession.
27. **Security on the ship.** Guards at the conference room scan her (ch 58), yet she walks into the cargo area at 4 AM on her word alone ch 62. It's fine if the guards know her by sight, which ch 62 says, but "Two mariners laced with sophisticated weapons" openly armed in an EU port invites harbour police. *Fix:* "two crewmen whose jackets did not quite hide their holsters".
28. **Nobody asks about her screaming nightmare** (ch 67). That's fine. But the balcony, elevators, 20-seat conference hall, chief steward, doctor, executive suites and delegates arriving at the gangway in limousines [ch 58, ch 60 make this a cruise liner, not a cargo vessel. *Fix, outside this section:* establish in Ch54 that it is a converted ro-pax or passenger-cargo vessel with an owner's deck.

### D. Geography and factual details
29. **The Tallinn restaurant** ch 61 is "an authentic Lebanese restaurant" designed "like an old castle with statues demonstrating sword fighting and Jousting". The mix is incongruous. Tallinn's famous medieval-themed dining is Hanseatic, not Lebanese. *Fix:* choose one, either a medieval-themed Old Town restaurant or an elegant Lebanese one in Kadriorg. "Lamborghinis and Maserati's" on pebbled Old Town streets is overdone: much of the Old Town is pedestrianised and cobbled. "18th-century streetlights" is an anachronism, since street lighting was gas and from the 19th century. *Fix:* "old-fashioned lanterns".
30. **"The Baltica Concert"** (ch 69). "Baltica" is an international folklore festival held in summer (usually July) and rotating between the Baltic states. It wasn't in Ventspils in April 2014. Ventspils's big concert hall ("Latvija") opened only in 2019. *Fix:* "a concert at the town's theatre house" or "an evening of organ music at St Nicholas' Church" (Ventspils's Lutheran church). Keep it generic.
31. **"The vessel flagged off from Tallinn"** (ch 62). "Flagged off" is Indian-English usage for starting a race or event. *Fix:* "cast off" / "sailed from".
32. **"I need to clear their primage"** (ch 60). Primage is an archaic surcharge paid to a ship's master, not a stevedores' fee. *Fix:* "clear the stevedores' invoices".
33. **"rushing towards the abaft"** (ch 63). "Abaft" is a preposition. *Fix:* "towards the stern" / "aft".
34. **"lay unconscious on a wooden shaft … the open shaft"** (ch 63). Unclear. *Fix:* "on the steel deck beside the open hatch".
35. **"slipped inside a hidden rail by the end of the raft"** (ch 68). There is no raft, and a "hidden rail" is meaningless here. *Fix:* "ducked behind a stack of containers near the stern rail".
36. **"conference room was a flight up the deck … turned around the curb"** (ch 60). *Fix:* "one deck up … turned the corner".
37. **Viciebsk.** It is on the Western Dvina (Daugava) and near the Russian border (l.1247). It is correct that it's the final stop, reached by land or air from Warsaw. The Belarusian form "Viciebsk" is legitimate, but most English readers know "Vitebsk". Decide and stay consistent.
38. **Maxwell's drive from Latvia to Gdynia** (ch 64). The Russian exclave of Kaliningrad sits in the way, so the route runs through Lithuania and the Suwałki gap: about 800 km and 10–11 hours. It is plausible within the timeline. A single line ("a long day's drive skirting Kaliningrad") would show the author knows the map.
39. **"He has lost a lot of gore"** (ch 63). *Fix:* "blood".

## 4. Pacing and flow

**Structure.** The section has three movements:
- **Tallinn espionage (Ch57–61, ~3,370 words).** This is the most tense part of the section. It contains the book's first genuine spy set piece (plant the pen, near-discovery, the file, the dropped camera, retrieval).
- **The voyage (Ch62–66, ~2,810 words).** The medevac is a strong set piece. It is surrounded by thin connective chapters.
- **Ventspils (Ch67–69) plus the Maxwell cutaways (Ch64, Ch70).** The richest material, the Finch reveal and the name slip, lands here. So does the most summary-by-narration and inner brooding.

**Where it drags:**
- **Sleeplessness and brooding recur about seven times.** Ch57 ch 57, Ch61 end (ch 61), Ch62 open (ch 62), Ch65 end (ch 65), Ch66 (ch 66), Ch67 open (ch 67), and Ch69 open (ch 69). Nearly every chapter opens or closes with her tossing, turning, splashing water on her face or "brooding" (7 uses). Cut at least half of them.
- **Benedikt's self-reproach also repeats.** Ch59 (ch 59), Ch63 (ch 63), Ch66 [ch 66] and Ch69 (ch 69) are four paragraphs of the same argument ("he wanted to help, feared the higher-ups, kept failing"). One strong version, ideally Ch63's, is enough.
- **The father confrontation repeats three times with little escalation.** Ch61 dinner, Ch66 ("By everyone you meant you") and Ch69 (the stroll) each end with "he's hiding something". Only Ch69 adds new information (the name slip). *Suggestion:* cut the Ch66 argument down to the sarcastic exchange and her tears, and let Ch69 carry the weight.

**Where it rushes:**
- **The warehouse visits** (Tallinn, ch 61, one sentence; Ventspils, ch 69, one sentence). The plot says she is looking for anyone who knew her father, but she never questions a single worker on the page. Finch's reveal would land harder if she had first tried and failed with someone else in Ventspils.
- **The Ventspils stay** (Ch69, ch 69). Two days of Market Square, restaurant, concert and warehouse "evidence" go by in three paragraphs. That is fine as a montage, but the "valuable evidence" line is an unearned hand-wave.
- **The love realisation** (Ch67, ch 67). She goes from "why am I bothered" to "I am in love!" in two paragraphs, straight after a nightmare in which he is menacing. The nightmare is a good idea: love shadowed by dread. The narration explains it away with "I have to stop overthinking" (ch 67). Let the image linger.

**Scene order and merges:**
- **Ch57 (333 words)** is only a transition. Merge it into Ch56's tail or into the top of Ch58.
- **Ch58 + Ch59 + Ch60 → one or two chapters.** They form one continuous morning. Two near-discovery beats ("Benedikt's voice startled her", pale face, sweat, restroom) arrive back to back in Ch58 and Ch59. Keep the dropped-camera sequence, which is the better scene, and soften the first one. Ch58's hook (recorder not switched on) is excellent, so keep it as a chapter break.
- **Ch65 (318 words)** is one exchange. Fold it into the end of Ch63: after the helicopter she finds him and thanks him, and his "you are changing me" lands while the scene is still hot.
- **Ch64 (238) and Ch70 (233)**, the Maxwell cutaways, add almost nothing, and Ch70's hook is not paid off. Merge them into a single Maxwell chapter placed just before Gdynia. Give it one piece of new information (a tip from Drake, or the port contact revealing an unexpected cargo), or cut them and move Maxwell's jealousy into Ch71.
- **Ch68 (361)** is short but perfect in function. Keep it as a sharp standalone chapter. Moving the Ch69 montage before it would let the Finch reveal close the Ventspils sequence on a high: the Ventspils days pass, then Finch, then the Benedikt stroll. Alternatively keep the order but date it correctly.

**Info-dumps:** none major. The long Benedikt interior paragraphs (ch 63), (ch 69) are the nearest thing, and each could lose 40%.

## 5. Prose problems in this section

**Recurring issues:**

1. **Near-miss vocabulary (malapropism).** This is the biggest texture problem, with several per chapter.
   - "He passed a gullible smile." (ch 58). Also "she didn't want to show her gullible side" (ch 66). The word needed is *sheepish* or *vulnerable*.
   - "couldn't conceal the slunk smile on his face" (ch 58). Probably *sly*.
   - "she was flirting in the danger zone of getting caught with her miscreant" (ch 59). The word needed is *misdeed*. Also "an illusion created by his miscreant heart" (ch 66).
   - "She took a few deep breaths to calm her stature." (ch 59). The word needed is *nerves*.
   - "He has lost a lot of gore." (ch 63)
   - "she tried reasoning but couldn't attribute to any convulsive answer" (ch 67). The word needed is *conclusive*.
   - "the most genuine words he could ravel" (ch 68). The word needed is *muster*.
   - "the words came out invariably" (ch 61). The word needed is *involuntarily*.
   - "Despite all the recent proceedings a surreptitious smile encountered him." (ch 66)
   - "made him delirious" (ch 59), meaning *furious* or *distraught*.
   - "his inability to accost her" (ch 63), meaning *approach*.
   - "She was at her wits to find a way out" (ch 58), meaning *at her wits' end*.
2. **Said-bookisms and "blurted".** "blurted" appears 8 times, plus "announced" (7) and "stated" (5), often for calm speech: "he stated with a deep voice" (ch 60); "Stephen … blurted hysterically" (ch 57); "Benedikt blurted in his most unconventional way" (ch 69). Default to *said* and *asked*.
3. **Stock physiological reactions.** "Her face turned pale" (ch 58), "His face turned pale" (ch 66), "Your face turned pale" (ch 61). "drenched in sweat" (ch 58), "Perspiration covered her from head to toe" (ch 59), "perspiring profusely from head to toe" (ch 67). "failed miserably" ×3 [ch 57, ch 59, ch 67].
4. **Misused "saunter".** It means a leisurely walk but is used for anxious or menacing movement: "As she was sauntering her way back" right after panic (ch 58); "ghost-like creatures sauntering towards her with unprecedented angst" (ch 67).
5. **Dropped possessive "s" and article errors.** "Sanchez agitation" (ch 57), "Stephen anxiety" (ch 57), "Sanchez mind" (ch 60), "Benedikt ego" (ch 66), "the guard voice" (ch 60), "Daniel disappearance" (ch 69), "the previous day episode" (ch 58), "He was tad impatient" (ch 59), "I need few volunteers" (ch 63).
6. **Typos and punctuation.** "We would be sailing \`soon" (stray backtick) (ch 60). "his tone was overtly casual?" (ch 60). "Please leave?" (ch 62). "Am I am avoiding the truth?" (ch 67). "She was struggled hard" (ch 69). "place in on the table" (ch 59). "Maserati's" (ch 61). "chauffeured driven" (ch 60). Dialogue and narration also run together in single paragraphs (ch 59), (ch 59), (ch 59), (ch 59), (ch 59).
7. **Indian-English idiom that will read as errors to UK/US agents.** "The vessel flagged off from Tallinn" (ch 62); "Maxwell had an old connect" (ch 70); "It was over twenty minutes she was into it" (ch 59); "she naively accepted to come along" (ch 69); "I will not insist you to leave" (ch 69).
8. **Overwrought similes.** "The dark clouds were looming over the rough sea made the port city look exquisite, like some artwork from Guggenheim." (ch 68) (also ungrammatical). "like a hawk stooping towards its prey she leaped towards the camera" (ch 59). "she was like a boat without an anchor, drifting to oblivion" (ch 61).
9. **Head-hopping inside scenes, with no breaks:** Ch59 [ch 59→ch 59], Ch61 [ch 61, ch 61], Ch63 [ch 63→ch 63], Ch66 [ch 66→ch 66], Ch69 ch 69. Add scene breaks (the manuscript's "**\**" dividers) at each POV switch, or keep each chapter to one POV.

## 6. Strengths

- **The Ch58 chapter-end twist:** "In the entire hullabaloo, she forgot to turn the recorder on." (ch 58). It is simple, human and funny, and it turns a near-miss into a second problem to solve. Protect it.
- **The dropped-camera sequence** ch 59 is the section's best set piece. A man on the phone, a juggled handset, a camera sliding through a stack of paper: it is physical, visual comedy-suspense in a Hitchcock register. It needs tightening and one fix (ch 59 "Benedikt saw the device"), but the idea is strong.
- **The "lost notebook" retrieval** ch 60. It is economical, and the bored post-meeting guards are a nice realistic touch.
- **The medevac:** "Don't thank me. Thank the lady. She made this happen." (ch 63). It is Benedikt's best line so far: gallant, proud, and deflecting. With the MRCC/Border Guard fix (§3.21) this becomes a turning point for his character.
- **Finch's arc across three chapters:** hostility ch 62, shame (ch 63), then the gift of the letter ch 68. It is cleanly motivated by cause and effect: her compassion for the worker earns the lead. This is the best plotting in the section. "You are a good person. Take care of yourself." (ch 68) works because it is plain.
- **The name slip:** "You have told me before, you don't know my dad then how do you know his name?" (ch 69). This is real detective logic grounded in dialogue. Sharpen and protect it (§3.17).
- **The nightmare image** ch 67: "out of the shallow darkness, she could see the hazy shadow of Benedikt … Benedikt's hand was slowly extending towards her." Love and menace in one gesture. It is an honest foreshadowing of his confession (l.2958).
- **The mirror scene:** "You are here for a mission. You don't have time to fall in love. Find your dad and get the hell out of this place." (ch 67). It is direct and voiced, and her own register comes through.
- **Benedikt's sarcasm** in Ch66, "I hope you are intelligent enough to know that if by any stroke of luck your father is still alive, any snooping around can put his life in deep danger." (ch 66), gives him an edge that the soft-focus romance otherwise sands off.
- **Ch68 as a whole** is a tight, well-shaped chapter: the atmosphere, the ambush of a voice, the secret meeting aft, the letter, the countdown ("another four days … excruciatingly long" (ch 68)).
- **Planting Benedikt's guilt:** "What he mentioned was partly true" (ch 61) and "the fear of Sanchez coming to know what he knew about her father" (ch 63) set up the later confession fairly.

## 7. Honest assessment

This section contains some of the book's best plotting ideas. There is a proper espionage morning with a double near-miss. There is a moral set piece at sea that pays off in cause and effect (her compassion earns Finch's trust, and Finch hands her the key to Viciebsk). There is a clean detective beat in the name slip. What hides these good bones is the connective material: seven rounds of sleepless brooding, four near-identical Benedikt self-reproach paragraphs, three circular confrontations about the father, and two Maxwell chapters that promise action and deliver none. Nearly half the word count is interior restatement. The prose problems are real but fixable line by line: malapropisms ("gullible smile", "lost a lot of gore", "convulsive answer"), "blurted", pale faces and sweat, and head-hopping without breaks. The logistics are more dangerous for credibility with an agent. The Ventspils–Gdynia dates don't work. The "six hours" doesn't square with the 9 PM docking. A stevedore sails with the ship. The gadgets contradict Ch56. And a cargo ship has elevators, a balcony and a 20-seat boardroom. An acquisitions reader who spots two of these will start distrusting the rest.

**The single most important fix:** tighten Ch57–70 around its three real turning points: the Tallinn meeting heist (Ch58–60 merged into one tense sequence), the medevac → Finch → letter chain, and the name slip on the Ventspils deck. Cut or merge the connective chapters (57, 65, and 64+70) and the repeated brooding and self-reproach, and make Benedikt's POV honest about what he knows (ch 69). That should take the section from about 8,500 to about 6,000 words, and it would read as a thriller rather than a diary of anxiety. The calendar errors (§3.1–3.3) and gadget continuity (§3.10–3.11) should be fixed in the same pass.
