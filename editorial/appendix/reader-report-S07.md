## 3. Continuity, logic and factual errors

### A. Timeline and dateline
1. **Ch55 dateline versus "thirty minutes" [ch 54, ch 55.**
   - The problem: they left at 4:30 AM, drove 30 minutes and boarded at about 5:00–5:15. Benedikt says "see you in thirty minutes", which puts the meeting near 5:45, yet Ch55 is headed 7:30 AM. He then says "I wasn't expecting you so early", although Alex brought her exactly when told.
   - **Fix:** in (ch 54) have him say "I will see you in my office at half past seven." Then either cut "wasn't expecting you so early" or make her deliberately early (7:10).
2. **"The next three days we would be loading the cargo" (ch 55).**
   - The problem: the ship sails at 4:00 AM on 23 April (Ch60, book.md l.2486), which leaves only two days (Monday and Tuesday). This also matches Benedikt's own plan in Ch21 (l.787: "starting Monday… Wednesday was when project 'sub rosa' would commence").
   - **Fix:** "The next two days…"
3. **"The voyage that starts two days from now" (ch 53).**
   - The problem: this matches Jules's "three days from now" from 18 April, but the ship actually sails on the 23rd. The two leaders will expect a departure on the 21st.
   - **Fix:** either have Sanchez say "we board in two days", or add one line in Ch55 where she notes the delay to herself. Two options:
     - Jules's decoding was off by two days, which is a nice small irony.
     - The loading counts as "the voyage".
4. **"Four years back" (ch 52).**
   - The problem: Maxwell met Sanchez in 2012 (Ch12–15, the intern competition), so it has been about two years.
   - **Fix:** "two years back".
5. **"5:00 PM… still had one hour to kill" (ch 51).**
   - The problem: at 3:30 he has 90 minutes.
   - **Fix:** make it "an hour and a half", or drop the arithmetic.
6. **The unseen second meeting (ch 56).**
   - The problem: it happens "few minutes after she left" on the 19th, but Ch53 ended with her disappearing in a car. She would have to turn round, go back and meet them again, all while Benedikt's driver waits. Meanwhile the whole of 20 April is skipped.
   - **Fix:** put the kit handover on Sunday the 20th and give it one line of setup at the end of Ch53. Better still, dramatise it (see section 4).
7. **The injury heals too fast.**
   - The problem: the calf was shot through on the afternoon of 18 April (l.1499–1515). On the 19th she can't stand unaided in the morning, yet she "leaped forward to grab her sticks" (ch 50) and later rises to hug him (ch 51). By the 21st she is "off the sticks" (ch 55). A through-and-through gunshot to the calf would mean at least a week or two on crutches, or at best a stick.
   - **Fix:** keep one stick through Ch55–56. This also gives her a useful visible vulnerability on the ship. Or make it "down to one stick". Change "leaped" to "reached".
8. **Scene order and dateline in Ch52.**
   - The problem: Benedikt leaves her suite a little after about 4:00 (the 3:30 chapter plus the 5-PM countdown), and Ch52's call is at 4:00. That is tight but workable.
   - **Fix:** push Ch52 to 4:15 PM and Ch53 to 4:45 PM.

### B. Plot logic and motivation
9. **Captive to free agent overnight.**
   - The problem: in Ch49 her room is "well guarded to prohibit any escape route". By the afternoon of the same day she is free to phone her brother, refuse to give her address, cross town to a café, meet the undercover officer hunting Benedikt, and leave "in the car waiting for her" (ch 53). Whose car is it? If it is Benedikt's, his driver has seen her with "Bradley", a TransPacific employee who worked beside Evan. That is a huge risk that nobody registers.
   - **Fix, two options:**
     - (a) She tells Benedikt she is meeting Stephen; he insists on his driver and she must hide Maxwell's presence. The driver becomes a threat.
     - (b) She slips out in a taxi, which is consistent with her tradecraft.
   - Either way, one sentence in Ch51 or Ch53 closes the gap.
10. **She tells Benedikt about Bradley (ch 50).**
   - The problem: "…her interaction with Bradley. She told him how she found about him and how she managed to get the map." She has just told the trafficker, in effect, that a TransPacific program manager recruited and trained her to steal his map. That exposes an undercover officer. Benedikt never follows up; the name "Bradley" does not appear in his thread again.
   - **Fix:** have her hold Bradley back deliberately (for example, "She told him everything, except Bradley"). This keeps her intelligent and the reveal in Ch53 intact.
11. **Promise to Benedikt versus behaviour [ch 51, ch 53].**
   - The problem: she promises openness and agrees to keep the trip from Stephen. Within an hour she tells Stephen and Maxwell about the voyage and accepts a spy kit. That duplicity is dramatically good, but the text never acknowledges she is breaking a promise.
   - **Fix:** add one line of interiority in Ch53 or Ch56 ("She had broken her first promise to him within the hour").
12. **Maxwell's professional passivity ch 53.**
   - The problem: he is a police officer with a boss (Drake) who just authorised a private jet. He knows the ship, the port, the date, the route and that Benedikt is in Tallinn. Yet he lets a 17–20-year-old civilian board a trafficking vessel alone and settles for "call us daily". He doesn't tail her car or take its plate, and there is no Estonian police liaison.
   - **Fix:** give him a reason he can't act, such as no jurisdiction, no admissible evidence (the bug was illegal, see item 14), or Drake refusing to escalate without proof. Then Sanchez's evidence-gathering becomes necessary, not just stubborn. One or two lines would do it.
13. **"Scotland Yard" in Ayr (ch 53).**
   - The problem: Scotland Yard is the London Metropolitan Police. Policing in Ayr is Police Scotland (Strathclyde Police before April 2013). A cross-border UK trafficking case would sit with SOCA, or the **National Crime Agency** from October 2013.
   - **Fix:** "I am an undercover officer with the National Crime Agency", or with "the Serious Organised Crime Agency" if he started before 2013. Also, "agent" is American usage; "officer" is British.
14. **The illegal bug ch 53.**
   - The problem: an unauthorised microphone in a private boardroom would be inadmissible. At minimum it needs warrant language, and Maxwell is otherwise careful.
   - **Fix:** either "with a warrant I had fought three months for" or "strictly off the books, which is why none of it can go to court". The second version also solves item 12.
15. **Maxwell suspected a teenage intern of being a secret agent (ch 53).**
   - The problem: this strains belief given her age.
   - **Fix:** soften it to "I wondered who had sent you".
16. **Two versions of how she found John Patterson and Ayr.**
   - The problem:
     - Ch50 (ch 50): the father let the name slip by accident. The evidence for Ayr is a star on her geography-project map of Scotland (ch 50), Ayr receipts, and a notebook of addresses from the office bag (ch 50).
     - Ch12 (l.411): "the same diary, which had John's name scribbled on it… the page that had the map of Ayr… The letter TP scribbled within parenthesis next to John's name".
   - **Fix:** reconcile them. The office-bag notebook (ch 50) should be the diary with John's name, "(TP)" and the Ayr map page. Say so in (ch 50): "…and on one page, in his hand, 'John Patterson (TP)'". Then (ch 50) can stay as the first memory and the diary confirms it.
17. **The bakery logic (ch 50).**
   - The problem: the family knew he went to the bakery every Sunday; Sanchez watched him leave for it (Ch1). They wouldn't assume "he went to work", and they would check the bakery within the hour, not leave it to a police officer to trace "the path to the bakery".
   - **Fix:** "He never came back from the bakery. The baker said he had not come in that morning…" Then the choir witnesses remain the police's discovery.
18. **"The choir group from the Kirk… saw a person entering the car" (ch 50).**
   - The problem: Ch1 puts him leaving at around 6 AM. A choir outside at that hour is unlikely.
   - **Fix:** push the bakery run to 8 AM in Ch1, or make it "a woman on her way to early service".
19. **"Mom… at the church" on Saturday afternoon, back at 6:30 PM [ch 50].**
   - The problem: this is plausible (cleaning or flowers), but after "one Sunday morning I decided to put together a plan" (ch 50) the days blur.
   - **Fix:** keep it, but one word such as "choir practice" would ground it.
20. **Age and independence (ch 50).**
   - The problem: an early-teen girl (Ch1) moves alone to another town and "started doing some odd jobs after college hours and was earning enough money to rent a place". The book says "school" elsewhere. This is a book-wide age problem; this paragraph makes it acute.
   - **Fix:** pin her age (for example 15 in Feb 2010, 17 in Nov 2011, 19–20 in 2014) and make the Ch1 description ("early teens") match. Use "school" consistently for secondary education and "college" only if she has left school.
21. **Benedikt's two faces [ch 50].**
   - The problem: he is the custodian of the trafficking route, yet he promises to "find your father" without a flicker of self-awareness. Sanchez, who knows exactly who he is, never presses him on it.
   - **Fix:** one exchange would sharpen both characters. For example, she asks "And what if it was your people?" and he doesn't answer.
22. **Benedikt does not already know the father.**
   - The problem: the Ch55 photo reveal only works if he knows Daniel by face, not by name. Sanchez tells him the "entire story" (ch 50), including presumably her surname and her father's first name.
   - **Fix:** keep the father unnamed in her telling (it currently is, which is good). You could also add in Ch49/50 that he knows her only as "Sanchez", which Ch49 implies.
23. **"Don't ask me how I got access" (ch 53) versus Maxwell's "How can you trust Benedikt" (ch 53).**
   - The problem: this works only because Stephen earlier asked "how did you escape from Benedikt?". Fine, but Sanchez never answered that question, so her "business deal" (ch 53) is all the men get.
   - **Fix:** a brief reaction from Maxwell would help (for example, he guesses she has turned Benedikt's feelings into access and is jealous). That would also pay off his infatuation.
24. **She "had never seen a ship from such close distance before" ch 54.**
   - The problem: she grew up in Scotland, lived in the port town of Ayr, and spent the previous week in Nazaré and Lisbon.
   - **Fix:** "had never set foot on one".
25. **Maxwell's retelling ch 53.**
   - The problem: "I followed you… the plane to Lisbon… At the Lisbon airport, you… evaded me as well." The reader already saw this; it is only acceptable if Sanchez learns something new. The one new element, "instead of coming after you I decided to chase them… that was a terrible mistake", is never explained.
   - **Fix:** say concretely what he learned by chasing Benedikt's men (was that how he found Tallinn?).
26. **The spy kit's route [ch 56].**
   - The problem: Maxwell gave her "a small bag which could easily fit into her handbag", yet on board it sits in a sewn-in hidden compartment of her suitcase with "the cloth covering the bag". Who made the compartment? When did she get a suitcase at all? She arrived in Tallinn on a stretcher with nothing.
   - **Fix:** Benedikt had clothes and a case bought for her (one line in Ch53/54), and Maxwell's pouch came with a false lining she fitted herself. Or simplify: the pouch is sewn into the lining of her handbag.
27. **"Camera" versus "video recorder" earrings ch 56.**
   - The problem: "The left earring was the camera and the right was the video recorder" is technically confused.
   - **Fix:** "the left earring took stills, the right recorded video" (each with its own lens), or one earring camera plus a separate recorder.

### C. Geography and ships
28. **Spelling:** "Palijassaare" [ch 54, three times] should be **Paljassaare**.
29. **"Palijassaare was in the northwestern part of the peninsula" (ch 54).**
   - The problem: Paljassaare is itself a peninsula in north-west Tallinn (Põhja-Tallinn district).
   - **Fix:** "Paljassaare lay on its own small peninsula in the north-west of the city." The name means roughly "bare island", from *paljas* (bare) and *saar* (island), so "Barren Island" is acceptable. A nice true detail to add: part of it is a bird reserve, which would feed the "scenic but dull" note.
30. **"It took them thirty minutes to get to the harbor" (ch 54).**
   - The problem: from Freedom Square (Hotel Palace) to Paljassaare is about 7 km, roughly 12–15 minutes on empty roads at 4:30 AM.
   - **Fix:** "a quarter of an hour".
31. **"The sun rising beyond the edges of the ocean" (ch 54).**
   - The problem: it is the Baltic Sea (Gulf of Finland, Tallinn Bay). In late April the sun rises in the east-north-east at about 5:45 EEST, over the bay towards Viimsi, so there may be land on the horizon. The pre-dawn timing in (ch 54) is correct.
   - **Fix:** "the sun lifting out of the bay".
32. **"The busy serpentine streets of Estonia" (ch 53).**
   - **Fix:** "of Tallinn". A single street isn't "of Estonia".
33. **"Ayr, a port town" (ch 50) versus "Ayr was a port city and a major transportation hub" (ch 50).**
   - The problem: Ayr is a modest town on the Firth of Clyde (population about 46,000) with a small harbour, not a "major transportation hub". Ch11 also calls it "a large city".
   - **Fix:** "a busy harbour town on the Firth of Clyde, with Prestwick airport next door". Prestwick is real and genuinely a freight airport, which helps the logic.
34. **"About three hours away from our village" (ch 50).**
   - The problem: Girbin is fictional, and three hours from Ayr places it in the Highlands or the North East.
   - **Fix:** fine, but decide where Girbin is and keep it consistent.
35. **Ship plausibility ch 54–55.**
   - The problem: a jet-black *cargo* ship has a "bank of elevators", balcony cabins with king-size beds, an owner's office "spanning the width of the stern" with French windows and a balcony, a "captain's great-cabin" (an age-of-sail term), and "two marines" on guard. Merchant ships don't carry marines.
   - **Fix:**
     - Establish in one sentence that DAZZLE is a converted ro-pax or reefer with refitted owner's quarters. Owner's suites on merchant ships are real; balconies are rare but could be justified by a refit.
     - Change "bank of elevators" to "a lift".
     - Change "great-cabin" to "the captain's quarters".
     - Change "marines" to "armed guards", and match Ch60's "mariners", which is also wrong.
36. **"Towards the end of the shaft" (ch 54).**
   - The problem: a shaft is the lift shaft.
   - **Fix:** "at the end of the corridor".
37. **"They started spiraling down the narrow path towards the aft" (ch 56).**
   - The problem: both her cabin and his office are on the upper deck [ch 54, ch 55], so there is no reason to go down.
   - **Fix:** "They set off along the narrow passage towards the stern", or say where they are going (the loading deck with Benedikt, perhaps).
38. **"Head of the stevedore" (ch 55).**
   - **Fix:** "the head stevedore", matching Ch60.
39. **Register slips for Scottish characters.**
   - The problem: "mom" [ch 50 and elsewhere] and "soccer ball" (ch 50) are Americanisms in the mouth of a Scottish girl. A Scottish family says "Mum" and "football".
   - **Fix:** the author may want a single global decision here.
40. **Kali (ch 50).**
   - The problem: *kali* is a real Estonian fermented bread drink, so this is a good detail. But ordered as "a bottle of Kali" and then "gulped" at lunch, the reader needs a gloss.
   - **Fix:** gloss it once in Ch49 ("kali, the dark, faintly sour bread drink the Estonians love"), and use lower case.

### D. Cross-chapter

## 4. Pacing and flow

**Overall shape.** The section has two halves. Part IV's close (Ch50–53) is almost entirely *talk*: two long confessional monologues (Sanchez about 2,000 words, Maxwell about 1,300), a phone call and a lovers' quarrel. Part V's opening (Ch54–56) is almost entirely *description*: the port, the ship, the cabin, the office, a suit, the gadgets. The only real *event* in 9,000 words is Benedikt's reaction to the photograph (ch 55). For a thriller entering its final act, that is too little.

**Where it drags**
- **Ch50's backstory monologue ch 50** is the section's biggest drag.
  - The material is new to the reader, since the 2010–2011 gap was never dramatised, and parts of it are affecting.
  - But it is delivered as a single uninterrupted quoted speech of about 1,900 words, including a flashback within the flashback (basement → bedroom → closet → dinner) with process detail nobody needs: the stool, the dust, latching the door, drawing the curtain ch 50.
  - At the moment she says "Let me come to the point" (ch 50), Benedikt's "I like details" (ch 50) is charming, but it licenses another 700 words.
  - Recommendation: cut by half. Keep the bakery and the choir, the policeman going cold, the geography map with the star (a lovely detail), the Ayr receipts, "San", and the dinner-table lie to her mother. Summarise the search choreography in two sentences. Interleave two or three short beats of Benedikt reacting. His face at the bakery detail would be a superb early tremor that sets up Ch55.
- **Maxwell's confession ch 53.** The first half (Scotland Yard, the sealed corridor, the vase) is new and good. The second half (training her, the poker tip, following her through Zurich and Lisbon) retells what the reader saw. Cut ch 53 to about three sentences and keep only the new information: his motive, and what he learned by chasing Benedikt's men.
- **Maxwell's gaze ch 53.** This is 350 words of rapture before she speaks. "The fact that Maxwell still held calmness was the epitome of bravery" undercuts him. Trim to one strong image.
- **Ship and room description ch 54, ch 55].** Four set-piece descriptions in a row, each with its own "tastefully decorated" inventory. Keep the black hull with "DAZZLE" and the balcony sunrise. Cut the furniture lists by two-thirds.

**Where it rushes**
- **The turn from captive to ally ch 50–51.** This is the section's key reversal, and it happens across a quarrel and a 15-line apology. Benedikt's decision to take her aboard a trafficking voyage, among the people he calls "wolves", deserves a moment of cost. What does he risk with his principals? What does he tell the captain about her?
- **Ch52 is thin (565 words).** Stephen and Maxwell's reaction to the call is the only content. It could merge into Ch53 as its opening.
- **The spy-kit handover (ch 56)** is a key plot device delivered as a one-paragraph flashback. Either dramatise it briefly at the end of Ch53 (Maxwell presses a pouch into her hand as she leaves, which is also a stronger chapter ending than the car vanishing), or cut the flashback and let the reader discover the kit with her in Ch56 with only a clause of explanation.

**Scene order and merge suggestions**
- **Merge Ch52 into Ch53.** The phone call becomes the opening of the café chapter from Maxwell's POV, and the meeting follows.
- **Move the kit handover** to the end of Ch53, as above. This fixes the timeline gap in item 6 of section 3.
- **Merge Ch54 into Ch55**, or trim Ch54 to about 500 words. The boarding and the office are one continuous morning, and the photo reveal is the payoff that should come sooner.
- **Rework Ch56's ending** so the chapter ends on something: the reconvened meeting, Benedikt's changed manner, or her first secret recording. At present the Ch55 cliffhanger dissipates into brooding plus an Alex exchange, and Ch57 then says nothing happened.
- **Cut ch 51**, the repeated guilt and love beat (item 41 in section 3).

**Repeated beats inside the section**
- Hand-holding followed by the "heart missed/skipped many beats" line, twice [ch 50, ch 53].
- "Took a pause to observe her reaction", four times [ch 51, ch 53, ch 55].
- Sanchez is euphoric, then crashes ch 50, ch 51 and elsewhere].
- Benedikt stares and she looks away, three times [ch 51, ch 55].

## 5. Prose problems (section-specific)

The prose has the manuscript's usual ornateness, but in this section it concentrates in three areas: **romantic interiority**, **misused vocabulary** and **dialogue tags**. Counts within S07: "loom" 7, "in haste" 6, "lost in" 5, "voice" 24, "with great interest" 4, "continued further" 4, "took a pause" 4, "saunter" 4, "delirious" 3.

**1. Wrong word or near-miss (the most frequent problem)**
- "You are nothing but a stubborn **gunk**." (ch 50). The intended word is *mule* or *fool*; "gunk" is sludge.
- "there was clear **ingenuity** in his **gullible** voice" (ch 55). Should be *ingenuousness* in an *artless* voice.
- "Sanchez's eyes **bellowed** hope." (ch 55). Eyes cannot bellow; try *shone with*.
- "she bellowed with an **orotund** voice" (ch 50); "blurted **pompously**" (ch 50); "fuming with **dander**" (ch 50).
- "our home being too **remorseful**" (ch 50). Should be *mournful* or *full of him*.
- "He was always a mystery to **him**" (ch 53). Should be *her*.
- "It was hard not to ignore the clout Benedikt had" (ch 54). The double negative says the opposite of what is meant.
- "The room though had a contemporary appearance, it also sported a modern outlook" (ch 54). This is a tautology. "Outlook" is also misused for *manner* or *look* across the section: "charming outlook" (ch 53), "mysterious outlook" (ch 53), "weird outlook" (ch 55).
- "Benedikt was **appalled**" at a hug (ch 51), and they "were downright **appalled**" (ch 53). The meaning is *stunned*.
- "he had to be more considerate and **emphatic**" (ch 50). Should be *empathetic*.

**2. Purple romantic interiority that tips into bathos**
- "The top two button of her shirt was left open exposing her long neck, and the fact that Maxwell still held calmness was the epitome of bravery. She was the prose for his poetry, the central protagonist of his stories…" (ch 53)
- "She was floating away, hallucinated and delirious, gently dissolving in his aura." (ch 50)
- "She tried hard to oppress her avidity but failed miserably as she squirmed vulnerably." (ch 50)
- "Gnarled in his ensemble she slowly continued further" (ch 50)

**3. Stacked, redundant dialogue tags and adverbs**
- "and hushed in an imperious tone" (ch 50); "with a tremulous voice" [ch 51, ch 53]; "with utmost care eased out one word at a time" (ch 51); "responded in an equally shrieked tone" (ch 50). Most lines need only "said", or no tag.
- "she answered, ignoring his question in a complete unconventional manner" (ch 55).

**4. Scenic description built from abstractions**
- "The casts of silver radiance encouraged by the pre-dawn sunrays created a mesmerized stature. And the subtle chilliness dissolved in the air with perfect discretion." (ch 54)
- "The midmorning sun was fighting hard with its ether to warm the atmosphere with a distinct verve… gently warming the cold gelid airs." (ch 56)

**5. Head-hopping within scenes**
- Ch53 moves from Maxwell (ch 53) to Sanchez (ch 53) to Stephen (ch 53) to Sanchez (ch 53).
- Ch55 moves into Benedikt's head for a paragraph (ch 55) inside a Sanchez scene.
- Pick one POV per scene. For Ch53, Sanchez's POV is best: we get to *see* Maxwell earn her trust.

**6. Grammar and mechanics (for the line edit)**

- Stray zero-width characters inside words ("quandary​", "quiet​ steps", "voice​") in Ch51 [ch 51, ch 51, ch 51]. Strip them in the edit.

## 6. Strengths

- **The opening line of Ch50** echoes the prologue's windowsill with real emotional precision: *"I can never forget that dreadful Sunday. I saw him for the last time from the windowsill of my room, slowly drifting away into the morning mist, never to return."* (ch 50). It closes the loop with Ch1 beautifully. Protect it.
- **"San, one day I will take you to all those places. I want you to see it for yourself."** (ch 50). A single intimate line that gives the father a voice, and it is thematically loaded, since the voyage fulfils it darkly. Consider echoing it at the book's climax.
- **The geography-project map** [ch 50]: a child's school map where the father drew his routes and starred Ayr. This is an excellent, novelistic clue, domestic and uncanny. It also rhymes with the smuggling map, one father's map against another. Protect it, and consider foregrounding the rhyme.
- **The police officer "turned cold"** (ch 50). Understated and sinister: it implies reach and corruption without saying it. It is worth a payoff later.
- **"I like details."** (ch 50). Benedikt at his most attractive and most dangerous in three words. Keep it exactly.
- **Maxwell recognising the voice**: *"Where is Stephen?" came a curt reply. Maxwell wouldn't have failed to recognize that voice even in his sleep.* ch 52. Economical and good.
- **Maxwell's unanswered questions** (ch 52): "Did she dodge Benedikt? How come she wasn't in a hurry?… Why didn't she want them to come and get her?" This is the reader's own suspicion, voiced; it creates real tension.
- **Stephen finds his spine** ch 53: "Sanchez had never seen her brother so determined… There was something in him that calmed her agitation." A small, well-placed character arc beat.
- **The vase bug** (ch 53): breaking the pot, apologising, and replacing it with a wired vase. This is a concrete, clever, visual piece of tradecraft that should survive any cut.
- **The ship's first sight**: "It was jet black in color with 'DAZZLE' scribbled on the hull" (ch 54). The black hull with an ironic name is a strong image; "dazzle" also evokes dazzle camouflage, which is thematically apt.
- **The dinner-meeting flirtation** ch 55: "I see my name in tomorrow's dinner meeting." / "Can I not have dinner with you?" This is the only moment of genuine lightness and play between them. Protect it (fixing "ingenuity/gullible").
- **The photograph** ch 55: the best scene in the section. It is set up simply ("Do you have any picture of your father?"). The response is physical and immediate ("his face turned white"), followed by the lie about an urgent call and the retreat to the bedroom. It restores thriller momentum in three paragraphs.
- **Unpacking the kit** ch 56: the latch, the camera sweep, the bathroom, the towel on the floor, the hidden zip. Procedural texture of the kind thriller readers enjoy. It shows Sanchez as competent.
- **Alex's hurt** (ch 56): "Madam, this is my only work? Did I offend you by any chance?" A touching, humanising line for a minor character, and a nice complication of her wish to be unwatched.

## 7. Honest assessment

This section carries a lot of the book's machinery. It fills in the missing 2010–2011 backstory, explains Maxwell, turns Sanchez from captive into infiltrator, and gets her aboard the ship. Its individual ideas are good, some very good: the starred school map, the vase microphone, the photograph that drains Benedikt's face, and the black ship called DAZZLE. But it is told far more than it is dramatised. Two consecutive chapters rest on long monologues that summarise things the reader either already knows or doesn't need at that length. The romance is written in a register (hearts skipping beats, "floating… dissolving in his aura", "the epitome of bravery") that works against the thriller tension the author clearly wants. The logic of the Tallinn chapters is loose: a guarded captive is suddenly free, the undercover officer's cover is casually exposed, and a police officer lets a teenager sail into a trafficking operation on a promise to phone daily. Each gap can be closed with a sentence or two, but together they make the key turn of the book feel convenient rather than earned. Part V then opens with three chapters of décor, and the one true event, the photo, is followed by a chapter that ends mid-corridor.

**The single most important fix:** make the Tallinn turn *earned and dangerous* rather than talked through. Concretely:
- Cut Ch50's monologue roughly in half, and let Benedikt *react* inside it. A flicker at the mention of the Sunday bakery or the parked car plants the Ch55 reveal.
- Have Sanchez deliberately withhold Bradley.
- Give Maxwell a reason he cannot simply call in the police (for example, the bug was off the books and nothing is admissible). That makes her going aboard necessary, not merely stubborn.
- Merge Ch52 into Ch53 and end it on the kit being pressed into her hand.
- End Ch56 on a real beat that answers the photograph.

Done well, this turns about 9,000 words of exposition into roughly 6,500 words of rising tension leading into Part V.
