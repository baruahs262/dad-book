# S04 — Developmental read, Chapters 24–30 (ch 24–30)

Section length: about 9,900 words. By chapter: ch24 2,093 · ch25 1,433 · ch26 1,290 · ch27 594 · ch28 1,270 · ch29 1,123 · ch30 2,084.
All of the section takes place on one day, Thursday 17 April 2014, which was Maundy Thursday. Good Friday (18 April) is a public holiday in Portugal. Missing IDs (ch 26, ch 27, ch 28, ch 29) are scene-break markers (`**\**`) and hold no text.

The section follows three threads. (A) Sanchez and Stephen land in Lisbon, shake off the welcoming party and reach Nazaré and Jules's farmhouse. (B) Maxwell sees Francis's men at the airport, loses Sanchez, follows Francis to Benedikt's jet and plants a bug in the van. (C) Benedikt arrives and berates Francis, then hints at a "bait" plan.

## 1. Chapter-by-chapter summary

### Chapter 24 — "12:00 PM 17th April 2014, Lisbon, Portugal" ch 24
- **POV:** Sanchez mainly, with lapses into Stephen's head [ch 24, ch 24]. Omniscient narrator.
- **Setting:** TAP flight approaching Lisbon; Lisbon airport (immigration, restrooms, arrivals); the metro; "City Hall" square; a café; a bus stand and a lot of unlicensed private cars.
- **Beats:**
  - The plane circles and lands. Stephen fakes sleep, and both scan for "goons" as they get off ch 24.
  - After immigration they spend 20 minutes in a restroom and come out disguised. Sanchez wears khimar/veil and a padded abaya ("middle-aged lady from an Arab nation"). Stephen wears a moustache, a "Bonnie hat" and the uniform of a Portuguese army *cadete aluno* (ch 24). Backstory: Sanchez carried the Arab clothes for her Copenhagen escape and planned the disguises in Zurich (ch 24).
  - They split up for the metro. Sanchez spots a corpulent man and his companions watching the exit and walks past them unrecognised (ch 24).
  - Sanchez gets off at "Santa Apolónia" and Stephen at "Gare do Oriente", both supposedly "in City Hall". Sanchez changes again, into a Nazaré-style "seven skirts" outfit, and the narrator explains the skirts (ch 24).
  - Tourist description of the City Hall square. Sanchez waits in a café and taps Stephen on the shoulder; they fake a lovers' reunion ch 24.
  - Walking dialogue. They realise they are being traced through tickets bought in their own names, and agree to "ditch the train" and travel without leaving an identity trail ch 24. Running gag: Stephen is starving and Sanchez won't let him eat ch 24.
  - The next bus to Nazaré is 30 minutes away. A boy touts a private van, and Sanchez bargains in Portuguese from €200 down to €170 ch 24. They leave within five minutes (ch 24).
- **Reader learns:** Benedikt's people were waiting at the airport; Sanchez is skilled at disguise and speaks Portuguese; the destination is Nazaré, though the reason is not yet given.
- **Ending hook:** Weak. It ends on Stephen's admiration of Sanchez (ch 24) rather than on a threat.

### Chapter 25 — "12:00 PM 17th April 2014, Lisbon, Portugal" ch 25

- **Note:** ch24 and ch25 are the same arrival from two POVs, and together they work well. Because the reader knows the disguise, the reader knows exactly why Maxwell cannot find them. This is the section's best structural idea.

Upstream causes (S03, flag to that editor):
- Ch22 is dated "16th April" but is clearly the morning of the 17th.
- Ch23 has Benedikt's jet leaving Zurich at 9:00 AM Swiss time. A ~2h40 flight lands at ~10:40 Lisbon time (Lisbon is one hour behind Zurich). That puts Benedikt on the ground **before** Sanchez by the ch24 header, and at about the same time if she lands at 10:30.
- Ch21 has Sanchez's flight already gone at 8:35 AM and "landing in next hour".

The day needs one master clock.

**Proposed master clock (fewest edits):**

- Ch26: **1:30 PM, Nazaré.** They arrive mid-afternoon, and a late lunch replaces "dinner" (see below).
- Ch27/28: **12:30 PM**, Lisbon Airport, then Montijo. Push Benedikt's Zurich departure in ch23 to ~11:00 Swiss time, or have the jet held for clearance, so that he lands at ~1:10 PM.
- Ch30: **1:10 PM.** Replace every "dusk/darkness" cue with shade from the tower blocks of Vale da Amoreira, which (ch 30) already half-does ("The shades of the tall building were slowly covering the streets"). Replace "Maxwell waited for another thirty minutes" with a long afternoon wait, so the bugging happens at ~6 PM and ch32's 7:30 still works.
- Ch29: **8:30 PM.** Place it **after** ch30.

**Chapter order.** The order 26 (2:30) → 27 (1:30) → 28 (1:30) → 29 (8:30) → 30 (4:30) is **not** parallel action. It is a reverse jump with no signal. Chapter 30 continues ch27 directly (Maxwell in the car park waiting for the jet). Recommended order:

- Alternatively, fold ch30's opening ch 30, which retells the landing, into the end of ch27 and move ch29 to follow ch30.
- Also warn the S05 editor: ch31 (18 April, 8 AM) is followed by ch32 (17 April, 7:30 PM), another reversal.

**Place names in datelines.** Montijo and Vale da Amoreira are on the south bank of the Tagus in Setúbal district, not in Lisbon. Use "Montijo, Portugal" for ch27, ch28 and the start of ch30, and "Vale da Amoreira, Portugal" once he arrives. Ch26 should read "Nazaré, Portugal".

**Internal clock inside ch26.**
- They arrive "late afternoon" (ch 26); the restaurant is "buzzing" "despite the early evening hour" (ch 26); they finish at "quarter past eight" (ch 26).
- That is a 3–4-hour meal for two fugitives who are supposed to be anxious. Portuguese kitchens also often close between about 3:00 and 7:00 PM.
- **Fix:** Make it a late lunch finished by about 4:30. Then add the hours they kill in town and on the bench before phoning Jules. The bench scene then becomes the dusk it already describes ("the emptiness of the night" (ch 26)).

### 3b. Airports, borders and procedure

1. **Schengen: there is no immigration on this flight.** Switzerland joined Schengen in December 2008. A TAP flight from Switzerland (Zurich or Altenrhein) to Lisbon is an intra-Schengen flight in 2014. There is no passport queue, no diplomatic lane and no immigration chief who can confirm that anyone "cleared immigration".
   - Affected: the long queues [ch 24, ch 25], Maxwell's diplomatic row (ch 25), the armed guard "at the entrance to the immigration" (ch 25), the chief officer scene ch 25, and Francis's "immigration officer told him" (ch 28).
   - This is the most visible factual error in the section. Any European agent or reader will notice it.
   - **Fix (keeps every beat):** The queue becomes the slow crush off the apron bus or jet bridge and the baggage hall. Maxwell's "ID" gets him airside through a PSP (airport police) officer. The chief-officer scene becomes airport police checking **CCTV**. The CCTV can show two passengers matching the pair entering the restroom and **a woman in an abaya and a young soldier** coming out 20 minutes later. That gives Maxwell and the reader the disguise reveal, pays off ch24 and removes the need for ch 25's triple search.
   - The alternative is to route the flight from a non-Schengen origin, but that means changing S03.
2. **"Arrived from Lisbon on TAP airlines this morning"** (ch 25). This should be "from Zurich" (or "from St. Gallen", per ch22).
3. **Maxwell checks the carousel display** "to find the carousel number for the flight they were on" (ch 25). He was on that flight himself (ch22, seated a few rows ahead), so he would already know. The same goes for "his flight" in general.
4. **Maxwell's ID is never specified.** A foreign "badge" would not get anyone through a Portuguese border post, past a guard with a drawn gun (ch 25) or onto the chief's floor. Give the reader one clause, for example "his Europol liaison card". Ch32 later says he had sent "his police officer friend" to guard where Sanchez was headed. The reader needs to know what authority Maxwell has, at the latest here.
5. **Montijo "private terminal".** In 2014 Montijo is **Base Aérea n.º 6**, a Portuguese Air Force base. It had no civilian executive terminal with a receptionist (ch 28), and it has two runways, not one (ch 27). Private jets serving Lisbon used Humberto Delgado Airport's general-aviation apron or Cascais-Tires aerodrome.
   - **Fix:** Keep Montijo but make it sinister: Benedikt's reach gets a private jet cleared into a military base. That is better for the plot. Replace "receptionist" with an Air Force duty sergeant, and keep Maxwell outside the perimeter fence.
   - Montijo reappears later in the book (book.md line 1685), so fix it consistently.
   - One more question the text should answer: why doesn't Benedikt land at Lisbon Airport, where Francis already is? One sentence (avoiding airport scrutiny) settles it.
6. **Cars on the runway.** Francis "took the van straight to the tarmac, parked at the edge of the runway" (ch 28). No airport, and especially no air base, lets a civilian van drive onto the airfield unescorted, let alone park by a live runway. Change to "was waved through to the apron by a sergeant he paid".
7. **"The jet ladder unfolded … Benedikt appeared on the jet bridge"** (ch 28). Airstairs and a jet bridge are different things. Keep "airstairs".
8. **Maxwell hears the argument from the car park** (ch 30). That means hundreds of metres, a barbed-wire fence and spooling-down engines. He could only *see* it, or use binoculars.
9. **Francis was told to catch the pursuer too** (ch21: "I might send you a picture of another person … If you find him, get him as well"). Francis also knows Maxwell "from the past" (ch 25). Yet Maxwell stands in the coffee queue, passes the diplomatic desk, runs past the arrivals hall and then **sits drinking coffee in full view for 20+ minutes** [ch 25, ch 27].
   - This is a plot hole, and it makes Maxwell look frivolous. His stated motive, wanting "the chance of seeing Francis's expression" (ch 25), is petty for a man who has just lost the woman he loves in a city full of her hunters.
   - **Fix:** Have him watch from cover (a newspaper, a pillar, the upper mezzanine). Give him a hard reason to stay on Francis: Francis will lead him to Benedikt, and he knows where Sanchez is going, which ch32 claims retrospectively ("he knew where she was headed"). Put that knowledge **here**, or ch25's panic and ch32's calm contradict each other.
   - The pursuer thread is also dropped: Benedikt never asks Francis about "the other person" in ch28. Add one line of dialogue.
   - Also: Maxwell bought his TAP ticket at the counter with his own credit card (ch22). Benedikt's people traced Sanchez through ticket data and could identify Maxwell the same way. This is a free plot opportunity.
10. **The police officer's exit** (ch 27). "The officer took the car to the nearest gas station and got off the car." No reason is given, and in ch30 Maxwell is suddenly driving alone. Ch32 says he "had sent his police officer friend to guard the place where he was expecting her". **Fix:** State it here, for example: "He sent the officer north to Nazaré with the photograph and took the wheel himself." That fixes this hole and seeds ch32.

### 3c. Lisbon geography

11. **The Airport → "City Hall" metro route is invented** ch 24].
    - Lisbon's City Hall (Paços do Concelho, Praça do Município) has no metro stop called "City Hall", and there are not "two stations in City Hall".
    - **Santa Apolónia** and **Gare do Oriente** are railway terminals about 7 km apart. Oriente is in Parque das Nações and is only the 3rd stop from the airport, so Stephen would get off **before** Sanchez, not "ten minutes after" her (ch 24). From Oriente he could not "pace" to City Hall.
    - The airport's Red Line doesn't reach Santa Apolónia; that needs a change to the Blue Line at São Sebastião.
    - "The train, which from the airport only comes to the City Hall" (ch 24) is false. The Red Line runs to São Sebastião, with changes to three other lines.
    - **Fix A:** They meet at Praça do Comércio. Sanchez gets off at Terreiro do Paço and Stephen at Baixa-Chiado (both Blue Line, both 5 minutes' walk away; one change at São Sebastião).
    - **Fix B (cleaner for the plot):** Make **Oriente** the rendezvous. It is an intercity train *and* bus hub, and the crowded Vasco da Gama shopping centre is next door. That also solves item 12.
12. **Bus to Nazaré.** Intercity coaches to Nazaré (Rede Expressos) leave from the **Sete Rios** terminal (Jardim Zoológico metro), and some routes use Oriente. There is no Nazaré bus stand "five minutes" from City Hall (ch 24). Relocate the tout scene to Sete Rios or Oriente.
13. **City Hall architecture** (ch 24). "Rebuilt in the late 18th century, … 17th-century gothic looks" is wrong. The medieval building was lost in the 1755 earthquake. The current Paços do Concelho is neoclassical, built 1865–1880 after an 1863 fire. Gothic is wrong in any century here. Also, "street food stalls" and "monumental … must-see" overstate it; Praça do Comércio fits better.
14. **"Breeze traveling from the nearby ocean"** (ch 24). Central Lisbon's breeze comes off the Tagus estuary. Say "from the river".
15. **"This westernmost city of continental Europe"** (ch 27). Lisbon is the westernmost *capital*; Cascais and Sintra lie further west.
16. **Signs "all in Portuguese"** (ch 24) at a tourist transport hub is unlikely. It's a small point, and cutting it costs nothing.

### 3d. Nazaré geography and culture

17. **Alcobaça** (ch 26). It is a separate town about 12 km **southeast** and inland, not "the northeastern part of Nazaré". The hilltop restaurant with "the vastness of the Atlantic Ocean" (ch 26) and the walk after dinner to "wooden benches by the beach corridor" (ch 26) cannot both be in Alcobaça.
    - **Fix:** Send the taxi, or the funicular, up to **O Sítio**, Nazaré's clifftop quarter. It is 110 m above the beach, full of restaurants, and has the Atlantic panorama the text describes. Afterwards they walk or take the funicular down to the promenade (Avenida da República) and the beach. It is geographically exact and more atmospheric.
18. **"Seven skirts"** (ch 24). The *sete saias* is the costume of **Nazaré's** fishwives specifically, not a general "traditional Portuguese" outfit, and not something that blends in in central Lisbon. The folk explanations are the seven days, seven colours of the rainbow, seven virtues and **seven waves** (women waiting for the fishing boats counting waves). There is nothing "biblical" about the waves.
    - **Fix:** Have Sanchez put the costume on *in Nazaré*, where it camouflages her. Or keep it in Lisbon but let Stephen tease her for dressing "like a Nazaré fishwife in the middle of Lisbon". Drop "biblical".
19. **Fado** (ch 26). It dates from the 1820s–1840s in Lisbon's poor quarters; "15th-century" is wrong. The accompaniment is the twelve-string *guitarra portuguesa* plus the *viola* (classical guitar), not "two people with a wire-strung acoustic guitar". Fado in a Nazaré tourist restaurant is plausible.
20. **Menu** (ch 26). "Acorda" needs the cedilla (*açorda de camarão*). Cod is fine, but Nazaré is famous for fresh grilled fish, *caldeirada* and dried fish (*peixe seco*), which is better local colour.
21. **"Cabeco" embossed** (ch 26). If this is the restaurant's name it should be *Cabeço*. Otherwise cut it, since the reader cannot parse it.
22. **"Twenty pesos"** (ch 29). Portugal used euros in 2014. The line should be "twenty euros".
23. **Valado dos Frades** (ch 29). The location and the ~5 km distance are **accurate**, which is good.
    - "Paddy fields": the lowland is drained lagoon farmland. Check whether rice is right; "flat fields" or "the old lagoon fields" is safer and won't read as Asian to UK readers.
    - "Clear moon sky": the full moon was 15 April 2014 (the lunar-eclipse night). Two days later it rose at roughly 10 PM, so at about 9 PM it would be low or not yet up. Verify, or use starlight.
    - "Past nine pm and most of the families would have gone to bed" is un-Portuguese, since dinner is often at 8–9 PM. Say "the lights were few".
24. **Missed local colour.** Nazaré is world-famous for the giant waves at Praia do Norte (McNamara's record, 2011). It is Holy Week (ch31 has church bells on Good Friday morning), so the processions and Easter crowds would explain the "swarming" tourists in April. Both are there for the taking.

### 3e. The Portuguese dialogue (Brazilian, and partly wrong)

The phrases mix Brazilian forms (*você*, *compartilhar*, *estande*) with errors. Suggested European Portuguese, which matches the setting:

### 3f. Disguise and logistics

25. **Stephen's cadet uniform** (ch 24). Where did they get a Portuguese army *cadete-aluno* uniform in Zurich overnight? ch 24 explains only Sanchez's abaya. Impersonating a soldier is a crime and draws attention: a uniformed cadet stepping off a civilian flight from Switzerland is memorable, not invisible. A "boonie" hat (misspelled "Bonnie") is field kit, not dress uniform; Portuguese army dress uses the beret (*boina*).
    - **Fix:** Make Stephen a backpacker or a Fátima pilgrim. It is Holy Week, so pilgrims are everywhere, and a pilgrim needs nothing but a scallop-shell or rosary and a rucksack. Or keep the uniform but say where it came from.
26. **Khimar** (ch 24). A khimar covers head and shoulders. A garment covering the face "from her nose" down is a **niqab**. Say "khimar and niqab".
27. **Three costume changes in one day**: Arab woman, Nazaré lass, tourist [ch 24, ch 26]. Each is described at length. The middle one serves no purpose, since she changes in the metro toilets and then only walks to a café. Cut it, or move it to Nazaré (item 18).
28. **The lovers charade** (ch 24) sits oddly after the siblings "hurtle" at a dyspneic pace through the streets (ch 24). Lovers stroll. Pick one.
29. **"Sanchez had strict instructions to keep the phones off"** (ch 26). As written, *she* received the instructions; the intended meaning is that she *gave* them: "Sanchez had ordered them to keep their phones off."
30. **Local SIM** ch 29. Sanchez, the trained operative, says "Why didn't it come to my mind before?" This undercuts her competence. A new SIM in the same handset is still traceable by IMEI, which is exactly the kind of thing she should know. Let *her* say that, and have Jules lend them a spare phone instead.
31. **"Jules was expecting them but was unsure when"** (ch 26). How does Jules know they're coming at all, if the phones have been off? One clause fixes it, for example "Stephen had emailed him from Zurich".
32. **Sanchez wanders off** ch 26. The paranoid one crosses the street without a word while Stephen is in a phone box, and Stephen can't see her across a street for five minutes. The emotional beat is good, but give it a line of self-awareness from her ("I'm sorry. I needed a minute") or make the distance real (down onto the sand).
33. **Gate contradictions** (ch 29). "The house welcomed them with an open gate. It had two distinct pillars, but no gate." Say "gateposts with no gate". The "corridor … several hundred feet" should be a driveway, lane or track.
34. **Windows** (ch 29). The "edifices … designed using bricks in a crisscross diamond shaped fashion" are presumably openwork brick screens (*grelhas*), and "open all season" with a plastic drape is unclear. Describe the actual object.

### 3g. The van set piece (ch30)

35. **Route prose contradicts itself** ch 30.
    - Maxwell loses sight of the van, then can "get a clear sight of the van even when he was behind a few kilometers". Nobody can see a van several km away.
    - "Moved his car to the middle lane" of "a four-lane road": with two lanes each way there is no middle lane.
    - The van turns towards the A33 in ch 30 and then "took the ramp to enter A33 south" in ch 30, which is a duplicate.
    - Montijo to Vale da Amoreira is roughly 15–20 minutes in total, not 10 plus 20 minutes on the motorway.
    - "Amoreria" is a typo for **Amoreira**.
36. **The pay car park with an attendant** (ch 30) in Vale da Amoreira, a social-housing estate, is unlikely and unnecessary. He can simply park on the next street.
37. **Keys** ch 30. The van has remote central locking ("unlocking beep" (ch 30)). A "set of keys" needs explaining (a locksmith's try-out set or jiggler keys) or should become another tool.
    - **The key bunch left hanging in the passenger door** (ch 30) while two men stand at the van, one of them "standing outside" (ch 30), and then kneel to pick up papers (ch 30), is not credible; they would see it.
    - **Fix, and it is also the section's best tension opportunity:** Have Maxwell *realise while under the van* that the keys are hanging in the lock, a metre above a man's knee. Play the whole document-dropping moment against that. Then the discovery isn't a flat afterthought ("How could I do this?"); it drives the scene.
38. **Hiding** [ch 30].
    - Clinging to the exhaust with a leg looped round it is what you do when a vehicle is about to drive off. Under a parked van he would lie flat behind a wheel.
    - The exhaust would still be hot after ~30–60 minutes parked. The text anticipates this, but it is a distraction.
    - The two men "got to the ground" to collect papers and still don't see a man and a backpack under the van at eye level. At minimum, put the papers on the far side, or have one man's torch sweep just short of him.
    - It is unclear who drops the papers and what the "large crashing sound" is (papers don't crash). If it is a box of files, say so, and make the documents matter (see Pacing).
39. **"He was dripping in sweat … unsure whether … the hot weather"** (ch 30). A mid-April evening in Setúbal is typically about 18–22 °C, so "hot weather" is a stretch.
40. **Maxwell doesn't speak Portuguese** (ch 30), yet his plan is to eavesdrop on Portuguese-speaking crews through a bug. He needs a translator, meaning the police officer, which is a reason for the ch 30 phone call. Say so.
41. **POV slip** (ch 30): "Benedikt wasn't in a hurry. There wasn't even a shade of urgency he demonstrated while driving." Benedikt isn't driving, and Maxwell can't see into the van. Rephrase as Maxwell's inference ("The van was in no hurry").

### 3h. Other logic

42. **Maxwell's triple search** ch 25 repeats itself. More importantly, he never checks the restrooms, which is where they are. This is a good irony once the CCTV fix is in (3b.1).
43. **Francis's POV is duplicated.** Maxwell watches his panic (ch 27), ch28 retells it ch 28, and the "men to every outlet" order is given twice.
44. **Status inconsistency.** Francis is the "undisputed king" of the land (ch 25) and can "commit any crime in Portugal … unscathed" (ch 30), yet Benedikt treats him like a valet ("useless baboons" (ch 28)), and Francis frets that Benedikt can "dethrone" him (ch 28). This can be made consistent in one sentence: he is king in Lisbon, but Benedikt's paymasters are bigger. As it stands, it just reads as tonal whiplash.
45. **Damian: "We are on the run for the last four days"** (ch 28). The pursuers are not "on the run". Use "on her trail". Four days (13–17 April) is correct.
46. **"The immigration officer told him about her departure"** (ch 28). It should be "arrival" or "that she had cleared".
47. **Motivation for Nazaré arrives late.** Nazaré is named in ch 24 with no explanation, and the reason (Jules and the map) appears only at ch 26, ~2,500 words later. Either seed it on the plane in ch24 (one line: "Jules would be waiting in Nazaré, if Stephen was right about him") or make the withholding deliberate and flagged.
48. **"Portuguese was her third language in school"** (ch 24). Portuguese is a rare third language in a Scottish school. If there's no family reason (the name Roberto?), say where she learned it; it also underpins ch31.
49. **Stephen's joke** (ch 26), "If it wasn't for you we would have been hungry and tortured", doesn't land. It reads as praise that she prevented hunger, which is the opposite of the running gag. Try "If it weren't for you we'd be well fed … and tortured."

## 4. Pacing and flow

**Overall shape.** The section contains two genuinely good set pieces: the twin-POV airport escape (ch24/25) and Maxwell bugging the van (ch30). They are joined by roughly 3,500 words of travelogue with **no antagonist pressure on the protagonists at all**. After ch 24 nothing threatens Sanchez or Stephen for the rest of the section: the drive is "uneventful" (ch 26), dinner is long, the evening is serene and they sleep without dreams. A thriller can afford one breather chapter here, but ch26 and ch29 together are two.

**Where it drags**
- **ch24 middle** ch 24: the disguise description, a second disguise, the seven-skirts explainer and the City Hall guidebook paragraph. That is roughly 800 words before the siblings exchange a line.
- **ch25** ch 25: three near-identical search loops. Then ch 25: a nine-paragraph bureaucratic stand-off whose only output is one fact. Compress it to one paragraph, or replace it with the CCTV reveal (3b.1), which turns dead time into a payoff.
- **ch26** ch 26: restaurant ambience, view, fado explainer, wine, menu, costume change. Then **ch 26 is a backstory dump** in the middle of the scene. Move the Jules backstory to the moment Stephen dials him, cut it by half, and let Jules's personality do the rest when he appears.
- **ch29** ch 29: three paragraphs of drive and house description. The house tour (stucco, stone wall, diamond brick windows, chair arrangement) can go to two sentences; we will see the house in daylight in ch31.
- **ch30** ch 30: about 600 words of lane-by-lane route directions (two-lane road, right turn, A33, middle lane, ramp, exit, unnamed street, one-way lane, car park, ticket). One paragraph would do.

**Where it rushes**
- The bus-stand negotiation ch 24 is brisk and good, but the escape from Lisbon then happens off the page. A single checkpoint (one of Francis's men eyeing the vans at Sete Rios) would keep the heat on.
- Benedikt's switch from fury to calm ch 28 happens during a phone call we can't hear. It's fine as a mystery, but give the reader one visible sign of what changed, such as a glance at a text or a smile.
- The payoff of the bugging: the receiver shows "a blank tone" (ch 30), and the chapter ends on dinner. The set piece needs a sting, such as the first crackle of voices, a word he half-catches ("Nazaré"?), or the documents.

**Repeated beats**
- Francis's airport panic appears three times [ch 27; ch 28; ch 30].
- The jet landing is told twice in full ch 28 and ch 30.
- The starving-Stephen gag appears five times [ch 24, ch 24, ch 26, ch 26]. It is charming twice and wearing by the fifth.
- Costume changes: three.
- "Maxwell/Francis mumbled to himself" as a device, three to four times.

**Merge, cut or move**
- **Merge ch27 into ch28** as a single Montijo chapter from Maxwell's POV, with Francis as the visible sufferer. Alternatively keep ch28 in Francis's POV and cut ch27 to three sentences at the start of ch30. The current three-chapter spread (27, 28, 30) covers one continuous piece of action with two retellings.
- **Merge ch26 and ch29** into one Nazaré chapter: arrival, meal, bench, Jules, farmhouse. Cut about 40% of the description. Place it after the van chapter so the day ends in Nazaré at 8:30 PM.
- Recommended final order: **24 (Sanchez, airport) / 25 (Maxwell, airport) / 27+28 (Montijo) / 30 (Vale da Amoreira, the bug) / 26+29 (Nazaré evening)**. The section then ends on the protagonists falling asleep, unaware of the planted bug, Benedikt's "bait" and the unknown "him", which is a far stronger ironic close than ending on Maxwell ordering Asian food.
- **Make the documents matter** (ch 30). If one sheet blows under the van, Maxwell pockets it, and it carries a Nazaré address or a photo of Jules, the van chapter has a hook and the bait plan gets a face.

## 5. Prose problems (section-specific)

Recurring issues, most serious first. All quotes are verbatim.

1. **Thesaurus misfires**: words chosen for register rather than meaning. This is the most frequent problem, and it is what most often makes the voice sound unintentionally comic.
   - "Throughout the walk, she was **superfluous** and vigilant." (ch 24) ("on edge")
   - "She was **dyspneic**, and she spoke with an **oscillating** tone" (ch 24) ("breathless"; "uneven")
   - "they concluded their dinner, looking **contempt** and regaled." (ch 26) ("content")
   - "From his **delirious** appearance, it was evident he was aware" (ch 27) ("frantic")
   - "Maxwell wanted to confront on their **incessant** behavior" (ch 25) ("indifferent / unhurried")
   - "observing every passing vehicle with **irresolute** eyes" (ch 26) ("wary")
   - "The occasional goats and horses grazing the land down below was a **scintillating** view." (ch 26)
   - "a dark pair of glasses, which concealed any trickle of emotions from any **puerile** guessing." (ch 28)
   - "The creeping **eerie** in the van was rattled by the ringing tone" (ch 28) ("eeriness / silence")
   - "Looks like the entire team is here to find a girl", he mumbled, and a **vicious** smile crossed his lips." (ch 30) ("wry")
   - "draped in the shroud of silence they searched for their own **solicitude**." (ch 29) ("solitude")
   - "Stephen succeeded in **coercing** her to believe him." (ch 26) ("persuading")
2. **"Stature" for demeanour or posture** (3 times): "Her preoccupied stature was in deep perturbation" (ch 24); "With a calm stature, she responded" (ch 24); "his stature reflected uneasiness" (ch 25).
3. **"Blurted" as the default dialogue tag** (8 times), often in impossible combinations:
   - "Sanchez looked towards Stephen and **blurted in a subtle voice**" (ch 24) (an oxymoron)
   - "with an appealing tone she blurted" (ch 24)
   - "The chief officer blurted, 'We have some rules here.'" (ch 25)
   - "with a grating sound blurted" (ch 28)
   - Prefer "said" or no tag; keep "blurted" for genuine outbursts.
4. **Missing articles**, above all "few" for "a few" (about 30 instances): "After few minutes of pretense" (ch 24); "you got to wait for few more hours" (ch 24); "He ran his eyes over the place few more times" (ch 25); "for good twenty minutes" (ch 24). This is a mechanical find-and-fix for the line editor.
5. **Homophones, typos and malapropisms**: "as it was **taxing** on the tarmac" (ch 24) (taxiing); "they **dawned** a different look" (ch 24) (donned); "a **Bonnie** hat" (ch 24) (boonie); "**disbelieve**" [ch 25, ch 28]; "in a **discrete** … manner" (ch 25) (discreet); "**last ditched** attempt" (ch 27); "the occasional **creeks** of the seagulls" (ch 26) (cries/shrieks; *creaks* is also wrong); "stucco **complimented** well" (ch 29); "laid back on the seat" (ch 28) (lay); "go **pass** the house" (ch 30); "Vale da **Amoreria**" (ch 30); "for a" (ch 26) (fora); "**To passersby's**" (ch 24); "put a deaf ear" (ch 24) (turned).
6. **Missing possessives**: "Sanchez incisive eyes" (ch 24); "Maxwell annoyance" (ch 25); "Benedikt rage" (ch 28); "Francis speaking rights" (ch 28); "Jules house" / "Jules car's" (ch 29); "the flight attendant voice" (ch 24).
7. **Emotion labelled, not dramatised**, usually as a stacked adjective: "He was perplexed, agitated and scared, all at the same time." (ch 26); "Maxwell was flabbergasted." (ch 25); "He was bewildered." (ch 25); "Francis was enraged." (ch 28); "With every passing minute, he was morphing into a disgruntled being." (ch 28).
8. **Speechy, stiff dialogue**, especially villains and arguments:
   - "Shame on you and you know what, I am better off without you useless baboons. You all are a bunch of inept people I have as a part of my defunct team." (ch 28)
   - "It is a big distraction and a nuisance." (ch 24)
   - "My opinion, we need to find a way to travel without disclosing our identity" (ch 24)
   - The ideas are right, but the rhythm is written rather than spoken.
9. **Guidebook paragraphs**, inserted in the narrator's own voice: seven skirts "mostly calling out the seven days, seven colors of the rainbow or the biblical seven waves" (ch 24); "Though the building was rebuilt in the late 18th century, it still held the architectural and artistic significance of the 17th-century gothic looks." (ch 24); "Fado express sadness, but the version performed by the band hardly reflected any glumness." (ch 26). Where the facts are wrong these do double damage.
10. **Over-choreographed physical action** that obscures rather than tightens. The backpack-strap sequence under the van: "While keeping the leg looped around the exhaust he let go one of his hand as he slowly tried to get one of the straps of backpack slid down. The strap on the loose hand came out with few tries. Half of the bag was touching the ground and the other half dangling on the other shoulder." (ch 30). ch 30 is a single 520-word paragraph; break it into beats.
11. **Stock and mixed images**: "row houses arranged like pearls on a string" (ch 24); "It appeared as if the boy was God sent" (ch 24); "making Francis jump out of his skin" (ch 28); "made Maxwell's heart skip few beats" (ch 30); "Sanchez saw a 6 feet tall **structure** approaching them" (ch 29) (a person called a "structure"); "his endowed Greek like appearance" (ch 29).
12. **Head-hopping within scenes**: Sanchez to Stephen in ch 24 and ch 24; Francis to Benedikt in ch 28 ("Benedikt came to complete attention"); Maxwell's POV reporting Benedikt's inner state in ch 30.
13. **Word echoes inside short spans**: "famished" 4 times across ch 24–26; "urgency" 6 times; "extreme" 7 times ("extreme precaution", "extreme urgency", "extreme caution", "at the extreme" twice); "soon" 23 times; "untoward" 4 times; "tribulation(s)" 3 times.

## 6. Strengths

- **The twin-POV airport escape (ch24/25) is a real thriller mechanism.** The reader watches Sanchez vanish into a restroom and come out as someone else, then watches the man who loves her tear the terminal apart looking for a woman he has walked past. The chief officer's line "they have cleared immigration about 20 minutes back" (ch 25) is a clean reversal. The best moment is Maxwell's grudging admiration: *"he knew Sanchez had outsmarted not only Benedikt's goons but him as well"* (ch 25). Protect this structure; just move it off the immigration desk.
- **Consistent cross-POV detail.** Francis is "corpulent" to Sanchez (ch 24), "the stout person wearing a checked shirt" in her dialogue (ch 24) and "visibly obese" to Maxwell (ch 25). Benedikt "throwing his hands in the air" is seen by Maxwell (ch 30) exactly as Francis experiences it (ch 28). The author is tracking the choreography across viewpoints, which many debut thrillers fail to do.
- **Characters learn.** Stephen works out that they are being traced through tickets bought in their own names (ch 24), and in the next scene they pay cash to an unlicensed driver. Francis's men are then shown "scanning information of all travel tickets bought in the past hour" (ch 28), a quiet payoff the author may not have fully realised. Make it explicit.
- **A competent antagonist.** Benedikt deduces the disguise from nothing: *"They must be in disguise. I am positive that's how they escaped you. Tell your men to look under the skin."* (ch 28). The "bait" line (ch 28) and the unexplained call ("Give him some time. I am sure soon you will have some good news" (ch 28)) give the section its best forward pull.
- **Henchmen with humanity.** Damian consoling Francis (ch 28) ("this girl had deceived all of us, including the chief … I know chief is angry now, but he will come around") is a nice, unexpected grace note that makes the villains' camp feel like a workplace.
- **The sibling comedy.** The food-deprivation gag, used sparingly, humanises them. *"You are an ingenuous soul."* (ch 24) is exactly right, since *ingenuous* (naive) is the correct word, deliberately teasing. Keep "Stephen lost the battle and with dropped shoulders followed Sanchez, disgruntled and hungry." (ch 24).
- **The bench scene** is the section's best quiet writing, and the voice should be preserved here: *"He was instantly drawn to the quietude that resided in the emptiness of the night. Unlike the streets, which were swarming with tourists, there was no visible soul near the water."* and *"The talks receded, and they kept to themselves as if agreeing to an unstated protocol to stay by the ocean for as long as time would permit."* (ch 26). Also *"Stephen, come sit beside me. This place is so pristine, so beautiful. You need to experience this."* (ch 26).
- **Jules arrives with energy.** He is loud, warm and funny: *"Irmã that is because of Stephen's influence. When you meet, smart people like me ideas start to flow"* (ch 29). After 30 chapters of paranoia, a genuinely likeable ally is welcome.
- **Night-drive atmosphere**: *"the occasional farmhouses appearing far off from the road confirmed by the distinct dim light, which stood out in the openness."* (ch 29) and *"they weren't disturbed by any nightmares, they were too tired to accommodate any dreams."* (ch 29).
- **Accurate local choices**: Valado dos Frades as Jules's village; the A12 across the Vasco da Gama bridge as the route to Montijo; the *sete saias* as a Nazaré emblem; Vale da Amoreira's tower blocks as a gritty south-bank safehouse. The author has done research; it needs aligning, not replacing.
- **The van set piece has the right bones**: hiding, the house door opening, feet at eye level, documents falling, the keys left in the lock. Rearranged so the keys are discovered *during* the danger, it could be the section's high point.
- **Dramatic irony in ch 30**: Maxwell is puzzled that Benedikt is resting "when Sanchez was still on the loose", while the reader has just heard about "the bait". Good use of the multi-POV structure.

## 7. Honest assessment

This section has the skeleton of a solid middle-book movement. The heroes slip the net, the antagonist lands angry and then goes quietly calm, and the shadow-protector plants a bug. The two set pieces (the dual-POV airport and the van) show real plotting instinct, and the cast gets two welcome new colours in Jules and a humanised Francis/Damian. But the section is undermined by three things an acquisitions reader will notice within pages:
- **A clock that contradicts itself.** Three different times are given for one jet landing, dusk falls at 5 PM in April, and the chapters run backwards (8:30 PM before 4:30 PM).
- **Logistics that don't survive a Lisbon map or a Schengen passport.** There is no immigration from Switzerland, no metro "City Hall", Alcobaça has no ocean, Montijo is an air base, and Jules pays in pesos.
- **About 3,500 words of travelogue in which nothing threatens the protagonists.**
Line by line, the prose has the book-wide habits in concentrated form (thesaurus substitutions, "blurted", "few" without its article, guidebook asides), but the voice carries warmth and some lovely quiet moments that must be kept.

**The single most important fix:** rebuild 17 April on one master clock and one accurate map, and re-sequence the chapters to match. Concretely:
- Land at ~10:30.
- Replace "immigration" with baggage hall and airport-police CCTV. This also gives Maxwell the disguise reveal.
- Merge ch27/28 at Montijo, then ch30.
- Put the combined Nazaré evening (ch26+29, trimmed by ~40%) last, so the day closes on the siblings asleep, oblivious to the bug and the bait.
The factual corrections (O Sítio for Alcobaça, Oriente or Sete Rios for City Hall, euros, fado, Montijo as a military base) all slot into that rebuild. It would turn a section that currently reads as loose and error-prone into a tight, cross-cut sequence whose ironies (the restroom Maxwell never checks, the ticket trail, the bait) actually land.
