## 1. Chapter-by-chapter summary

### Chapter 71 — "3:00 PM 26th April 2014, Gdynia, Poland" (ch 71)
- **POV:** Sanchez (ch 71). Slides into Stephen (ch 71), then Maxwell (ch 71).
- **Setting:** Gdynia port and ship. Then the drive to Warsaw, Warsaw Old Town, and a coffee shop near the hotel.
- **Beats:**
  - During an inspection tour with Benedikt she spots a torn crate of "gold-plated statutes". They look like the remains of a 2nd-century-BC Vietnamese temple, which she remembers from school in Ayr. She photographs them with a body camera, uploads the photos to an online account and wipes the camera (ch 71).
  - Unloading ends. She spends a sleepless night dreading the arrival in "Viciebsk" (ch 71).
  - They drive to Warsaw. Benedikt has meetings, so she goes sightseeing ch 71.
  - She phones Stephen. He has been watching the hotel entrance and never saw her leave (ch 71).

  - Maxwell gives his word. He privately resolves to act "like a professional" (ch 71).
- **Reader learns:** what TransPacific is smuggling (antiquities), and that the evidence is now with the police.
- **Hook:** Maxwell's uneasy promise, a quiet plant for his betrayal in Ch 81/83.

### Chapter 72 — "8:00 AM 27th April 2014, Gdynia, Poland" (ch 72)
- **POV:** Benedikt (ch 72), then Sanchez (ch 72). The switch happens mid-sequence.
- **Setting:** Rain "in Gydnia", although the text says the Warsaw stay is already over. Then a hotel breakfast and Benedikt's private plane to Belarus.

### B. Geography and real-world facts

1. **"Viciebsk" and "Mova" (ch 73).** *Viciebsk* is a legitimate Belarusian romanisation, but an English-language thriller should use *Vitebsk* unless the author deliberately wants Belarusian forms throughout. "Mova" is just the Belarusian word for "language". Write "they spoke Belarusian or Russian". In practice Vitebsk warehouse workers in 2014 would overwhelmingly speak Russian.
2. **"500 Rubles" (ch 74).** In 2014 Belarus used the Belarusian ruble at about 10,000 to the US dollar, so 500 BYR was about five cents. "What he earns in a good week, he earned it in a day" is impossible. Use "a twenty-euro note" or "fifty dollars". Visitors would carry hard currency, and it gives the tip real weight.
3. **"The place was strategically located at the border of Belarus" (ch 78).** Smolensk city is about 70 km from the Belarus border. Either say "Smolensk region" and put the compound near the border town of Rudnya, which also fixes the one-hour drive, or keep the city and correct the distances.
4. **"European subcontinent" (ch 78).** Wrong term. Use "the rest of Europe".
5. **Liozna (ch 81)** is a small town in Vitebsk Oblast on the Vitebsk–Smolensk road, about 40 km east of Vitebsk and 15–20 km short of the Russian frontier. "At the border" is loose but defensible: "a small town a few miles short of the Russian border".
6. **The Belarus–Russia border in 2014.** Under the Union State there were *no* passport or customs posts on this road border, though third-country nationals (British Sanchez, Stephen, Daniel, Maxwell) were not legally permitted to cross it. So:
   - "Stephen wait at the border" (ch 78), "walk across the street" (ch 85), "the Liozona Border" (ch 85) and "far into the Russian side of the border" (ch 84) suggest a physical checkpoint that did not exist.
   - This is an opportunity: an invisible, unguarded border with a road sign and a forest is eerier and more accurate. One sentence would do ("There was no barrier, no guard, only a blue sign and the same birches on either side").
7. **Visas.** British citizens needed visas for both Belarus and Russia in 2014. Benedikt could have arranged Sanchez's (a line would do). But Daniel, held for years with no passport, is now in Belarus illegally. A line about Maxwell and the British Embassy in Minsk would close that gap.
8. **Maxwell's armed arrest team in Belarus [ch 81, ch 83].** This is the biggest factual and legal problem in the section. A Scotland Yard officer has no power of arrest in Belarus, cannot carry firearms there, and could not quickly assemble six armed men there. Lukashenko's Belarus in 2014 would not help a British operation.
   - "Scotland Yard" is itself odd for an Ayr-based case, which would belong to Strathclyde Police or, after April 2013, Police Scotland. That is outside S09, but flag it for the global pass.
   - Fixes: (a) make the men Belarusian officers acting on an Interpol Red Notice that Maxwell obtained with Sanchez's evidence. This also explains why Benedikt knows he is safe "once he is in Russia" (ch 83). Or (b) make them private hires, which darkens Maxwell and fits his obsession.
   - Either way, one explanatory sentence is essential.
9. **Antiquities (ch 71).** "Gold-plated statutes… residuals of second century BC temple from Vietnam". Vietnamese antiquities trafficking is real (Óc Eo and Champa material). "Second century BC" is possible for Dong Son–era bronzes, but gold-plated temple statuary from that date is dubious. Suggest "Cham temple statuary" or "Óc Eo-era gilt figures". Also, "Something she had learned about during her school days in Ayr" is thin: she left school only about two years earlier. Say "a documentary she had once watched", or cut the attribution. And why would Vietnamese antiquities come via the Baltic to Belarus? Give it one line (they arrived via Rotterdam and are bound for Moscow collectors).
10. **1 May** is a public holiday (Labour Day) in both Belarus and Russia, and April/May 2014 is the height of the Crimea crisis. Neither is required, but either would give free texture.

### C. Character and plot logic

1. **Daniel's backstory is never explained. This is the book's central mystery.**
   - Ch 1 [book line 11–13]: Daniel vanishes on a Sunday bakery run in Girbin, February 2010, watched by a waiting black Range Rover. Ch 49: he was "a contract deliveryman".
   - Part II: John Patterson says there was a *package* whose instructions came "from Evan Slender".
   - Ch 67: Finch says Daniel "used to work on this ship" and moved to Vitebsk with Albert.
   - Ch 75: Albert says "They took him away… He is in Russia." Ch 76: Benedikt says "I was there on the day your father was captivated".
   - Nobody ever explains:
     - Where Daniel was taken (Girbin? the ship? Vitebsk?).
     - How a Scottish van driver came to be working on a Baltic freighter and living in Belarus.
     - What was in the package.
     - What the "evidences" are that kept him alive for four years [ch 75, ch 76, ch 85].
     - Why the police officer MacCallan went cold.
   - The ending needs a scene where Daniel, or Benedikt, or Albert, tells this story. The obvious place is Ch 75 (Albert) or Ch 85 (Daniel in the car).
2. **Daniel never reacts to Benedikt.** Benedikt was present at Daniel's capture [ch 76, ch 84], yet Daniel spends a car journey with him in Ch 85 and neither man speaks. This is the book's richest unwritten scene: the captive recognising the man who asked for him to be killed.
3. **Benedikt's confession contradicts itself** [ch 76]. "I had nothing to do with his disappearance" is followed three lines later by "I told the Russians to get rid of him, but trust me, nothing untoward happened." The second sentence is morally enormous and is waved away. Sanchez's overnight forgiveness (Ch 77) is never dramatised: ch 77 gives us "Everything appeared clear" without telling us *what* became clear. **Fix:** add a paragraph where she reasons it through. For example: he was a junior man then; he has since risked everything; or she decides to use him and trust later. Otherwise readers will not accept the final "Yes us".
4. **Sanchez abandons her just-freed father** ch 85. After four years of searching, she sends her emaciated father to walk alone round a bend toward a brother she is furious with, and drives off with the man who once asked for him to be killed. "We will all live together like we used to before" (ch 85) is contradicted in her next speech.
   - Her stated motive (the Russians will hunt Benedikt (ch 85)) is sound, but it needs to be felt as a sacrifice, with a beat of real cost.
   - Daniel's acceptance ("I am sure you have a reason" (ch 85)) is implausibly passive.
5. **Kosovo's tail is a Chekhov's gun that never fires.** Kosovo orders his men to "discretely follow Benedikt" (ch 85), and Evan promises to "closely monitor" him. Benedikt drives Daniel to the border "without any untoward incident" (ch 85), and Sanchez ends with "before the Russians come looking for us" (ch 85).
   - Either the tail must appear (the natural climax), or Benedikt must spot and shake it on the page.
   - As written, the Russians, Evan and TransPacific all escape any on-page consequence.
6. **Evan's decision makes no sense unless it is a trap** (ch 85). He suspects Benedikt ("if found guilty, he would not be spared") yet persuades Kosovo to release Daniel. That only works if Evan means to catch Benedikt in the act. Then *pay it off*, or cut the suspicion.
7. **Sanchez blames the wrong person.** ch 81 and ch 83 say Stephen was "too naive" and "unaware of his companion's ulterior motives". Yet ch 83 frames it as "her brother whom she trusted with her life, betraying her belief", ch 83 calls Stephen "ashamed", and ch 85 says she "couldn't believe Stephen would hide such a thing from him [her]".
   - Either make Stephen complicit (he knew and said nothing), or turn her anger on Maxwell, who broke his explicit promise (ch 71).
   - She should throw that promise back at Maxwell in Ch 83. He gave his word in Ch 71 and the payoff is missing.
8. **Maxwell's collaboration in Ch 85 is unexplained.** The day after Benedikt held a gun to Sanchez's head in front of him and drove off with his team's weapons, Maxwell has "arranged all the necessary protection" for the handover (ch 85) and makes no second arrest attempt. One line is needed: Sanchez told him the truth by phone, or struck a deal.
9. **The guns ch 83.** "Stephen, get all the guns and put it in the car trunk". Which car? If it is Benedikt's, he drives into Russia with a boot full of police firearms, and nobody mentions it again. If it is Maxwell's car, the men can simply walk back and re-arm. Also, "Maxwell tried hard to chase him but failed" (ch 83): chased how, on foot and unarmed? And the headcount is muddled: "Six armed men approached them, three from each side of the car and Maxwell from the front" (ch 83).
10. **The pistol (ch 83).** "the one that Benedikt gave her before leaving for Smolensk" is never shown being given. Plant it in Ch 79 or Ch 80. It would also show his trust in her, and so matters to the romance.
11. **How did Benedikt know about Noŭka?** (ch 76) It is a great reveal line, but the mechanism is never given (the cab driver, the room-service boy, or one of his men). Add one line in ch 76: "The driver you hired works for the hotel. The hotel works for us."
12. **Who handed her the address?** (ch 74) The "fragile looking frame" is never identified. Albert asks "How did you find me?" (ch 75), she promises "I will give you all the details" and never does. Let Albert's answer identify the messenger (his nephew, or a man Daniel once helped) to close the loop.
13. **"His friend in Gdynia" (ch 74).** Finch is on the ship (met at Ventspils, Ch 67–68). It should be "from his old friend Finch, on the ship".
14. **Anatoly and Kosovo both "head of operations"** [ch 78 vs ch 82]. Make Anatoly "the facility's manager" and Kosovo "the regional boss".
15. **"Sanchez's father was not in any confinement" (ch 79)** is contradicted within the same paragraph ("still in their internment") and by Albert (ch 75). The intended meaning was probably "Finding Sanchez's father had been easier than he feared."
16. **Ch 78 ch 78 describes the border escape plan before Benedikt knows Daniel is there.** Move that paragraph to Ch 79, where "He explained the plan to her" (ch 79) is currently a one-line summary. That will also fatten the stub chapter.
17. **Benedikt's scheme is flimsy by design, but he should see the flaw earlier.** At ch 80 he realises Anatoly's bosses will call his bosses, which is exactly what any competent operator would have predicted. Have him anticipate it and gamble anyway ("He had counted on the Russians' pride, and on Evan never answering his phone before noon").
18. **Warsaw surveillance (ch 71).** Stephen is watching the hotel and she walks past him. It is fine as comedy, but how did Stephen and Maxwell know the Warsaw hotel? Via Maxwell's port contact (Ch 70)? One clause would do.
19. **The unnamed caller in Ch 84 (ch 84).** Name Evan so the reader connects him to Part II's Evan Slender. "Worse, you took my name?" only lands if we know whose name it is.
20. **The last line undercuts the ending (ch 85).** "He was hopeful the tides would turn and soon she would accept his love." She accepted it three lines earlier ("Yes us"). Cut, or rewrite as his disbelief that she has.
21. **Loose ends at The End:**
    - What exactly Daniel knew, and the package.
    - Evan Slender's fate.
    - TransPacific's takedown (asserted only (ch 85)).
    - Kosovo, Anatoly and the tail.
    - Maxwell's heartbreak and his professional reckoning.
    - Stephen's reunion with Daniel (offstage).
    - **Mother** (never mentioned in the reunion, though Ch 53 establishes she is alive).
    - John Patterson and MacCallan.
    - Albert and Finch.
    - The map, the book's original MacGuffin: not mentioned in S09 at all.
    - Benedikt's legal status (wanted for a hostage-taking in front of a police officer).
    - Sanchez's gunshot wound.

    A thriller may leave the lovers on the run, but it must close the *mystery* and at least one villain's arc.

## 4. Pacing and flow

**Overall shape.** The section has three movements:
1. Poland: the evidence handover and the love letter (Ch 71–72).
2. Vitebsk: the Albert hunt, the confrontation and the reconciliation (Ch 73–77).
3. Smolensk and the border: the two visits, the ambush, the release (Ch 78–85).

Movement 2 is the best-paced run in the section. Movement 3 has the section's best idea (the hostage ruse), but then puts the real rescue into two summary sentences.

**Where it drags:**
- **ch 71, Warsaw sightseeing.** Travelogue with no plot function ("From the sprawled gothic churches and the neoclassical palaces to the modern skyscrapers…"). Compress to one sentence, or use it: she notices she is being watched, or the rebuilt Old Town mirrors her own reconstructed life.
- **ch 71, sleepless night, then waking, cold shower and smooth drive.** A routine beat this series repeats at ch 74, ch 77 and ch 79. Cut to a line each.
- **Ch 73 and Ch 74 are the same beat twice:** she asks workers about Albert, they walk away. Merge them into one chapter. Keep the internet Russian study, the notebook, the old man and the note, and cut the first fruitless evening visit.
- **ch 83 repeats ch 81.** We are told Maxwell's arrest plan in Ch 81 and then told it again from inside his head in Ch 83.
- **Ch 80's car dialogue ch 80** loops through "Don't you see the ruse?" and "Maybe you are doubting unnecessarily" three times. Tighten by a third.

**Where it rushes:**
- **ch 71, the antiquities discovery.** This is the moment the book finally tells us *what* TransPacific traffics, and it is reported in retrospect in one paragraph. Dramatise it as a half-page scene in Ch 70 or 71: the ripped wrapper, the gilt face in torchlight, Benedikt's voice behind her.
- **Ch 75, Albert.** 489 words for the only witness to Daniel's life in the east. Albert should get a full scene: what Daniel did on the ship, why they moved to Vitebsk, the night he was taken, what he was carrying. This is where Chapter 1 and Part II should be paid off.
- **Ch 77, the reconciliation.** She moves from "vengeance" to "I want to trust you one last time" after a jog. The turn needs a reason on the page.
- **Ch 79.** 237 words, including "He explained the plan to her." Merge it into Ch 78 and show the plan.
- **Ch 85 (ch 85), the rescue itself.** "Daniel was brought to the room… and to Benedikt's disbelief, he left the place with Daniel unscathed." Four years of searching resolve in one sentence, offstage, from the least invested POV. This is the section's single biggest pacing failure.
- **Ch 85 ch 85.** The key villain decisions (Evan's reversal, Kosovo's tail) are told in summary *after* the fact. Dramatise them in Ch 82 or a new short chapter.

**Scene order:**
- Ch 82 (Kosovo, 10 AM) sits after Ch 81 (Liozna, 12 PM). Place it straight after Ch 80's office scene, or fold it into Ch 80 as a short POV break.
- Ch 81's reveal of Maxwell's ambush (ch 81) kills the surprise of Ch 83. Two options:
  - Suspense: keep the reveal, but then Ch 83 must play the dramatic irony, cutting between the approaching car and Maxwell's men.
  - Surprise: cut ch 81's final three sentences so the guns cocking in ch 83 shock the reader as much as Sanchez.
  - I recommend surprise.

**Merge, cut and move:**
- Merge 73+74, 78+79 and 81+82 (or reorder).
- Cut the Warsaw travelogue.
- Move the antiquities discovery into scene.
- Consider building a new chapter between 84 and 85: the night in Russia, where Sanchez and Benedikt finally talk about what he did in 2010. It is currently skipped, and it is where the romance would be earned.

**The climax (Ch 83).** Structurally this is the emotional climax, and the idea is excellent:
- Sanchez chooses Benedikt over her brother and the law.
- She turns her own history into a weapon ("His men had shot me once").
- Benedikt says "I will always love you" believing she has betrayed him.

It reverses her rejection on the plane in Ch 72 cleanly. But it has two faults:
- (a) It is staged against the book's *good* guys. The antagonists (Evan, Kosovo) never face a climax.
- (b) The legal and logistical impossibility of Maxwell's team (§3 B8) undermines it.

It needs more beats: a close-up of Stephen's face as he loads the guns, and one line from Sanchez to Maxwell about his broken word.

**The final chapter (Ch 85): satisfying and earned?** No, not yet.
- The rescue is offstage.
- The tail is dropped.
- The father–daughter reunion gets a single paragraph and two lines of dialogue, and then she leaves him.
- The mystery of *why* Daniel was taken is never solved.
- The mother is forgotten.
- The romance resolves ("Yes us") without Sanchez or Daniel ever confronting Benedikt's order to "get rid of him".
- The last paragraph ("They had a few hurdles to cross, some barriers to dodge and many places to visit before they could repose") reads like a sequel teaser rather than an ending.

There is one real, and probably unconscious, structural rhyme worth building on:
- In Ch 1 the young Sanchez watches from the window "waiting for her father to dissolve in the morning mist". He never comes back.
- In Ch 85 she watches him disappear "along the road as it bent away from her vision" (ch 85), this time toward safety.

That is the book's natural last image. **Make it deliberate:**
- Have Daniel call her "San".
- Have a bakery, a muffin, or the window game come up in the reunion.
- End close to that image rather than on Benedikt's hope.

## 5. Prose problems (section-specific)

These are specific to S09. The general style report covers the global issues.

**a) Malapropisms and vocabulary misfires.** This is the biggest reader-trust problem, and it clusters at emotional peaks, where it does most damage:
- "the day your father was **captivated**" (ch 76) (captured). This is the moral climax of Benedikt's confession and it reads as a pun.
- "She woke up a tad **crapulent**" (ch 71). Crapulent means hungover from over-indulgence.
- "without **inhabitation**" [ch 72, ch 85] (inhibition). It also appears in Ch 53, so it is a recurring error.
- "gold-plated **statutes**… the **residuals** of second century BC temple… cleared every **scathe** of its existence" (ch 71) (statues / remains / trace).
- "the Russians were putting a **coy**" (ch 80) (ploy); "put a **drab** on your years of strenuous efforts" (ch 80) (damper); "she didn't want to **gutter** it down" (ch 80).
- "to hold on to her **schmaltz**" (ch 79); "your **runic** spell" (ch 80); "**restless and jejune**" (ch 76); "his **colloquial** tone" (ch 77); "a sense of **possessiveness**" (ch 77); "this **abstruse** person" (ch 85); "**absolute desirousness**" (ch 81); "Her **palpations** were dissolved" (ch 80).
- "His smile appeared more like **Russian Mores**" (ch 80). The meaning is unclear. Did the author intend "a Russian mask"?
- "**cobble-webbed** streets" (ch 71); "the **long-stressed** road" (ch 83) (long, straight road); "**5'0 Clock green** shadow" (ch 72); "a **settlement** in Smolensk" (ch 75) (compound).

**b) "Stature", "countenance" and "visage" used for mood, posture or face:**
- "his confused **stature**" (ch 77)
- "a more puzzled **stature**" (ch 80)
- "raised her view to get a glimpse of his **stature**" (ch 76)
- "with a poised **stature**" (ch 75)
- "the guise of getting betrayed loomed his **visage**" (ch 83)

**c) Stacked dialogue tags with adverbial tone phrases.** "tone" appears 20 times and "voice" 22 times in under 10,000 words:
- "Maxwell explained with a somewhat troubled tone" (ch 71)
- "hysteria consumed her being, as she blazoned the words with an asphyxiated tone" (ch 80)
- "her unwillingness to answer the question was visible in her asphyxiated voice" (ch 71)
- "There was elation, an unprecedented joy in his colloquial tone" (ch 77)
- "He cried with an undisputed ecstasy in that elated tone" (ch 85)

Most tags should become "said", or disappear.

**d) Recycled physical and emotional clichés within one section:**
- Lightning: "pierced Benedikt's heart like a lightning bolt" (ch 72); "like lightning had struck her and bolted her on the chair" (ch 76); "frozen as if lightning had struck him" (ch 85).
- "a shade below screaming" (ch 76) / "a shade short of screaming" (ch 80) / "a shade below pale" (ch 85).
- "sotto tone / sotto voice" [ch 73, ch 77]; "with utmost discreetness" [ch 74, ch 81]; "pretext" ×5; "brooding" ×4; "competing thoughts" [ch 80, ch 83].

**e) Garbled sentences with no recoverable meaning:**
- "Her aura was viciously mysterious and her temperament invariably impulsive, if at all he had a peek into her mind." (ch 73)
- "Those words refuted the scariness she was undermining with." (ch 76)
- "The way his eyes rolled on him, it failed to abstain his wide grin." (ch 80)
- "His eyes were hollow, fixated firmly on his pale gloomy face." (ch 76) (his eyes fixed on his own face)
- "Maxwell wore a cunning smirk as if he had won the battle of some celestial titans." (ch 83)

**f) Mechanical errors:**
- Missing possessive 's, which recurs: "Benedikt voice" (ch 76), "Sanchez insistent" (ch 78), "Sanchez curiosity" (ch 78), "Benedikt countenance" (ch 82), "Kosovo tapered green eyes" (ch 82), "Stephen uneasiness" (ch 83), "Benedikt tone" (ch 83), "Sanchez annoyance" (ch 85), "Benedikt vigilance" (ch 85).
- "He passed a wry smiled" (ch 75); "Would you know where in Russia is, he?" (ch 75); "the evidences" ch 75; "go pass" (ch 71); "rationale argument" (ch 84); "hide such a thing from him" for "her" (ch 85).
- Dialogue punctuation: commas outside the quotation marks ("Viciebsk”, she mumbled" (ch 71)); a missing closing quote ("What was your guilt? Sanchez snapped." (ch 76)); "continued,” The evidences" (ch 75).
- ch 75 puts two speakers' lines in one paragraph ("And what? Please tell me." is Sanchez's).
- "Albertttt" (ch 73) is unintentionally comic at a tense moment.

**g) Head-hopping inside scenes:**
- ch 72 to ch 72: Benedikt's POV, then Sanchez's "She couldn't take her eyes off him".
- Ch 76 moves between Sanchez and Benedikt paragraph by paragraph (ch 76, ch 76).
- Ch 83 runs Maxwell, then Sanchez, then Benedikt, then Maxwell within two pages.

Omniscient narration is allowed, but here it flattens the key suspense beats. In Ch 83, stay in Sanchez's head from ch 83 to ch 83.

**h) Telling the emotion instead of the action at peak moments:**
- "Sanchez was overwhelmed. Her emotions had no boundaries." (ch 79)
- "Benedikt's joy had no boundaries" (ch 85)
- "Tears were flowing in abundance for all the right reasons. There were no words. All that was visible was teary eyes and smiling faces." (ch 85)

**i) The love letter ch 72.** Its sincerity works, but its diction wobbles ("no strings that will behold us"; "abolish this viewpoint"; "let the destiny lead us where it wants us to take"). Benedikt is a feared, articulate operator, and his letter could be shorter and more precise without losing its ardour.

## 6. Strengths

- **The Noŭka reveal** ch 76. "It doesn't appear you are sick anymore" / "Good, that must be a long stroll to Noŭka." This is a genuinely good thriller beat. Benedikt's power is shown through one precise, quiet sentence and a named place. Protect it exactly. (Just add how he knew.)
- **The hostage ruse** ch 83. This is the best set-piece idea in the ending. Sanchez's split-second choice ("Make me your hostage"), Benedikt's "I will always love you, no matter what happens" said while he believes she has betrayed him, and the cruelly effective lie built from a true history: "**His men had shot me once and I have no doubt he will shoot me again.**" It pays off her Portugal gunshot (Marty, Ch 40s), reverses the rejection on the plane (Ch 72), and shows Sanchez's resourcefulness. It is the book's best example of character driving plot.
- **The note she cannot read** (ch 74). "Though she couldn't read it she knew it was an address." A small, true observation that makes the language barrier concrete.
- **The internet-Russian notebook** [ch 74, ch 75]. She reads phonetically from a pad and people turn away. It is realistic, a little humiliating, and in character.
- **Dramatic irony of Kosovo's absence** [ch 80 vs ch 82]. Anatoly says "Mr. Kosovo is not here until late in the evening", and the next chapter shows Kosovo at his desk giving orders. The two-chapter structure works here.
- **Cause and consequence with Slender's name** [ch 80, ch 84]. Benedikt invokes "Mr. Slender" as leverage, and it comes back as the angry phone call: "Worse, you took my name?" This is tight plotting.
- **Albert's first line** (ch 75). "Are you Sanchez? He used to talk about you all the time." It is the most moving sentence in the section, and it earns tears without asking for them.
- **Daniel's physical return** (ch 85). "He looked wearily thin. The clothes he was wearing were larger than his frame… One thing that didn't fail was his smile that was glued to her memories for all these years." The oversized clothes are exactly the right concrete detail.
- **The disappearing-father rhyme** (ch 85). "slowly Daniel walked away, disappearing along the road as it bent away from her vision" mirrors Ch 1's "waiting for her father to dissolve in the morning mist". It is the book's best structural image. Make it deliberate (§4).
- **The mirage** (ch 83). "the car appeared as if it was floating on a body of water". A good cinematic image for the moment before the ambush. Only the season ("summer sun") is wrong.
- **Benedikt as "a dethroned king"** (ch 76). "He appeared like a dethroned king, lost and dismayed, aloof and confused." Strong and economical.
- **Warsaw's "resurrection"** (ch 71). "the town reflected the time-tested turbulent history and its resurrection" is historically apt (the rebuilt Old Town). If the travelogue is cut, keep this sentence.
- **Self-aware interiority** (ch 72). "Till then this was the only way to keep Benedikt at bay or maybe this was her pretentious belief." The narration doubts its heroine, which is a nice touch of irony.
- **The letter device** ch 72. A man who commands armed men hands over a folded note and flees to the back of his own plane. It is a lovely, humanising contradiction, and the scene idea is strong even if the letter's prose needs trimming.
- **The final chapter end hook of Ch 78** (ch 78). "I saw your father and I have a plan for his escape." It is clean, propulsive, and the right length.

## 7. Honest assessment

The ending contains the raw material of a satisfying close: the Noŭka reveal, the confession, the two tense visits to a Russian compound, a brilliantly reversible hostage bluff, and a reunion image that rhymes with the first chapter. But as drafted it resolves the romance and skips the thriller. The book's engine was set up in Chapter 1 and Part II: a father taken from a Scottish village, a package ordered by Evan Slender, a cover-up by the local police, a route map, a Russian end-point. None of it is explained. Albert, the one witness, gives three sentences. The rescue happens offstage in one line. The villains (Evan, Kosovo, Anatoly) suffer nothing on the page. Kosovo's tail is ordered and forgotten. The climax is fought against the heroine's own brother and the police rather than against the antagonists.

The datelines in Ch 78–85 are also badly scrambled: two weeks pass in an hour, "the next day" is the same day, and a Belarus scene is headed "Somlensk, Russia". Maxwell's armed team operating in Lukashenko's Belarus will not survive an informed reader. And the romance's final "Yes us" asks the reader to forgive Benedikt's "I told the Russians to get rid of him" without Sanchez, or Daniel, ever reckoning with it. An acquiring editor would be charmed by the hostage scene and deflated by the last two chapters.

**The single most important fix:** rebuild Chapter 85 (with Albert's scene in Ch 75 as its setup) into an on-page climax and revelation. Dramatise the extraction from the compound. Let Kosovo's tail actually close in at the Liozna border. Let Daniel recognise Benedikt and, in the car, finally tell what he saw and carried in 2010 and why he was kept alive. Have Sanchez's choice to leave with Benedikt cost her something visible. End on the father-walking-away image that rhymes with Chapter 1. Everything else in this section is line-editing and dateline repair. This fix decides whether the book *ends* or merely stops.
