# THE TELL — Story Bible

*Single source of truth for the structural revision. Every chapter writer follows this document. Where the author's text (rev/src/ch00–ch85) conflicts with it, **the bible wins**; the conflict is listed so the writer knows what to change. Chapter references: "ch 12" = author's original chapter; "N12" = new chapter in the 60-chapter revised order (Structure Plan §2).*

**Title:** *The Tell* (replaces *The Last Scintilla*). Three meanings, planted lightly and never explained in the text:
1. the poker tell: Bradley in N13, "Every player at that table has a tell." Sanchez reads Alberto's in N2 and finds none on Benedikt;
2. Benedikt's tell: his face at the photograph of her father (N36), the first crack in the famous poker face;
3. telling: Daniel "never told them" for four years, then tells Sanchez in the car (N59), and Benedikt tells her everything on the night in Russia (N57).

The word "tell" appears in narration no more than three times in the whole book. The title is never mentioned in the text.

**Binding editor-in-chief decisions** (built in throughout): Sanchez is 16, 18 and 20. The evidence is under the windowsill board. Benedikt is German-born and raised in Denmark, and he chose her first. Maxwell is NCA, off the books, with no admissible evidence. His superior is Drake, and the Belarusian major comes from Drake. The Smolensk wait is **one day** (2–4 May 2014). The author's plot, cast and set pieces stay.

## 1. The true story, in chronological order (2003 – 4 May 2014)

Organising facts: **TransPacific** is an Ayr-based logistics group (co-founded in the 1990s by Evan Slater and two partners). Its UK head office is a four-storey glass building on the Prestwick road, and it runs a bonded warehouse in the freight area at Prestwick Airport. Its legitimate business covers the whole of the regional and European freight trade. The **Northern Route** is its secret one: looted antiquities, mainly Cham temple statuary from central Vietnam, flown into Prestwick as "reproduction garden statuary". They are held in the bond, sent by feeder to Tallinn, carried on the company ship **DAZZLE** (Tallinn → Ventspils → Gdynia), then moved by road (Gdynia → Warsaw → the Vitebsk warehouse). From there they cross the uncontrolled Belarus–Russia road border to **Kozlov's** compound near Smolensk, and on to collectors in Moscow. The route is re-drawn every quarter on a coded map (coloured dots = weekday, small numbers = date). The map travels in Benedikt's briefcase. Each quarter's voyage has a code name; April 2014's is **Sub Rosa**. The annual private poker game at Helsingør is where the route's backers "settle the quarter": they win and lose to one another to move money. That is why a security chief sits at a £10 million table playing TransPacific's stake.

### 2003–2009: before
- **2003–08.** Rui Matos, a freelance Portuguese cartographer and Adriana Lobo's partner, designs the route-map cipher for TransPacific and visits Ayr head office several times a year. **John Patterson**, a TransPacific freight driver and dispatcher in Ayr, drives him about, and the two become fishing friends.
- **Good Friday, 10 April 2009.** Rui has seen what the maps carry and tells TransPacific he will stop. He is found at the foot of the cliffs at O Sítio, Nazaré. The death is ruled an accident. Adriana knows better and has lived alone with the maps and her fear ever since. John writes to her every Christmas.
- **Sept 2009.** Daniel Roberti (46), a self-employed contract deliveryman, starts regular runs for TransPacific through a driver agency ("Carrick Couriers"). He is **never on TransPacific's staff lists**. John dispatches him. Daniel tells his family nothing about any single client. At home "work" is simply "the agency".
- **Jan 2010.** TransPacific hires **Benedikt Voss** (27) as a junior security officer on the Northern Route under the then-head, **Carsten Mørch** (Danish, fifties). Benedikt's army friend **Damian Holm** joins with him.

### February 2010: the package
- **Tue 2 Feb 2010.** On a direct instruction from Evan Slater's office, John hands Daniel a sealed, wrapped wooden crate and a waybill signed "E. Slater" for delivery to **Bay 7** of the Prestwick bond ("garden statuary, reproduction — for consolidation, Tallinn"). A contractor is used so that no staff record exists. On the A77 the crate slides and its wrapping splits. Daniel sees a gilded face, a dancing woman crowned with a little temple, plainly old and plainly real. At Bay 7 he finds a dozen more crates marked the same way. He photographs the statuette and the stacked crates with his compact camera. He hands the bond clerk the carbon and **keeps the top copy of the waybill**. His work diary (the notebook Sanchez later finds) reads: *"Tue 2 Feb — JP (TP) — sealed pkg, E.S. office — Bay 7 bond — 11.40."*
- **Thu 4 Feb.** Daniel goes to **Sergeant Iain MacCallum** at Girvan police office (Strathclyde Police), whom he knows from the village. He shows him the photographs on the camera's screen. MacCallum writes the statement **by hand** and tells him to bring prints. Diary: *"Thu 4 Feb — MacC. Statement."*
- **5–10 Feb.** MacCallum telephones TransPacific "to check the consignment". Evan's office learns the driver's name. Within days MacCallum, who has gambling debts, is paid and warned. The handwritten statement is never logged.
- **Fri 12 Feb.** MacCallum tells Daniel that "Customs looked; they're reproductions; leave it." Daniel knows he has been sold. That night he takes the memory card out of the camera, folds the waybill, puts both in the tobacco tin and hides it under the loose board of Sanchez's windowsill. Every winter he has promised to fix that board. Now he wedges it firm. He plans to take the tin to HM Revenue & Customs in Glasgow on **Monday 15 Feb**. Diary: *"Mon — Glasgow. HMRC."* He tells no one.
- **Sun 14 Feb 2010, about 8:00 AM.** The Sunday game: Daniel goes out for muffins. Sanchez (16) wipes the misted window with her sleeve and watches him "dissolve in the morning mist". The sill board, which has rocked under her elbows all winter, is firm for once. Isabel and Stephen are asleep. At about 8:05, outside the bakery, men from a **black Range Rover** with tinted glass take Daniel: Mørch, a driver, and one of Kozlov's Russians. **Benedikt sits in the back seat.** He does not touch Daniel, and he does nothing. The kirk choir, early for practice, sees "a man get into the car". The bakery bag is left on the pavement. In the car Daniel bluffs about the copy that will reach the police. They take his camera from his van and find it empty, so they believe him. His van is found burnt out on a forestry track near Dalmellington three days later.
- **Feb–Aug 2010.** The Robertis search for him. MacCallum leads the missing-person inquiry, at first energetically, then goes cold. He stops answering calls and is "callous" when confronted. He never mentions the statement. The police never ask about John Patterson.

### 2010–2013: Daniel's years in the east (told by Yuri in N51 and by Daniel in N59)
- **Feb–Apr 2010.** Daniel is held and questioned at a TransPacific property in the Baltic and then at Smolensk. He never tells. Evan decides he is worth more alive until the "copy" is found. Kozlov suggests he be put to work where he can harm no one: on the ship.
- **Apr 2010 – Sept 2012.** Daniel works as a prisoner-labourer on **DAZZLE**. He has no passport and is locked in at every port, and he is told his family will pay if he runs or talks. The bosun, **Finch** (English, from Hull), treats him decently and calls him "Dan the Scot". Daniel learns the whole route. He befriends **Yuri Avilov**, the ship's Belarusian cook, a free man.
- **Sept 2012.** Yuri quits the ship at Gdynia and rides the lorry convoy home to Vitebsk with Daniel hidden in the load. At the Vitebsk warehouse they slip away at night to Yuri's turquoise house in **Novka**, a village just west of Vitebsk. Kozlov tells Evan the Scot has run and will be found. Evan assumes he is dead, or soon will be.
- **Sept 2012 – Oct 2013.** Daniel hides in Novka and does cash farm work. He does not write home: he believes any letter would be traced, would bring TransPacific to his family's door and would tell them to search the house. He saves money to go to the **British Embassy in Minsk** (planned for November 2013).
- **Mid-2013.** Mørch retires (heart trouble). **Benedikt becomes head of security, Northern Route.** He has been the map's courier and custodian since 2012.
- **Oct 2013.** On his first tour as head, Benedikt stops with Kozlov's warehouse liaison at a roadside café on the Vitebsk ring road where the route's lorries stop. Daniel is unloading potatoes into the café kitchen. Benedikt knows the face from the back seat at Girbin, though he never knew the surname. Daniel knows his. Benedikt says to the Russian: "That man ran from the Dazzle. He should not be anywhere near my route. Get rid of him." He means *take him off my route*. He does not ask what they will do. That night the Russians take Daniel from the café yard in a car with **Smolensk (67) plates**. Benedikt's tour report to Ayr notes only "a former route worker found at Vitebsk, handed to the partners". Evan reads it as *dealt with*. Benedikt does not know whether Daniel is alive.
- **Oct 2013 – May 2014.** Daniel is held at **Kozlov's compound** in the forest about 30 km north-west of Smolensk. He packs crates and mends things, and he is not caged but watched. **Anatoly** (facility manager) likes him. **Kozlov** keeps him alive for two reasons: the copy has never been found, and a living Daniel is Kozlov's private insurance against Evan.

### The family, 2010–2014
- **Isabel Roberti** stays in Girbin, teaching and singing in the choir. **Stephen** (19 in 2010) gives up his place at Glasgow and works as a mechanic in Girvan to keep the house going.
- **Autumn 2010 (Sanchez, 16–17).** MacCallum has gone cold. Sanchez makes her three-point list. A memory of the migratory-birds geography project, when Dad drew his travel lines on her map and starred **Ayr**, sends her to his office bag. In it she finds **one notebook**, his work diary: addresses, John Patterson's name marked "(TP)", a page with a street map of Ayr, and the *"pkg"* entries she does not understand. There are also Ayr receipts. (There is only one notebook. The school map was only the memory that led her to it.)
- **Nov 2011 (18).** Having finished her Highers, she persuades Isabel to let her move to **Aunt Nina** (Nina Kerr, Daniel's widowed sister, a dressmaker in Newton-on-Ayr). She starts at Ayr College in January 2012 and by February has her own studio flat, paid for by evening jobs.

### 2011–2013: Ayr (Part Two)

Interview Mon 26 Mar, offer Mon 2 Apr. The post is a **part-time placement** (afternoons, alongside college) in customer service under **David Buchanan**, starting **Mon 16 Apr**. Disclosure Scotland check, not a drug test.

He has already looked her up and **knows her father is a missing contract driver who once drove for TransPacific. He uses it.** He enrols her in the gym, kickboxing, karate and climbing, and threatens to expose her.

At night at the back of an old harbour shed on the Ayr quay, Bradley times her up a drainpipe with a stopwatch and says nothing when she makes it. In the all-night café afterwards he takes **the corner seat facing the door**.

When she tells him she will need someone to read TransPacific's maps, he gives her **Adriana Lobo** in Nazaré, "Rui's Adriana", and agrees to ring Adriana when the time comes. He is the "gruff voice" she calls from Copenhagen.

### February–April 2014: the plan
- **Fri 14 Feb 2014.** Four years to the day. In a café on Ayr's Low Green, Bradley (corner seat, facing the door) gives her the target: **Benedikt Voss**; the private tournament at the **Øresund Palace Hotel, Helsingør, Monday 14 April**; the suite at the top of the new wing; two guards on the corridor; vodka with two olives; and the quarterly route map in a telescopic cylinder in his briefcase. **The honey trap is her idea.** Bradley is silent. She asks who he really is, and he won't say. He gives her money in an envelope. The escape beyond the hotel she keeps to herself: "That part is mine." He tells her every player at that table has a tell. That night she rings Stephen: "I need a driver in Switzerland." Stephen is the only one who knows the plan.
- **Mar 2014.** Two scouting stays at the Øresund Palace as "Rita", with a Copenhagen address, paying cash, always the same corner room two floors below the suite. Aunt Nina alters the black gown, lining the sleeves with hidden pockets, and asks no questions. Bradley resigns from TransPacific at the end of March ("a family matter in England").

At about 5:45 she rings John from a platform payphone ("I have the map. Can you ring Adriana and tell her I'll be there in the next few days?"), then rings back fifteen minutes later. She buys a ticket to **Basel** for cash. Maxwell buys the same by card as **"T. Harlow"**. Then comes a day of changes and a night train south from Hamburg.

The CCTV shows her waiting for him in the coffee shop. Cruz the taxi driver and the station CCTV complete the picture. His **passport contact** matches a CCTV still to the passport scans of passengers from UK flights into Copenhagen: **Sanchez Roberti, 20, British, of Ayr.** The DSB CCTV and a card payment give "Trevor". He reports the theft to his seniors (Evan) **as "Rita" only and keeps her real name to himself**. He is given until the end of the week.

Stephen loses him in a flats car park, but Maxwell watches from an all-night petrol-station café (corner seat, facing the door). He follows the BMW for the better part of an hour and a half to Walenstadt and fits a GPS tracker. A contact brings him a **grey Škoda estate**. That evening Stephen cuts Sanchez's waist-long hair to her shoulders with the kitchen scissors, for the disguise.

Oskar calls: she was an **intern in Evan Slater's own office in 2012**. ## 2. Cast sheet

**Voice rules for everyone** (from the dialogue style study; binding):
- **Contraction policy is the main way the voices differ.** Benedikt, the Russians and Yuri use no contractions (Benedikt keeps his rare existing "don't"). Sanchez is formal as Rita and as "Anna" the assistant. With Stephen, Maxwell, or in anger or fear she contracts about one time in two ("don't", "can't", "I'm"). Stephen is the most contracted voice ("Let's", "I'm"), with one "Yeah" in the book. Maxwell is formal when he confesses and contracted when he gives orders in the field. Damian, the Portuguese crew and Jules contract freely in short lines.
- "Is that clear?", "Will you?" and "Any questions?" belong to **Benedikt only**. Maxwell's marker is "Look," + an imperative.
- Scottish speakers say Mum, football, kirk, enquire. No "Mom", "soccer" or "you guys".
- No new slang, no pidgin, no broken English for foreigners, no "comrade". Russian characters speak careful, uncontracted, slightly over-correct English.
- Thoughts go in italics without quotation marks. Only words muttered aloud take quotation marks.
- Dialogue tags: default to *said/asked* or no tag. At most two of the author's colour verbs (*blurted, announced, stated, declared, queried*) per chapter.

## 6. Rules of the world (geography, logistics, law)

### Time zones (2014)
| Place | Zone | vs Lisbon/Ayr |
|---|---|---|
| Scotland (Ayr, Girbin, Troon) | BST, UTC+1 (from 30 Mar 2014) | same |
| Portugal | WEST, UTC+1 | same |
| Denmark, Switzerland, Germany, Poland | CEST, UTC+2 | +1 |
| Estonia, Latvia | EEST, UTC+3 | +2 |
| **Belarus** | UTC+3 (fixed since 2011) | +2 |
| **Russia (Smolensk, Moscow)** | **MSK, UTC+4 until 26 Oct 2014** | +3 |

So Belarus to Russia is **+1 hour**: noon at Liozna is 1 PM in Rudnya. Girbin is two hours behind Belarus. In the air, use the departure zone (N31 is Lisbon time).

### Scotland
- **Girbin:** a fictional village in the Carrick hills of South Ayrshire, about **an hour** south of Ayr by road. It has a square with a bakery, a kirk (the choir practises early on Sundays) and a Catholic chapel for Isabel. It is policed from **Girvan** (Strathclyde Police before April 2013, then Police Scotland). Sanchez's bedroom window looks down the street to the square and the bakery.
- **Ayr:** a town, not a city. TransPacific head office is four storeys of glass on the Prestwick road. The **bonded warehouse** is in the freight area at **Prestwick Airport**, where cargo flights from Asia land. The Low Green is the seafront. John lives off George Street in Wallacetown. Ayr College (Ayrshire College from Aug 2013); the University of the West of Scotland's Ayr campus. Evan lives in **Troon**.
- **Agencies:** SOCA to 6 Oct 2013; **NCA** from 7 Oct 2013. Police Scotland from 1 Apr 2013. HMRC handles customs. Evidence gathered by a private citizen is generally admissible in Scotland, subject to fairness; a bug planted by an officer without authority is not.

### Denmark
- **Helsingør:** a small town at the narrowest point of the Øresund; Kronborg; a 20-minute ferry to Helsingborg (Sweden). The **Øresund Palace Hotel** is fictional: an 18th-century summer palace with a modern seven-storey wing and a casino; it is about ten minutes by taxi from the station. Danish and "Hej"; **kroner**. Cold spring nights (about 3–8°C).
- **Trains:** Kystbanen to Copenhagen H, about 45 minutes; first trains before 5 AM. Copenhagen → Hamburg about 4½ hours (ICE, with the Rødby–Puttgarden ferry). Then the night train south via Freiburg and Basel (Badischer Bahnhof, then SBB) to Zurich HB (arrival **04:20**: the book's fixed time; never print a train name or number). Counter tickets carry no name; card payments do. **No border checks on the way in 2014** (Schengen; Denmark had not yet reintroduced controls).
- **Copenhagen Airport (Kastrup):** about 45–50 minutes from Helsingør; Benedikt's jet flies from here. Passengers from the UK (non-Schengen) have their passports scanned on arrival, which is how the passport contact finds her. Danish citizens are not asked for passports at hotels, so "Rita" claimed to be Danish.

### Switzerland
- The **Walenstadt** flat is up a *Sackgasse* (blue sign), about 90 km from Zurich HB by the A3. Swiss motorway limit 120 km/h. Zurich is empty at 4 AM; Sechseläuten was Mon 28 Apr 2014 (one Böögg). There is an all-night petrol-station café.
- **Zurich Airport:** the early TAP flight to Lisbon (06:50, about 2 h 45); the business-aviation apron; the Hertz return. **Nothing flies to Lisbon from St Gallen–Altenrhein.**

### Portugal
- **Lisbon Airport (Humberto Delgado):** Schengen arrivals have no passport control; restrooms by the baggage reclaim. The metro red line from Aeroporto; City Hall at Praça do Município. **Sete Rios** coach station (Rede Expressos to Nazaré, about 1 h 50); private vans touting nearby.
- **Cascais–Tires Aerodrome:** business aviation, about 25 km west of Lisbon on the A5, with an FBO reception and apron access for vehicles by arrangement. Tires to Vale da Amoreira: A5, the 25 de Abril bridge, A2, A33, about 50 minutes (over an hour in Easter traffic). Tires to Seixal about 40 minutes.
- **South bank:** Vale da Amoreira (the safe house); **Seixal** (the warehouse and bunker); **Almada** (the private clinic; Hospital Garcia de Orta).
- **Nazaré:** the beach town and **O Sítio** on the cliff (funicular); the promenade benches; seven skirts; Holy Week crowds. **Valado dos Frades** (Jules, about 5 km inland); **Fanhais** (Adriana, about 6 km). Nazaré to Seixal is about 140 km, 1 h 35. Policing by the **GNR**; homicide by the **PJ**; Luís is **PSP** (Lisbon). **Good Friday (18 Apr):** church bells silent from Maundy Thursday to the Easter Vigil; the evening procession.

### Estonia and the ship
- **Tallinn:** Lennart Meri Airport; the **Hotel Palace** on Freedom Square (Vabaduse väljak) with the glass-cross War of Independence column. The Old Town is up Harju Street. **Paljassaare** harbour is about fifteen minutes from the centre. Kali (kvass). Easter Monday is not a public holiday in Estonia.
- **DAZZLE:** a converted ro-pax ferry (built 1987), about 150 m, **black hull with white capitals**, owned via TP Baltic OÜ (Tallinn) under a flag of convenience. The former passenger decks hold the owner's quarters: Benedikt's office and bedroom across the stern, his suite beside the captain's, former-suite cabins with balconies, lifts, and a conference room (the old lounge). Vehicle decks and a hold below. About 28 crew plus six armed private guards. **A helicopter winching area aft.** 14 knots. Tallinn (dep 04:00, 23 Apr) → Ventspils (arr about 21:00): at 10:00 she is off Hiiumaa, in Estonian waters, so the **Estonian Border Guard (PPA)** flies the medevac from Tallinn. Ventspils (dep about 19:00, 25 Apr) → Gdynia (arr about 11:00, 26 Apr). DAZZLE's position is public on AIS ship-tracking sites.
- **Ventspils:** Market Square; St Nicholas' Church. **Gdynia:** a busy container and ferry port. Gdynia to Warsaw about 4 hours by road; Warsaw's Old Town was rebuilt after 1944.

### Belarus and Russia
- **Visas (2014):** British and Danish citizens need a Belarusian visa (there was no visa-free entry until 2017), issued at the Belarusian consulate in Warsaw on a business invitation within a day or two (Sanchez's through Benedikt's private Vitebsk fixer, never through TransPacific). Russia needs a visa too: Benedikt holds a multiple-entry business visa; **Sanchez has none** and is in Russia illegally, which nobody checks. Daniel has no passport and gets an **Emergency Travel Document** from the British Embassy in Minsk.
- **Getting in:** Benedikt's chartered jet, Warsaw → **Vitebsk Vostochny** (an international airport with customs, used by charters). Maxwell and Stephen fly LOT Warsaw → Minsk and drive about 3 hours to Vitebsk.
- **Vitebsk:** the hotel is fictional ("the Hotel Dvina"); the route warehouse is an hour's drive outside the city down a dirt road; the ring-road café where the lorries stop; **Novka** is a village just west of the city (about 30 minutes by cab from the centre). Belarusian roubles, though euros talk. The police are the *militsiya* (МІЛІЦЫЯ); Makarov pistols; Zhuk works for the Vitebsk Oblast directorate.
- **The road:** Vitebsk → **Liozna** (about 40 km) → the border (about 15 km) → **Rudnya** (the first Russian town) → Smolensk (about 70 km from the border). **No barrier, no guard, no post in 2014** (the Union State): only a blue sign and the same birches on either side. By law third-country nationals may not cross here; in practice nobody stops a car. The compound is in forest about 30 km north-west of Smolensk, off the Rudnya road, about 45 minutes from the border, 1 h 45 from Vitebsk plus the clock change. Smolensk region plates: **67**.
- **Russian May holidays** (1–4 May 2014): skeleton staff at the compound; Kozlov "not here until late".

### Writers' non-negotiables
1. **One viewpoint per scene**, named in the first sentence; change viewpoint only at a scene break.
2. **No "little did they know"**, no narrator warnings, no sequel tease, no epilogue.
3. **Only Daniel says "San."**
4. **Benedikt never learns Daniel's surname, or knows that Sanchez is Daniel's daughter, before the photograph (N36).** From N33 (the bakery) he may *suspect*, which is why he asks for a picture. Oskar's brief was TransPacific links, never family.
5. **Evan never hears the name "Sanchez Roberti"** from Benedikt. He learns of "a girl" only from Kozlov on 3 May.
6. **Maxwell's evidence is never used in court**; the case rests on Sanchez's material, Daniel's tin and Daniel's testimony.
7. **Keep the author's voice:** formal, courteous, descriptive. One good travel aside per place (the Nazaré bench, Warsaw's resurrection, Dona Graça's soup). Read new scenes aloud beside an edited chapter.
