# Notes and Assumptions

You were away and there was no one to ask, so I made every decision myself. This file records each judgement call, so you can see what I assumed and undo anything you disagree with. Every change to the text is a tracked change, so nothing here is permanent.

---

## 1. How the edit was done

1. **Study.** The manuscript was read in full by nine readers, each taking one section, who made a chapter-by-chapter analysis of plot, characters, continuity, facts, pacing and prose. Seven more readers each studied one aspect of your style across the whole book: vocabulary, sentence rhythm and punctuation, dialogue, imagery, narrative voice, recurring grammar patterns, and how AI-style editing would differ from your own writing.
2. **Decisions.** From those reports I drew up a binding style sheet, a corrected timeline, a character-name review and a list of fact fixes.
3. **Line edit.** The book was divided into 24 sections of about 3,500 words. Each was line-edited against the style sheet by an editor who first read all the style reports.
4. **Second pass.** A different editor reviewed each section with fresh eyes. They reversed changes that drifted from your voice, restored your own words where the first editor had swapped them unnecessarily, caught missed errors and tightened the comments.
5. **Sweep, blind test and proofread.** A book-wide pass caught remaining patterns. In a blind test, a separate reader who had seen only samples of your untouched writing tried to spot edited sentences (details in section 5). Six proofreaders then cold-read the whole edited text.
6. **Assembly.** Every change was written into your original Word file as a genuine tracked change, keeping your formatting, fonts and layout. The result was checked two ways: "Reject All" reproduces your original text exactly, and "Accept All" reproduces the edited text exactly.

## 2. Decisions I made on your behalf

### Setting and timeline
- **"Stockholm, Denmark" is now Helsingør, Denmark.** Stockholm is in Sweden, but your text describes a small Danish town 40 minutes by train from Copenhagen, with Danish coffee, "Hej" and a grand old hotel with a casino. Helsingør fits all of that. The Swedish slips (kronor, "the poker game in Sweden") became Danish. If you prefer Sweden, change the date lines back to "Stockholm, Sweden" and "Danish" to "Swedish", and keep in mind that the 40-minute train to Copenhagen then won't work.
- **The Denmark chapters move two days later (12–13 April → 14–16 April).** Your later chapters put the Lisbon flight on 17 April and say it left the morning after she reached Zurich. Keeping 12 April would leave three days unaccounted for.
- **Chapters 78–85 move to 15–17 May.** Chapter 78 says it took Maxwell "a good two weeks" to convince his superiors, but its date line said the next morning. I kept your "two weeks" and moved the dates. The lighter alternative is to change the phrase to "a whole day" and date those chapters 2–4 May.
- **Chapter order is unchanged.** Where a chapter's events happen earlier than the one before it (Chapter 22 before 21, for example), I kept your order and made the times consistent. The structure plan recommends reordering.
- **Chapter 1 moves from 6:00 AM to 8:00 AM.** Your text has bright sun and churchgoers, and in February the Scottish sun isn't up at six.

### The heroine's age
- **Sixteen in 2010, eighteen in 2012, twenty in April 2014.** "Early teens" became "sixteen", "late teens" became "about twenty", "naïve teenager" became "naïve girl", and "school" became "college" in the 2012 chapters. This was the one change I considered non-negotiable for a modern publisher. As written, a reader could take her to be seventeen or younger in the seduction scene.

### Description of Sanchez
- In her first scene (Chapter 2) and in the love scene (Chapter 3), I cut the most body-focused phrases, including the line about her breasts, "ogled" and "exploring her body inch by inch". I cut by deletion only and added nothing. The scene and its events are unchanged. Agents and editors now flag heroines described through a man's appraising eye, particularly in the opening pages.

### Poker (Chapter 2)
- **Hand 1:** Alberto's straight flush needed the eight and nine of spades, which Benedikt was holding, and Benedikt had only four cards. Benedikt now holds a pair of aces with three unmatched cards, and Alberto the six to ten of spades.
- **Hand 2:** "one more king and a pair of queens" is a full house, which beats a straight. Alberto now has three kings, which lose to Benedikt's straight. This also matches Sanchez's read: "He has the cards, but not high in the order."
- **The pot** is now "over twenty million", because Benedikt could not win thirty-five million after losing the first hand. Alberto, who still has more chips, is "badly weakened" rather than "out of the game".

### Spelling
- **British spelling throughout,** kept as a separate set of changes (reviewer "Editor (Spelling)"). Your book is set largely in Britain and Europe, your heroine is Scottish, and a UK publisher would expect British spelling. The draft was mostly American (color, realize, center) with some British habits (towards, enquire, grey). If you query American agents, reject the whole Spelling set in one click.
- **"Mom" becomes "Mum"** for the Scottish family, in the same set.

### Names
- Every change is explained in `03_Character_Names.md`, and they are all under the reviewer "Editor (Names)". The leads are unchanged.
- **Inserted text.** Where the line edit inserted new words that include a name, the new name appears there directly. If you reject the Names set, search the document for the new names (Roberti, Francisco, Tiago, Nuno, Yuri, Kozlov, Ingrid and the others) to catch any in inserted text.

### Dialogue
- I kept your formal, uncontracted speech: "I am", "I will", "Let us". It is part of the book's texture. The dialogue report suggested giving Stephen and Sanchez more contractions in private, to help tell voices apart. That is a revision choice for you, not a correction, so I didn't impose it.
- Speech verbs used in the wrong sense (*hushed*, *blazoned*, *spoke "…"*, *stated* for a question) were fixed. About two-thirds of the *blurted*s were changed or cut. Your *continued*, *enquired* and *came the reply* stay.
- **"Leave me alone. Will you?"** Your tag-question style is kept in most places.

### Things I deliberately did not change
- **Structure.** No chapters were moved, merged or cut, apart from one deleted paragraph that was a garbled duplicate. The structure is for you to decide. See `02_Structure_and_Flow_Plan.md`.
- **Plot holes needing new material.** The sedative, the guns, the officer's body, the Schengen flight, Montijo air base and Maxwell's legal authority are all flagged in comments, not rewritten. A line editor shouldn't write new scenes in your name.
- **Scotland Yard.** Maxwell's employer should probably be Police Scotland or the National Crime Agency. I flagged this rather than changed it, because it affects his whole backstory.
- **The ending.** Only line fixes. The one exception: the last sentence, "and soon she would accept his love", contradicted her "Yes, us" three lines earlier, so it was trimmed. The comment offers an alternative.
- **Quote marks.** Your file mixed straight ("like this") and curly (“like this”) quotation marks and apostrophes. All of them are now curly, under a fourth reviewer name, **"Editor (Typography)"**, so you can accept them in one click. They are kept separate so they don't bury the real edits.
- **Spacing.** Double spaces and non-breaking spaces from your word processor are left alone. They are invisible, and a typesetter removes them automatically.
- **Formatting.** Chapter headings, fonts and page layout are exactly as you had them. Chapters 1–18 have bold italic date lines with superscript "th", and chapters 19–85 have plain bold date lines. I left that inconsistency alone. Where a date line's number changed, the new "th" is not superscript.

## 3. Limits of this edit, honestly stated

- **This is a line and copy edit plus an editorial assessment, not a rewrite.** The structural problems described in the letter remain until you revise.
- **The editors were careful, but they may still be wrong.** In a small number of places we couldn't tell what you meant. Those are marked "Query", and where we guessed, the comment says so. Always prefer your own knowledge of your story to our guess.
- **Facts were checked against public information** (maps, timetables, historical dates). A few items, such as the 2014 train services and airport routes, are worth checking again yourself before you finalise.
- **No visual render.** The container used for this work could not run a word processor to produce page images. The Word files were therefore checked structurally instead: schema validation, a simulated Accept All and Reject All, and a comparison with your original. If anything looks odd when you open the file, "Reject All" returns your original text.
