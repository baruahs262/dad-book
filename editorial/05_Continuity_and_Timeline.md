# Continuity, Timeline and Fact Check

This file has two parts:
1. **The corrected timeline.** Every chapter heading (the time/date/place line under each chapter number), what it said, and what the edit changes it to. These changes are in the tracked manuscript. Accept or reject them **as a group**, because they only hold together as a set.
2. **Continuity and fact problems that need your own rewriting.** These could not be fixed by changing a word or two. Each has a recommended fix. They are also flagged in the margin comments.

A further 170-odd small fact and continuity fixes (place spellings, currencies, foreign phrases, drive times, car makes, name typos) were made directly in the tracked manuscript, each with an explanatory comment where it mattered.

Chapter references ("ch 12") point to the chapter in the original manuscript.

## Part 2. Problems that need rewriting

These cannot be fixed by changing a word or two. Each has a recommended fix.


1. **Chapter order and merges.** The line edit keeps every chapter in place and marks the overlaps as "parallel". The following would read better restructured:
   - Ch4 gives away the pursuer before ch5's "we are being followed". Cut ch4 to one atmospheric paragraph, or fold it into ch5.
   - Merge ch6 and ch7, which cover the same morning, logistics and orders.
   - Ch10's airport and flight paragraphs (ch 10) tell ch22's scene again with different details. Cut them. End Part I on ch9's secret TransPacific call (ch 9), which is the strongest curtain in Part I.
   - Fold ch11 (about 450 words of summary) into ch12.
   - Consider interleaving the 2012 chapters with the 2014 ones instead of a nine-chapter block.
   - Move ch22 ahead of ch21 (Maxwell's 5 AM, then Benedikt's 7 AM). The reader then watches Benedikt chase a flight they know has gone.
   - Merge ch27 and ch28 at Montijo. Fold ch32 into the end of ch30. Merge ch26 with ch29 and place the result after ch30, so that 17 April ends with the siblings asleep in Nazaré.
   - Merge ch34 and ch35 as a cross-cut. Fold ch38 into ch37 as a cutaway to Benedikt's end of the phone.
   - Merge ch41 into ch43. Merge ch44 with ch46, and ch45 with ch48, so that Benedikt departs and Maxwell arrives in alternation.
   - Merge ch52 into ch53, and ch54 into ch55.
   - Fold ch57 into ch58, and ch65 into the end of ch63. Merge ch64 and ch70 into one Maxwell chapter with new information, or cut them.
   - Merge ch73 and ch74, and ch78 and ch79 (show the plan, which ch 79 only summarises). Put ch82 before ch81.
   - The **PART IV** divider (ch 36) falls between ch36 and ch37, in the middle of a gunpoint scene. Move it to ch42 (the capture) or ch49 (Tallinn).
2. **Rail geography (Helsingør → Zurich).** Trains from Copenhagen to Basel end at Basel, and Zurich lies beyond it, so there is no "detour she took at Zurich" (ch 7). In ch9 Benedikt counts "ten stations between Copenhagen and Zurich" (ch 9) and then "between Copenhagen and Basel" (ch 9). He crosses out Basel after deciding she stayed on past it. Hamburg was not a stop on the 2014 CityNightLine *Aurora* (Copenhagen–Basel). **Fix:** she buys a ticket to Basel and stays on a connecting service to Zurich. Benedikt reasons from the Basel CCTV that she never came through the concourse, and sends teams to Freiburg, Basel Badischer Bahnhof and Zurich.
3. **Journey time and the identity trail.** After the two-day shift (see Part 1) she leaves Copenhagen early on the 15th and reaches Zurich at 4:20 AM on the 16th. That is about 23 hours on the rails, which a reader can accept only with one sentence ("a day of changes and a night train south"). The cleaner option is the 18:10 CNL sleeper from Copenhagen, which would mean waiting a day in Copenhagen; that needs one line in ch3. Separately, Danish domestic trains record no names, so Damian cannot get a "photo ID" showing *Sanchez* (ch 7), nor the name "Trevor" for a fellow passenger. She also registered at the hotel as "Rita" (ch 7). **Fix:** move the Sanchez reveal to Benedikt's passport contact, which is already set up in ch 9. Take "Trevor" from an international sleeper or seat reservation, and have the station CCTV show only "a man in a dark windcheater".
4. **Where is the Swiss apartment?** Ch19 has "about two hours" on the A3 from Zurich (ch 19), and ch8 says "a good couple of hours" (ch 8). Then it is "about an hour" to Altenrhein (ch 22). Ch10 and ch22 are datelined "Zurich" and speak of "bail[ing] out of Zurich". **Fix:** name a town near Sargans or Walenstadt, trim the drive to "the better part of an hour and a half", and use that town in the ch10 and ch22 datelines. (ch 10 "south" → "north" is already in the tracked manuscript.)
5. **Flights and Schengen.** St. Gallen–Altenrhein had no TAP or Lisbon service in 2014; its only scheduled route went to Vienna. TAP flight numbers begin "TP", not "TAP020" (ch 22). Switzerland–Portugal is an intra-Schengen flight, so there is no passport queue, no diplomats' lane, no immigration chief and no "cleared immigration" (ch 24, ch 25, ch 28). **Fix:** fly from Zurich or Friedrichshafen, or route via Vienna. Turn the queue into the baggage hall. Replace the chief-officer scene with airport police checking CCTV, which gives Maxwell and the reader the payoff of the disguise ("a woman in an abaya and a young soldier came out of the restroom").
6. **Montijo and the south bank.** Montijo is Portuguese Air Force Base No. 6. It had no civil terminal or receptionist, it has two runways, and a civilian van cannot park "at the edge of the runway" (ch 27–28, ch 45). **Fix:** use Cascais–Tires, Lisbon's business-aviation field, or keep Montijo as a sinister favour ("a duty sergeant who had been paid"). Also:
   - Vale da Amoreira to Montijo is about 15–20 minutes, but ch 45's "over an hour" is kept because the clock needs it. Blame traffic or roadworks, or move the house.
   - The "warehouse a few miles south of the Lisbon dockyard" (ch 42) is across the river, in Seixal or Almada. Say so.
   - As noted at the top, the A12 references are correct.
7. **Lisbon and Nazaré geography and culture.**
   - There is no "City Hall" metro stop. Santa Apolónia and Oriente are about 7 km apart, and Oriente comes *before* the city (ch 24).
   - Buses to Nazaré leave from Sete Rios or Oriente (ch 24).
   - Alcobaça is a separate inland town. For a clifftop view of the Atlantic, use O Sítio (ch 26).
   - Fado is from the 19th century, not the "15th century", and is played on the *guitarra portuguesa* (ch 26).
   - The seven skirts belong to Nazaré, and there is nothing "biblical" about them (ch 24).
   - The City Hall is 19th-century neoclassical, not "gothic" (ch 24).
   - Lisbon is the westernmost *capital* (ch 27).
   - Nazaré is policed by the GNR, not the "Lisbon police" (ch 43, ch 45).
   - Stephen's Portuguese army cadet uniform: where did he get it? A Fátima pilgrim in Holy Week would work better (ch 24).
8. **The heroine's age, beyond the line fixes.** Age fix makes her 20, but the sexualised catalogue in her own POV reads as the male gaze: ch 2 ("full breasts were tantalizing"), ch 3 ("hunger, filthiness"), ch 3 ("He ogled her"), ch 49, ch 53.    - Maxwell suspected an 18-year-old intern of being a "government secret service agency" operative (ch 53). Soften to "I wondered who had sent you".
   - Bradley blackmails an 18-year-old intern into a year of combat training (ch 18). Maxwell's confession in ch53 should acknowledge this.
